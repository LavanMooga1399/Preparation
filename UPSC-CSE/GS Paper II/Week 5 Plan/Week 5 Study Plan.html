◆ The one number that should decide how you spend this weekend

I measured the four remaining notes: 29,688 words of teaching text, 68 questions and 100 trap statements. At honest revision pace that is 5.4 hours to read every word once and attempt every question once. You have roughly 19.

So the question is not "can I finish the syllabus". You can finish it by Saturday lunchtime. The question is what you do with the other fourteen hours — and the answer is not reading it a second time.

Week 4 is the evidence. You lost 20 questions. Your mean time on a wrong answer was 71.8 seconds and on a right answer 70.5 seconds — statistically identical. You were not rushing and you were not careless. You had read the material and could not retrieve it. Re-reading fixes the first feeling and none of the second.

🌱 What "retrieval" actually means here

Close the note. Say the answer out loud or write it, then open the note and check. That failed attempt — the two seconds where you can't quite recall whether the State PSC appears in Article 233 or 234 — is what builds the memory. Reading the same line again does not, however carefully you read it.

Concretely, in this plan: every reading session is immediately followed by its own question set with the note closed. Every wrong answer goes on a single sheet of paper. That sheet, not the notes, is what you revise on Sunday morning.

◆ What a good result looks like

Not 60. The Week 4 paper dragged the class average down to 42.16 and the topper to 63.96; if Week 5 is set at the same difficulty, a raw 50 would be a strong paper.

The number I will actually judge is your score divided by the class average. It has been 0.875 and 0.868 for two weeks running — remarkably stable, and the honest measure of where you stand. If that ratio crosses 1.0 this week, the coverage fix worked and there is no argument for coaching. That is the target: beat the class average, whatever the absolute number turns out to be.

The three rules for Sunday

  • 1 · Every "how many" gets margin marks. Twenty-one of the 50 questions in the Week 4 paper were this format — 42% — and you scored 52% on them. Write T or F beside each statement before looking at the options. This format cannot be solved by elimination, so guessing at the option level is throwing marks away.
  • 2 · Hunt the absolute word. any, all, every, only, never, must, shall. In Week 4 at least four questions turned on one qualifier. When you see one, test that word first and the rest of the sentence second.
  • 3 · Accuracy, not pace. You finished Week 4 in 57 of 60 minutes with identical time on right and wrong answers — you were never rushing. Holding Week 3's accuracy would have been worth +21 marks; perfect skip discipline only +7. Slow down on the ones you can actually get right rather than reaching for more attempts.
  • On a repeated question — this paper has a deliberate duplicate pair, and the real Week 2 paper had one — answer it identically both times and move on. You lost both copies in Week 2 by second-guessing the repeat.
  • Skip rule: only if you cannot narrow to two options. At +2 / −0.67, break-even is about 25% accuracy, so a two-way guess is clearly worth taking.
