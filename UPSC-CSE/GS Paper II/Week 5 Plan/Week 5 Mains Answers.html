Week 5 Mains — the five questions

The actual paper: five compulsory questions, 60 marks, one hour. Three at 150 words and two at 250. Every one sits inside the Week 5 notes — which is itself the finding worth noticing.

5 questions · 60 marks 3 × 10 marks · 2 × 15 One hour All budget-checked
12minutes per question — the real constraint
◆ Read the paper before you read the answers

Five questions, sixty marks, sixty minutes. That is twelve minutes each, including thinking. A 250-word answer at ordinary handwriting speed takes eight to nine minutes to write, which leaves three to four minutes to plan. If you have not decided your structure by minute four, the answer will sprawl and the last question will be rushed.

The examiner's remark on your script was "Keep it up, you have great potential. Keep practicing." That is a generous but non-specific comment, so it tells you nothing about where marks were lost. The model answers below are therefore written against MAINS_ANSWER_CRAFT.md rather than against the script: each carries a directive box, a skeleton, a word budget with what was cut and what it bought, and a cost warning.

🌱 The one habit that separates a 5 from an 8 on a 10-mark question

Answer the directive word, not the topic. "Examine" is not "describe". "Distinguish" is not "explain both". "Comment" is not "list".

Q1 asks whether the 2025 judgment effectively addresses the friction — so an answer that narrates the judgment scores badly however accurate it is. The examiner is looking for a verdict on effectiveness, supported by what the judgment does and does not resolve.

Every model answer below states its directive first, in the pink box, before anything else. Copy that habit: write the directive at the top of your rough space before you start.

Q1

The 2025 Article 200 judgment and Governor–State friction

M42UGSSP Week 510 marks · 150 words

Can the Supreme Court's 2025 judgment concerning the Governor's powers under Article 200 effectively address the recurring friction between State Governments and Governors over granting assent to Bills? Examine.

Directive: "Examine." The question asks about effectiveness, not content. A narration of the judgment, however accurate, does not answer it. You must reach a verdict: what the ruling settles, what it leaves open, and therefore whether the friction is resolved.
Skeleton
  1. What the friction is
  2. What 2025 settled
  3. What it left open
  4. Verdict on effectiveness

Model answer

The friction is structural: a Governor is a Union appointee holding office during the President's pleasure under Article 156, exercising a power under Article 200 that the Constitution left without any time limit.

What 2025 settled

Two judgments, pulling in opposite directions. In State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor of Tamil Nadu (April 2025) the Court held indefinite inaction illegal, confined the Governor to three options, imposed timelines and used Article 142 to deem ten Bills assented. On the 16th Presidential Reference, decided 20 November 2025, a Constitution Bench retained the three options and the rule that a Bill cannot be withheld without being returned — but held that no timelines can be read into Articles 200 and 201, that there can be no deemed assent, and that these actions are not justiciable.

What it left open

The remedy is now narrow: only prolonged, unexplained and indefinite inaction invites limited scrutiny, and a court may direct the Governor to act without touching the merits. Delay short of that is beyond reach, and the Governor may still reserve a reconsidered Bill for the President — reopening the very channel through which Bills were stalled.

The judgment therefore settles the law and not the friction. Its cause is the Governor's appointment and tenure, which Sarkaria and Punchhi addressed and no court can. Effective adjudication; incomplete remedy.

Value additions used: Arts 156, 200, 201, 142 · TN Governor (April 2025) contrasted with Special Reference No. 1 of 2025 (20 Nov 2025) · the "prolonged, unexplained, indefinite" test · the reserve-on-second-round survival · Sarkaria and Punchhi.
Budget check: 166 / 150 words. Cut: the count of fourteen referred questions and the Vice-President's "nuclear missile" remark — colour that does not bear on effectiveness. Bought: the closing distinction between settling the law and settling the friction, which is the sentence that actually answers "examine".
What would cost you marks: describing only the April 2025 judgment — it is no longer the governing statement of the law, and an answer that stops there is eight months out of date; and omitting Article 156, which is the reason the friction is structural rather than legal.
Q2

Review petition versus judicial review

M43UGSSP Week 510 marks · 150 words

Distinguish between a review petition and judicial review, while enumerating the Constitutional provisions relating to them.

Directive: "Distinguish" + "enumerate." Two demands. The distinction must be drawn on named axes — who, against what, on what grounds — not by describing each in turn. And the provisions must actually be listed, because "enumerate" is a scoring instruction.
Skeleton
  1. One line defining each
  2. Distinction on four axes
  3. Provisions, listed
  4. The link between them

Model answer

Judicial review is the power of a court to test the constitutionality of legislative and executive action and declare it void. A review petition is a request to the same court to reconsider its own judgment.

The distinction

Object: judicial review examines a law or an executive act; a review petition examines a judgment of the court itself. Forum: review lies to the very court that decided; judicial review is exercised by the Supreme Court and the High Courts over other organs. Ground: review is confined to an error apparent on the face of the record or newly discovered evidence, not a fresh argument; judicial review turns on unconstitutionality. Nature: review is a corrective of the judicial process; judicial review is a check on the other two branches.

Provisions

Review — Article 137, subject to laws made by Parliament and rules under Article 145; normally within thirty days. Judicial review — Article 13 (laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights are void), Articles 32 and 226 (the remedies), Articles 131–136, Articles 245–246 with the Seventh Schedule, and Article 372.

Both are limited: review does not permit rehearing, and judicial review does not permit examining a law's wisdom. Where review fails, a curative petition lies — a purely judge-made remedy from Rupa Ashok Hurra (2002).

Value additions used: Arts 137, 145, 13, 32, 226, 131–136, 245–246, 372 · the "error apparent on the face of the record" standard · the thirty-day limit · the curative petition and its 2002 origin · the wisdom limitation.
Budget check: 176 / 150 words. Cut: examples of famous review petitions and a sentence on open-court hearing of reviews — neither advances a distinction. Bought: the four named axes, which is what converts this from two descriptions into a genuine "distinguish", and the curative petition, which shows the sequence review → curative.
What would cost you marks: writing a paragraph on each concept without ever placing them side by side — the directive is distinguish; and forgetting that judicial review has no single article, which is the enumeration the examiner is testing.
Q3

Judicial independence and its constitutional safeguards

M44UGSSP Week 510 marks · 150 words

What do you understand by the concept of Judicial Independence? Enumerate the constitutional provisions to safeguard the independence and impartial functioning of the Supreme Court of India.

Directive: define + "enumerate." A short conceptual opening, then a list. This is one of the few questions where a dense itemised body scores better than flowing prose, because the examiner is counting provisions.
Skeleton
  1. What independence means — and from whom
  2. Appointment and tenure safeguards
  3. Financial safeguards
  4. Functional safeguards
  5. One line on what is missing

Model answer

Judicial independence means the ability to decide without fear or favour — insulated from the executive and legislature in appointment, tenure, salary and conduct, so that a judge may rule against the government that appointed him. It is part of the basic structure, which is why the 99th Amendment and the NJAC fell in 2015.

Appointment and tenure

Appointment by the President on the collegium's recommendation (Art 124(2), as read in the Second and Third Judges Cases); security of tenure, removal only for proved misbehaviour or incapacity by the demanding process of Art 124(4); a fixed retirement age of 65.

Financial

Salaries and allowances charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (Art 125) and therefore non-votable; emoluments cannot be varied to a judge's disadvantage after appointment, save during a Financial Emergency under Art 360.

Functional

Conduct not discussable in the legislature except on a removal motion (Arts 121, 211); Art 129 contempt power as a court of record; Art 146 — the CJI appoints the Court's staff and its expenses are charged; jurisdiction may be extended but not curtailed by Parliament; and a bar on practice after retirement (Art 124(7)).

The gap is accountability: no judge has ever been removed, and the Memorandum of Procedure remains unfinalised.

Value additions used: Arts 124(2), 124(4), 124(7), 125, 129, 146, 121, 211, 360 · basic structure and the 2015 NJAC judgment · the extend-not-curtail rule · the unfinalised Memorandum of Procedure.
Budget check: 181 / 150 words. Cut: the ad hoc and retired-judge provisions (Arts 127, 128) and the seat of the Court — real provisions, but not independence safeguards. Bought: the three-way grouping into appointment, financial and functional, which lets the examiner count the provisions at a glance, and the closing accountability line.
What would cost you marks: an undifferentiated list of articles with no grouping — the same content scores lower when the examiner has to sort it; and omitting the Art 360 exception to the salary protection, which is the qualifier that shows precision.
Q4

Removal of judges — provisions, ineffectiveness, reform

M45UGSSP Week 515 marks · 250 words

What are the constitutional provisions and procedures governing the removal of High Court and Supreme Court judges? Why have these remained largely ineffective in enforcing judicial accountability? Suggest pragmatic reforms.

Three explicit parts, so three explicit sections. Marks are allocated across all three — an answer that spends 200 words on the procedure and 50 on the rest cannot score well however accurate the procedure is. Aim roughly 80 / 90 / 80.
Skeleton
  1. The provisions and the process
  2. Why it fails — four reasons, evidenced
  3. Reforms, pragmatic and specific
  4. Verdict

Model answer

Article 124(4) governs the Supreme Court and Article 218 applies it to the High Courts. A judge may be removed only for proved misbehaviour or incapacity, by an order of the President following an address by both Houses in the same session, each supported by a majority of its total membership and by two-thirds of those present and voting. The Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968 supplies the machinery: a motion signed by 100 members of the Lok Sabha or 50 of the Rajya Sabha, which the Speaker or Chairman may admit or refuse; if admitted, a three-member committee — a Supreme Court judge, a High Court Chief Justice and a distinguished jurist — investigates.

Why it fails

Four reasons. The threshold is political: a double majority in both Houses makes removal impossible without cross-party consensus. The grounds are undefined — the Constitution nowhere defines "misbehaviour" or "incapacity". Admission is discretionary, so a motion can die before any inquiry. And resignation abates everything: an inquiry runs only against a sitting judge. The record proves it — no judge has ever been removed. In V. Ramaswami (1993) the committee found misbehaviour and the motion still failed on abstentions. In 2025–26, after cash was found at Justice Yashwant Varma's residence and a motion signed by 146 members was admitted, he resigned in April 2026 and the inquiry lapsed.

Reforms

Define misbehaviour statutorily; create a standing judicial accountability commission with judicial majority to receive and screen complaints, replacing the informal in-house procedure that has no statutory basis; provide graded sanctions — censure, withdrawal of work, transfer — so that removal is not the only remedy; and allow an inquiry to conclude and publish despite resignation.

The provision is not weak; it is binary. Accountability fails for want of a middle rung.

Value additions used: Arts 124(4), 218 · Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968 with the 100/50 thresholds and the three-member committee · V. Ramaswami (1993) · the Justice Varma sequence with 146 signatures and the April 2026 resignation · the in-house procedure's lack of statutory basis · graded sanctions.
Budget check: 297 / 250 words. Cut: the National Judicial Accountability Bill's history and a sentence on comparative practice in the UK — both interesting, neither load-bearing. Bought: the two named case histories, because "largely ineffective" is a claim that needs evidence and not assertion, and the closing "binary, not weak" formulation which converts a list of criticisms into a diagnosis.
What would cost you marks: using the word impeachment throughout — the Constitution says removal, and reserves impeachment for the President under Art 61; and offering reforms that are not pragmatic, such as lowering the parliamentary majority, which would trade accountability against independence and the examiner will read as unconsidered.
Q5

Courts of record, contempt, and whether the law needs a fresh look

M46UGSSP Week 515 marks · 250 words

What is meant by the statement that the Supreme Court of India and the High Courts are courts of record, and can punish for contempt of themselves? Also comment on whether the law of contempt as it stands today needs a fresh look.

Explain, then comment. The first half is definitional and must be precise; the second half demands a position. "Both sides have merit" is not a comment. Roughly 110 words explaining, 140 commenting.
Skeleton
  1. Court of record — the two limbs
  2. Contempt: civil and criminal
  3. The case for a fresh look
  4. The case against
  5. Verdict, with a specific reform

Model answer

A court of record has two attributes. Its judgments and proceedings are preserved as perpetual evidence, admitted without further proof and not open to question by any subordinate court. And it has an inherent power to punish for contempt of itself. Article 129 makes the Supreme Court a court of record and Article 215 the High Courts; a High Court may also punish contempt of subordinate courts under Section 10 of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971.

Contempt is of two kinds. Civil contempt is wilful disobedience of an order or breach of an undertaking. Criminal contempt is publication or an act that scandalises the court, prejudices a judicial proceeding, or obstructs the administration of justice. Notably the Constitution defines neither — the 1971 Act does, following the H.N. Sanyal Committee.

The case for a fresh look

"Scandalising the court" is a colonial offence abolished in England in 2013, and it is vague enough to chill legitimate criticism. The court is accuser, prosecutor and judge in its own cause. And the power sits uneasily with Article 19(1)(a), since truth became a defence only by the 2006 amendment, and then conditionally.

Against

Judges cannot answer criticism publicly; the power protects public confidence, which is the judiciary's only enforcement currency. Civil contempt is indispensable, since an unenforceable order is no order.

The distinction is the answer. Civil contempt should stay untouched; criminal contempt should be narrowed to acts that genuinely obstruct the administration of justice, with "scandalising" dropped — as the Law Commission's 274th Report (2018) considered. Confidence earned by reasoning survives; confidence compelled by punishment is brittle.

Value additions used: Arts 129, 215, 19(1)(a) · Contempt of Courts Act 1971 with s.10 and the 2006 truth-as-defence amendment · H.N. Sanyal Committee · England's 2013 abolition of scandalising · Law Commission 274th Report · the civil/criminal split as the reform lever.
Budget check: 291 / 250 words. Cut: named contempt proceedings against individual lawyers and a sentence on suo motu contempt procedure — vivid but they invite a digression into facts rather than principle. Bought: the England 2013 comparison and the Law Commission report, which turn "needs a fresh look" from an opinion into a documented debate, and the closing line, which gives the comment a position rather than a balance.
What would cost you marks: treating contempt as one thing — the entire quality of the comment rests on separating civil from criminal; and concluding that "a balance must be struck" without saying which limb you would change and how.

The reusable blocks — carry these into Week 6

  • Every answer opened with a definition or a structural fact, never with history. "The friction is structural: a Governor is a Union appointee…" beats "The office of Governor has been controversial since 1950."
  • Articles are cited inline, not in a bracket at the end. Art 124(4), Art 218, Art 129, Art 215, Art 137, Art 145, Art 125, Art 146, Arts 121/211, Art 360.
  • Two named cases per answer is the working target — enough to evidence, few enough to leave room for argument.
  • Currency wins marks. The 20 November 2025 advisory opinion, the Justice Varma resignation of April 2026, the 2026 strength increase to 38. Most candidates will be writing from a textbook that predates all three.
  • Every answer ends on a verdict, not a summary. "Effective adjudication; incomplete remedy." "The provision is not weak; it is binary." "Confidence earned by reasoning survives; confidence compelled by punishment is brittle."
  • The word budget is a substitution rule, never an accumulation rule. Each answer above shows what was cut to buy what was added. If you cannot say what you cut, you have not edited.
  • Timing: 12 minutes per question. Decide the structure by minute 4 or the last answer will be rushed — and the last answer here is worth 15 marks.
