Week 5 Mock Test — 50 Questions

Exact test format: +2 per correct, −0.66 per wrong, 0 for skipped. 60 minutes. Covers the full Week 5 syllabus — State Legislature (Days 1–2), the Supreme Court, the High Courts, subordinate courts and tribunals, and judicial review, activism and PIL.

Weighted to the format that is still leaking. Statements remains your weakest measured format at 64% from the Week 3 paper, so there are 13 of them here, plus 10 negative-phrased and 8 how-many. The answer key is balanced across (a)–(d) at 12 / 13 / 13 / 12, and there is one deliberate duplicate pair — answer it identically both times.

The five rules: (1) Keep the NOT discipline — mark each statement T/F, then re-read the stem and flip. It took you from 0/6 to 2/2. (2) Speed is not your problem: you finished Week 3 in 31 of 60 minutes and still lost marks on questions answered in under fifteen seconds. Read the whole stem. (3) On a Statements question, evaluate every statement before looking at the options. (4) Hunt absolutes — any, all, only, never, must. Several questions in this paper turn on a single qualifier. (5) On a how-many question, if two statements are genuinely unknown, skip — it has the worst guess-odds of any format.
Two facts in this paper changed in the last twelve months. Both are deliberate. The Supreme Court's sanctioned strength moved from 34 to 38 in 2026, and the rule that a Governor must assent to a re-passed Bill was narrowed by the November 2025 advisory opinion — he cannot withhold, but he may still reserve it for the President. If you answer either from an older textbook you will get it wrong, which is exactly the point of including them.

Q1How many

With reference to the Legislative Council of a State, consider the following statements:

  1. One-sixth of its members are nominated by the Governor.
  2. The fields from which the Governor nominates are identical to those from which the President nominates to the Rajya Sabha.
  3. Its Chairman is elected by the Council from among its own members.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(b) Only two. Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong, and by a single item: Article 171(5) lists five fields — literature, science, art, co-operative movement and social service — whereas Article 80(3) lists four, without the co-operative movement. Statement 3 is the contrast with the Rajya Sabha, whose Chairman is the Vice-President and not a member of the House. Note 04, Section 03.
Q2How many

Consider the following statements about the State Legislature:

  1. The quorum of a House is ten members or one-tenth of the total membership, whichever is greater.
  2. The Speaker of the Legislative Assembly may be removed by a resolution passed after fourteen days' notice.
  3. The Governor must address the Assembly at the commencement of the first session of each year.
  4. Not more than six months shall intervene between two sessions.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(d) All four. Art 189 quorum (note the floor of ten, which the Union Art 100 lacks); Art 179 removal with fourteen days' notice; Art 176 special address, obligatory unlike the discretionary Art 175; Art 174 six-month rule. Note 04, Section 05.
Q3Statements

With reference to disqualification of a member of a State legislature, consider the following statements:

  1. The question is decided by the Governor, who must act according to the opinion of the Election Commission.
  2. A question of disqualification on the ground of defection is also decided by the Governor.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only. Article 192 makes the Governor the deciding authority and binds him to the Election Commission's opinion. But defection is carved out — under the Tenth Schedule it is decided by the Speaker or Chairman, and that decision is subject to judicial review after Kihoto Hollohan (1992). Note 04, Section 05.
Q4Pairs

Consider the following pairs of Article and subject relating to the State Legislature:

  1. Article 174 — sessions, prorogation and dissolution
  2. Article 187 — secretariat of the State legislature
  3. Article 189 — voting, vacancies and quorum
  4. Article 213 — Governor's power to promulgate ordinances

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(d) All four. An 'all correct' pairs set placed deliberately — your recorded instinct is to reject option (d). Verify each pair rather than reasoning about how many ought to be wrong. Note 04, Section 05.
Q5Statements

With reference to Article 200 of the Constitution, consider the following statements:

  1. Where a Bill is returned by the Governor and passed again by the House, the Governor is bound to give his assent.
  2. The Governor may reserve a Bill for the consideration of the President.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(b) 2 only. This is the fact that changed in November 2025. The first proviso says the Governor shall not withhold assent on the second round — but the Constitution Bench in Special Reference No. 1 of 2025 departed from the April 2025 Tamil Nadu judgment and held he may still reserve the Bill for the President. So he is not bound to assent. Note 04, Section 04 and Topic 6 Note 01.
Q6How many

Consider the following statements regarding an ordinance promulgated by the Governor under Article 213:

  1. It can be promulgated only when the State legislature is not in session.
  2. It ceases to operate six weeks from the reassembly of the legislature.
  3. It can be used to amend the Constitution.
  4. In certain cases the Governor requires the prior instructions of the President.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(c) Only three. Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong — an ordinance cannot amend the Constitution, exactly as under Art 123. Maximum life is about six months and six weeks. Note 04, Section 05.
Q7Direct

Which one of the following committees of a State legislature is drawn entirely from the Legislative Assembly?

(b) Estimates Committee. As at the Union level, the Estimates Committee has no members from the upper House. Members of a Legislative Council may be associated with the Public Accounts Committee, but as associates who cannot vote. Note 04, Section 05.
Q8Assertion–Reason

Consider the following two statements:

  1. Statement-I: A Legislative Council cannot force the resignation of a State government.
  2. Statement-II: The State Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

(a). Both correct, and Statement-II is precisely why Statement-I is true: Article 164(2) makes the Council of Ministers responsible to the Assembly alone. Confidence flows from the lower house at both levels. Note 04, Section 04.
Q9Direct

The power to increase the number of judges of the Supreme Court is vested in

(b) The Parliament. Article 124(1) fixes seven judges besides the CJI 'until Parliament by law prescribes a larger number'. Every change since — 1956, 1960, 1977, 1986, 2008, 2019, 2026 — has been by ordinary Act. Note 05, Section 01.
Q10How many

Consider the following statements about the strength of the Supreme Court:

  1. The Constitution originally provided for a Chief Justice and not more than seven other judges.
  2. The strength was raised from 31 to 34 in 2019 by a constitutional amendment.
  3. The Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026 raised the number of judges other than the Chief Justice to 37.
  4. An Ordinance preceded the 2026 Act.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(c) Only three. Statements 1, 3 and 4 are correct — total sanctioned strength is now 38, and an Ordinance was promulgated on 16 May 2026. Statement 2 is wrong on the instrument: 2019 was an ordinary Act, not a constitutional amendment. Numbers right, instrument wrong — a standard construction. Note 05, Section 01.
Q11Statements

With reference to the collegium system, consider the following statements:

  1. The collegium for appointments to a High Court consists of the Chief Justice of India and the two senior-most judges of the Supreme Court.
  2. The collegium system was established by the Ninety-ninth Constitutional Amendment.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only. The collegium rests entirely on judicial interpretation of the word 'consultation' in Art 124(2) — no amendment created it. The 99th Amendment was the attempt to replace it with the NJAC, and it was struck down in 2015. Remember the two sizes: five for the Supreme Court, three for a High Court. Note 05, Section 02.
Q12Statements

With reference to the National Judicial Appointments Commission, consider the following statements:

  1. It was to consist of six members chaired by the Chief Justice of India.
  2. It was struck down because the Ninety-ninth Amendment had not been ratified by the required number of States.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only. The 99th Amendment was properly ratified; that was never the defect. It fell in October 2015 by 4:1 for violating judicial independence as part of the basic structure — because any two of the six members could veto a recommendation, extinguishing judicial primacy. A statement with the right outcome and the wrong ground is still false. Note 05, Section 02.
Q13How many

Consider the following statements regarding the removal of a judge of the Supreme Court:

  1. The Constitution defines 'proved misbehaviour' and 'incapacity'.
  2. A removal motion requires the signatures of one hundred members of the Lok Sabha or fifty of the Rajya Sabha.
  3. The Speaker or Chairman may refuse to admit the motion.
  4. The motion must be passed by a majority of the total membership of each House and by two-thirds of members present and voting.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(c) Only three. Statements 2, 3 and 4 are correct. Statement 1 is wrong — the Constitution uses both terms and defines neither, which is a standing criticism of the process. The signature thresholds come from the Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968, not the Constitution. Note 05, Section 03.
Q14How many

Consider the following statements about the original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court under Article 131:

  1. It extends to a dispute between the Government of India and a Union Territory.
  2. It extends to a dispute between two or more States.
  3. It extends to disputes arising out of a treaty entered into before the commencement of the Constitution and still in operation.
  4. It requires that a question of legal right be involved.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(b) Only two. Statements 2 and 4 are correct. Statement 1 fails because a Union Territory is not a State. Statement 3 is expressly excluded by Art 131's proviso — pre-Constitution treaty disputes are outside it, and are instead the subject of the Court's compulsory advisory jurisdiction under Art 143(2). Note 05, Section 04.
Q15Negative

With reference to the advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court, which one of the following is NOT correct?

(b) is NOT correct. Under Art 143(1) the Court may decline to answer — and did exactly that in November 2025, returning Questions 12 and 14 of the Presidential Reference unanswered. Only under Art 143(2), for pre-Constitution treaty disputes, must it report. Note 05, Section 04.
Q16Pairs

Consider the following pairs of Article and subject:

  1. Article 129 — Supreme Court as a court of record
  2. Article 137 — review of its own judgments
  3. Article 139A — transfer of cases
  4. Article 143 — special leave to appeal

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(c) Only three. Pair 4 is the mismatch: Article 136 is special leave to appeal; Article 143 is advisory jurisdiction. Pairs 1–3 are correct, and Art 139A was inserted by the 42nd Amendment. Note 05, Sections 04 and 06.
Q17Assertion–Reason

Consider the following two statements:

  1. Statement-I: The Supreme Court held in November 2025 that there can be no 'deemed assent' to a State Bill.
  2. Statement-II: Articles 200 and 201 prescribe no timeline within which the Governor or the President must act on a Bill.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

(a). Both correct and Statement-II is exactly the Court's reasoning — with no timeline there is no expiry, and without an expiry nothing from which assent could be deemed. Work the three steps in order: truth of I, truth of II, then the link. Note 05, Section 05.
Q18Statements

With reference to the High Courts, consider the following statements:

  1. The Constitution provides that there shall be a High Court for each State, and Parliament may establish a common High Court for two or more States.
  2. The number of judges of a High Court is fixed by Parliament by law.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only. Statement 2 reverses the rule: High Court strength is determined by the President, and the Constitution fixes no number. It is Supreme Court strength that Parliament sets by law. Statement 1 combines Arts 214 and 231. Note 06, Section 01.
Q19Negative

Which one of the following statements about a judge of a High Court is NOT correct?

(b) is NOT correct. Resignation goes to the President under Art 217(1)(a) — as Justice Varma's did in April 2026 — even though the oath under Art 219 is administered by the Governor. Two different functionaries in adjacent articles: a ready-made trap. Note 06, Section 01.
Q20How many

Consider the following statements comparing Article 32 and Article 226:

  1. Article 226 may be invoked for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for any other purpose.
  2. The remedy under Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right.
  3. A High Court may decline relief under Article 226 where an alternative remedy exists.
  4. Article 226 is narrower in scope than Article 32.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(c) Only three. Statements 1–3 are correct. Statement 4 is the reversal — Art 226 is wider in scope because of the words 'and for any other purpose'. Art 32 is the stronger guarantee but the narrower door. Both propositions are true at once. Note 06, Section 03.
Q21Direct

Under which one of the following Articles does a High Court exercise superintendence over all courts and tribunals within its territorial jurisdiction?

(b) Article 227. The supervisory jurisdiction, both judicial and administrative, subject to the armed-forces exclusion. The neighbours do different jobs: 226 writs, 228 withdrawal of a constitutional question from a subordinate court, 235 control over the district judiciary. Note 06, Section 02.
Q22Statements

Consider the following statements:

  1. A High Court has no jurisdiction to declare a law made by Parliament unconstitutional.
  2. The Supreme Court's power of superintendence extends over all courts in India.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(d) Neither 1 nor 2. Statement 1 fails because India has an integrated judiciary — a High Court exercising review under Arts 13 and 226 can strike down a central law. Statement 2 fails because superintendence under Art 227 belongs to the High Courts; there is no Supreme Court equivalent. Do not assume a question must contain a true statement. Note 06, Sections 02 and 06.
Q23Pairs

Consider the following pairs of writ and characteristic:

  1. Habeas corpus — available against a private individual
  2. Mandamus — lies to compel performance of a public duty
  3. Prohibition — issued after the lower court has passed its order
  4. Quo warranto — may be sought by any interested person

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(c) Only three. Pair 3 is wrong: prohibition is preventive and is issued while proceedings are pending. The writ issued after an order is certiorari, which is preventive and curative both. Fix the pair by timing, not by definition. Note 06, Section 04.
Q24Assertion–Reason

Consider the following two statements:

  1. Statement-I: A death sentence passed by a sessions court does not take effect until confirmed by the High Court.
  2. Statement-II: Every convict sentenced to death necessarily appeals to the High Court.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

(c). Statement-I is correct; Statement-II is not. Confirmation by the High Court is automatic and mandatory and happens whether or not the convict appeals. This is the classic Assertion–Reason failure mode — a true first statement paired with a plausible but false second. Note 06, Section 02.
Q25How many

  4. Salaries of High Court judges are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(c) Only three. Statements 1–3 are correct. Statement 4 is the split trap: salaries are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State, while pensions are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India. Note 06, Sections 01 and 05.
Q26Pairs

  1. Article 224 — additional and acting judges
  2. Article 224A — retired judges sitting at the request of the High Court's Chief Justice
  3. Article 222 — transfer of a judge from one High Court to another
  4. Article 231 — common High Court for two or more States

(d) All four. Another 'all correct' set. The 224 / 224A pair is the one to fix deliberately: 224 = extra serving judges for up to two years, 224A = a retired judge brought back. Note 06, Section 05.
Q27Negative

(b) is NOT correct. Under Article 233 only the High Court is consulted. The State Public Service Commission appears in Article 234, for posts below district judge. This is the most predictable question in the chapter. Note 07, Section 02.
Q28Statements

With reference to Articles 323A and 323B, consider the following statements:

  1. Tribunals under Article 323A can be established only by Parliament.
  2. Article 323A permits a hierarchy of tribunals whereas Article 323B does not.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only. Statement 2 is exactly reversed: 323B permits a hierarchy; 323A contemplates one tribunal for the Union and one per State, so it does not. Setters reverse a genuine contrast far more often than they invent a false fact. Note 07, Section 03.
Q29How many

Consider the following statements about the Central Administrative Tribunal:

  1. It was established under an Act of Parliament of 1985.
  2. Its jurisdiction extends to members of the defence forces.
  3. It is not bound by the procedure laid down in the Code of Civil Procedure.
  4. Appeals against its orders lie to a division bench of the High Court.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(c) Only three. Statements 1, 3 and 4 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong — the defence forces are expressly excluded, along with Supreme Court officers and parliamentary secretarial staff. Statement 4 became true only after L. Chandra Kumar (1997); before that, appeals went straight to the Supreme Court. Note 07, Section 03.
Q30Direct

In which one of the following cases did the Supreme Court hold that judicial review by the High Courts under Articles 226 and 227 is part of the basic structure?

(b) L. Chandra Kumar (1997). A seven-judge bench which reversed the premise of Sampath Kumar (1987) that tribunals were an effective substitute for High Courts. After 1997 they are supplementary, and no statute can exclude High Court review. Note 07, Sections 03 and 06.
Q31Assertion–Reason

Consider the following two statements:

  1. Statement-I: Provisions of the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021 relating to tenure and minimum age were struck down in November 2025.
  2. Statement-II: A legislature cannot re-enact a provision already invalidated by a court without curing the defect identified.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

(a). Both correct and Statement-II is the Court's stated ground — an impermissible legislative override. The Act had restored a four-year tenure and a minimum age of 50 after the Madras Bar Association judgments required five years and rejected the age bar. A legislature may remove the basis of a judgment; it may not re-enact the rule in new words. Note 07, Section 04.
Q32How many

Consider the following statements about Lok Adalats:

  1. They have jurisdiction over matters pending before a court as well as at the pre-litigation stage.
  2. An award of a Lok Adalat is deemed to be a decree of a civil court and no appeal lies against it.
  3. They cannot take up matrimonial or family disputes.
  4. A Permanent Lok Adalat may decide a dispute on merits where the parties fail to settle.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(c) Only three. Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong — matrimonial and family disputes are taken up; only the divorce decree itself and non-compoundable offences fall outside. Permanent Lok Adalats came in 2002 for public utility services up to ₹1 crore. Note 07, Section 05.
Q33Statements

  2. The presiding officer has the rank and status of a First Class Judicial Magistrate.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(b) 2 only. A Gram Nyayalaya hears both civil and criminal matters — indeed the Nyayadhikari's rank in statement 2 is itself a criminal designation, which contradicts statement 1 from within the same question. It is a mobile court and is not bound by the Indian Evidence Act. Note 07, Section 05.
Q34Negative

Which one of the following is NOT entitled to free legal services irrespective of income under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987?

(d) A senior citizen. Age alone is not one of the income-blind categories. SC/ST members, women, children, persons with disabilities, persons in custody, industrial workmen and mass-disaster victims qualify regardless of income; everyone else is subject to a prescribed ceiling. Note 07, Section 05.
Q35Direct

In India, judicial review implies

(a). The textbook definition, and the question rewards holding it word for word. (b) is the wisdom trap — courts test constitutionality, never policy merit. (c) describes a pre-enactment power India lacks. (d) is Article 137, a different power. Note 08, Sections 01 and 02.
Q36Statements

Consider the following statements:

  1. The Constitution of India defines its basic structure.
  2. The Constitution contains an express provision headed 'judicial review'.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(d) Neither 1 nor 2. Basic structure is judge-made, from Kesavananda Bharati (1973), and deliberately never exhaustively listed. Judicial review has no article of its own — it flows from Arts 13, 32, 226, 131–136, 245–246 and 372 read together. The pattern to distrust: 'the Constitution defines / provides for X'. Note 08, Section 01.
Q37How many

  1. It was made possible by the relaxation of the rule of locus standi.
  2. It was introduced into the Constitution by an amendment.

Q38Pairs

Mains practice — write these out by hand, 10 minutes each

M1. "The collegium system was a remedy for executive dominance that has become a problem of its own." Critically examine in the light of the Fourth Judges Case and subsequent developments. (15 marks · 250 words)
Skeleton: the four Judges Cases as a lineage, not a list → what 1993 fixed (the supersessions of 1973 and 1977) → what it created (no criteria, no reasons, no timelines) → why the NJAC failed too (the two-member veto against judicial primacy, 4:1 in 2015) → Ambedkar rejected both extremes; the unfinalised Memorandum of Procedure is the evidence → procedural reform, not structural.
M2. "Tribunalisation promised speed and expertise but has repeatedly been used to expand executive control over adjudication." Critically examine in the light of recent judicial pronouncements. (15 marks · 250 words)
Skeleton: Part XIV-A and the genuine case for tribunals → the structural defect: the Union is the largest litigant before them → Sampath Kumar (1987) → L. Chandra Kumar (1997), Arts 226/227 review as basic structure → Rojer Mathew (2019) → November 2025, the 2021 Act struck down as a legislative override → National Tribunal Commission and a five-year tenure.
M3. "Judicial activism is the response to institutional failure elsewhere; judicial overreach is its failure to stop." Critically examine with reference to recent constitutional developments. (15 marks · 250 words)
Skeleton: define review, activism and overreach separately → activism as reaction to vacuum (Hussainara Khatoon, Vishaka's sixteen-year gap, Vineet Narain) → where it crossed: Art 142 deeming assent to ten Bills, April 2025 → the self-correction of 20 November 2025, "not an unbridled scope that can negate the separation of powers" → the quotation is half right: the correction came from within, in seven months.
M4. "The High Court, not the Supreme Court, is the workhorse of constitutional remedy in India." Examine with reference to Articles 226 and 227. (10 marks · 150 words)
Skeleton: Art 226 is wider — "and for any other purpose" → Art 227 superintendence has no Part V equivalent → L. Chandra Kumar makes both basic structure → the limits: Art 226 relief is discretionary, and 371 of 1,122 posts lay vacant with pendency above 6.3 crore → sound in law, undermined in practice.
