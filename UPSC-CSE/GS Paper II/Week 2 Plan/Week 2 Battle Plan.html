UPSC question DNA — the archetypes, reverse-engineered

Both the genuine UPSC PYQs already verified across your notes and all 50 Week 1 coaching questions reduce to the same seven recurring shapes. Read Week 2's content today with these shapes in mind — for every fact, ask "which of these seven traps could be built from this?"

ArchetypeHow it's builtYour defence
Statement-counting2-4 statements, asks "how many are correct" rather than "which is correct" — removes the shortcut of eliminating obviously-wrong options.Evaluate every statement independently on paper/in your head before counting — never estimate.
Absolute-option overreachOffers "All of the above" / "Both" / "Neither" as one of four options, banking on you pattern-matching the option's shape instead of testing each clause.Treat "Both"/"All"/"Neither" as requiring you to have verified 100% of the sub-parts — never a default guess.
Confusable-neighbour swapTwo Articles/writs/doctrines that sit near each other in the syllabus (Article 20 vs 22, Prohibition vs Certiorari, Article 21 vs 25) — tests precision, not general awareness.For every pair of "sibling" concepts in a note, explicitly write the one-line distinction — this note's tables already do this; read them as pairs, not singles.
Match-the-pairsScholar-to-label, committee-to-chairman, Article-to-provision — four items, four labels, shuffled.Memorise these as an ordered table, not loose facts — the predicted-question blocks added to today's notes rehearse this exact format.
Assertion / Statement-I-IITwo statements, four options about correctness and whether one explains the other — tests whether you can separate "both true" from "true and causally linked."Always ask two questions separately: (a) is each statement true? (b) does statement II actually explain statement I, or just sit next to it?
NOT-type elimination"Which of the following is NOT a feature of X" — three genuine features plus one plausible-sounding intruder from a neighbouring topic.Read "NOT" twice before answering — the most common self-inflicted error is answering the un-negated question by habit.
Verbatim-wording trapTests the exact text of a well-known passage (Preamble, an Article) by substituting one synonym-but-wrong word.For the handful of passages UPSC quotes verbatim (Preamble, Article 21, Article 32, Article 51A), read the exact text aloud once today, not just the summary.

Mains: the same DNA, different execution

Every genuine Mains PYQ already verified in your notes rewards the same three things, regardless of topic: (1) a liftable opening line that states the thesis in one sentence; (2) a structured middle — a table, a merit/demerit split, or a timeline reproduced from memory, not free-flowing prose; (3) a named source — a case, a committee, or an Article number — closing each point, not a vague assertion. Today's enhanced notes give you a ready-made skeleton for exactly this in each topic's "Mains craft" section — practice reproducing the skeleton from memory, not re-reading it passively.
