Exact test format: +2 per correct, −0.66 per wrong, 0 for skipped. 60 minutes. Covers President, Vice-President, Governor, PM & Council of Ministers, and Parliament (structure, legislative process, budget, sessions, privileges).

Your weak formats are deliberately over-weighted — 8 negative-phrased, 8 "how many", 4 assertion-reason — because that's where your marks are leaking. One question appears twice on purpose. Answer it identically both times.

The five rules (from your own Week 1–2 data): (1) On any "NOT correct" question, mark each statement T/F first, then re-read the stem. (2) First pass: max 60 seconds per question — mark and move. (3) See all 50 by minute 45. (4) If you can't eliminate at least two options, skip — a blank beats a wrong answer by 0.66 marks. (5) On "how many of the above": if two or more statements are genuinely unknown to you, skip.

Q1Statements

With reference to the election of the President of India, consider the following statements:

  1. Nominated members of the Rajya Sabha do not participate in the election.
  2. Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry participate in the election.
  3. Members of State Legislative Councils participate on par with Assembly members.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). MLCs never vote in a Presidential election — only elected MPs and elected MLAs (including Delhi & Puducherry, added by the 70th Amendment, 1992). Nominated members of both Parliament and Assemblies are excluded. Note 00, Section 01.
Q2Direct

The value of votes in a Presidential election is currently computed using the population figures of which Census?

(b). The 1971 Census figures remain frozen for this purpose — a deliberate choice to avoid penalising States that controlled population growth. Note 00, Section 01.
Q3Negative ⚠

With reference to the impeachment of the President (Article 61), which of the following statements is NOT correct?

(d) is NOT correct — impeachment has never been used against any President. The other three are all accurate. If you picked a correct statement, you fell for exactly your 0%-format trap: the stem asked for the false one. Note 00, Section 02.
Q4How many ⚠

Consider the following statements about eligibility for the office of President:

  1. The candidate must have completed 35 years of age.
  2. The candidate must be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha.
  3. A sitting Governor must resign before filing nomination for President.
  4. A President's resignation is addressed to the Chief Justice of India.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(b). Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong — President, VP, Governor and Minister posts are expressly exempted from "office of profit," so a sitting Governor can contest. Statement 4 is wrong — resignation goes to the Vice-President. Note 00, Sections 01–02.
Q5Direct

The classic instance of the Indian President's "pocket veto" involved which Bill?

(b). President Zail Singh simply never acted on it — the Constitution sets no time limit for assent. The other three options are the three joint-sitting Bills (a separate anchor — don't mix the two lists). Note 00, Section 04.
Q6Statements

With reference to a Money Bill presented to the President, consider the following statements:

  1. The President can withhold assent to a Money Bill.
  2. The President can return a Money Bill to the Lok Sabha for reconsideration.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). No suspensive veto exists for Money Bills — they were introduced only on the President's own prior recommendation, so returning one makes no sense. Withholding assent, though rare, is constitutionally open. Note 00, Section 04.
Q7Assertion–Reason ⚠

Statement-I: The President of India may send Cabinet advice back for reconsideration one time, after which the reconsidered advice is binding.
Statement-II: The 44th Amendment, 1978 inserted this one-time reconsideration window into Article 74.

(a). The 42nd Amendment (1976) made advice binding; the 44th (1978) added the one-time return valve. Statement-II is precisely the source of the rule in Statement-I — the explanation link holds. Run the drill: truth of I → truth of II → then the link. Note 00, Fig 1.
Q8Pairs

Consider the following pairs regarding the President's Article 72 power:

  1. Commutation — substitution of one form of punishment for a lighter form
  2. Remission — reducing the period of a sentence without changing its character
  3. Respite — temporarily staying the execution of a sentence

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(b). Pairs 1 and 2 are correct. Pair 3 describes reprieve; respite means awarding a lesser sentence on special grounds (e.g., pregnancy). The five forms — pardon, commutation, remission, respite, reprieve — are a classic pairs bank. Note 00, Section 04.
Q9Negative ⚠

Which of the following is beyond a State Governor's power under Article 161?

(c). Pardoning a death sentence (and anything touching court-martial punishments) is exclusively the President's under Article 72. Everything within State-law offences short of that is within Article 161. Note 00 & Note 01, pardon comparison table.
Q10Direct

The electoral college for the Vice-President's election consists of:

(b). The inverse of the Presidential election on both counts: MLAs are excluded entirely, and nominated MPs DO vote. This paired asymmetry is the single most likely VP question. Note 00, Section 05.
Q11Statements

With reference to the removal of the Vice-President, consider the following statements:

  1. The removal resolution can originate only in the Rajya Sabha.
  2. The resolution must state a specific ground, such as violation of the Constitution.
  3. The Lok Sabha must agree to the resolution for the removal to take effect.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). Unlike the President's Article 61 impeachment, no ground needs to be stated at all for VP removal — RS effective majority + LS simple agreement is the whole process. Note 00, Section 05. (Remember this one — you may see it again.)
Q12Direct

When the office of President falls vacant, a fresh Presidential election must be held within:

(b). The Vice-President officiates in the interim (and while doing so, does not chair the Rajya Sabha — the Deputy Chairman takes over). Note 00, Section 02.
Q13Statements

With reference to the office of Governor, consider the following statements:

  1. The Governor is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal.
  2. The same person can be appointed Governor of two or more States.
  3. The Governor must be a permanent resident of the State to which they are appointed.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). Statement 2 comes from the 7th Amendment, 1956. Statement 3 inverts the actual convention — Governors are conventionally outsiders to the State, and residency is nowhere a legal requirement. Note 01, Section 01.
Q14Negative ⚠

Which of the following statements about the Governor is NOT correct?

(b) is NOT correct. Unlike the President (who must be Lok Sabha-qualified), Article 157 asks only citizenship + 35 years — no legislative qualification at all. Statements (a), (c), (d) are all true. Note 01, Sections 01–02.
Q15Direct

Compared with the President's options on a Bill, the Governor under Article 200 has one additional option, namely:

(c). Assent, withhold, and return-for-reconsideration all mirror the President's own options — reservation is the Governor-only fourth path, and deciding to reserve is itself discretionary. Note 01, Section 04.
Q16Assertion–Reason ⚠

Statement-I: If a State Legislature re-passes a Bill that the President (on reservation) had returned, the President is not obliged to give assent.
Statement-II: The forced-assent rule after reconsideration in Article 200 binds the Governor, not the President dealing with a reserved Bill.

(a). This is the reserved-Bill asymmetry: the Governor's suspensive veto is exhausted by re-passage, but the President's discretion over a reserved Bill never is. Statement-II states exactly why Statement-I holds. Note 01, Section 04.
Q17How many ⚠

Consider the following functions of a Governor:

  1. Reserving a Bill for the consideration of the President
  2. Recommending the imposition of President's Rule in the State
  3. Appointing the Advocate General of the State
  4. Determining royalty amounts payable to autonomous Sixth Schedule district councils

How many of the above are exercised as discretionary functions?

(c). Functions 1, 2 and 4 are discretionary. Appointing the Advocate General is an ordinary executive function done on ministerial advice — the standard "smuggle one routine function into the discretion list" trap. Note 01, Section 04.
Q18Pairs

Consider the following pairs of cases and their holdings:

  1. S.R. Bommai (1994) — a government's majority must be tested on the floor of the House
  2. Rameshwar Prasad (2006) — the 2005 Bihar Assembly dissolution was mala fide
  3. Nabam Rebia (2016) — the Governor may summon the Assembly entirely at his own discretion

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(b). Pairs 1 and 2 are right. Nabam Rebia held the opposite of pair 3 — summoning/proroguing is ordinarily on ministerial advice, and internal party dissidence is no ground for the Governor to act alone. Note 01, Section 05.
Q19Direct

Which Commission recommended that the "during the pleasure of the President" doctrine for Governors be deleted, with removal only through a resolution-based process?

(b). Punchhi (2010) went further than Sarkaria's softer "don't remove before 5 years except rarely" — it proposed deleting the pleasure doctrine outright. Neither has been implemented; Article 156 stands unchanged. Note 01, Section 02.
Q20Negative ⚠

With reference to Article 200, which of the following statements is NOT correct?

(c) is NOT correct. There is no rule requiring every Money Bill to be reserved — reservation is mandatory only in specific situations (e.g., a Bill endangering the High Court's position), otherwise discretionary. (a), (b), (d) are all true. Note 01, Section 04.
Q21Direct

A person who is not a member of either House of Parliament can remain a Minister for a maximum period of:

(b). Six months to get elected to either House, or the office is lost. The same 6-month figure recurs at State level — one number, two contexts. Note 02, Section 01.
Q22Statements

With reference to the 91st Amendment, 2003, consider the following statements:

  1. The total strength of the Council of Ministers, including the Prime Minister, cannot exceed 15% of the Lok Sabha's total membership.
  2. For smaller States, the number of Ministers including the Chief Minister cannot be less than 12.
  3. It barred members disqualified for defection from being appointed Ministers.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(d). All three are correct — the 15% cap (PM counted inside it), the floor of 12 for small States, and the anti-defection linkage all come from the same Amendment. Note 02, Section 03.
Q23How many ⚠

Consider the following statements about the Council of Ministers:

  1. The Cabinet is a smaller subset of the full Council of Ministers.
  2. Ministers of State attend Cabinet meetings as a matter of right.
  3. Deputy Ministers may hold independent charge of a Ministry.
  4. Collective responsibility binds Ministers of all three ranks equally.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(b). Statements 1 and 4 are correct. MoS attend Cabinet only when specially invited (2 is wrong); Deputy Ministers never hold independent charge (3 is wrong). Note 02, Section 03.
Q24Assertion–Reason ⚠

Statement-I: If a no-confidence motion passes in the Lok Sabha, a Minister of State who never attended a single Cabinet meeting must still resign.
Statement-II: Article 75(3) makes the Council of Ministers as a whole — not the Cabinet alone — collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

(a). The whole Council sinks or swims together precisely because 75(3) names the Council, not the Cabinet. The explanation link is direct. Note 02, Section 04.
Q25Negative ⚠

Which of the following statements is NOT correct?

(b) is NOT correct — confidence flows from the directly-elected House only; the Rajya Sabha can embarrass the government but never remove it. (a), (c), (d) are all true. Note 02, Section 04.
Q26Direct

Who is the ex-officio Chairman of NITI Aayog?

(b). And connect the threads: NITI Aayog also nominates a non-voting advisor to each of the five Zonal Councils — the fact that cost you Q16 in Week 2. Note 02, Section 02.
Q27Statements

The phrase "during the pleasure of the President" applies to both the Prime Minister and the Governor. Consider the following statements:

  1. A Governor can, in practice, be removed by the Union government at any time without stated reasons.
  2. A Prime Minister commanding a Lok Sabha majority cannot, in practice, be dismissed by the President.
  3. The phrase therefore has an identical practical effect for both offices.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). Same words, opposite reality: the Governor has no tenure shield at all, while the PM's "pleasure" tracks Lok Sabha confidence. Statement 3 is the giveaway wrong statement. Note 02, Section 01 trap box.
Q28How many ⚠

Consider the following roles of the Prime Minister:

  1. Leader of the House in the chamber to which he or she belongs
  2. Principal channel of communication between the President and the Council of Ministers under Article 78
  3. Ex-officio Chairman of the Finance Commission
  4. Recommending dissolution of the Lok Sabha to the President

How many of the above are correct?

(c). 1, 2 and 4 are correct. The Finance Commission is a separate constitutional body (Article 280) constituted by the President — the PM chairs NITI Aayog, not the Finance Commission. Classic body-swap trap. Note 02, Section 02.
Q29Direct

Under Article 79, the Parliament of India consists of:

(b). The President is an integral part of Parliament without being a member of either House — which is exactly why every Bill needs Presidential assent to become law. Note 01 (Parliament), Section 01.
Q30Statements

With reference to the Rajya Sabha, consider the following statements:

  1. It is a permanent body and is never dissolved.
  2. One-third of its members retire every second year.
  3. Its maximum strength provided by the Constitution is 245.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). The constitutional maximum is 250 (238 + 12 nominated); 245 is the current actual strength. The one-number difference between "maximum" and "current" is the whole question. Note 01 (Parliament), Section 01.
Q31Negative ⚠

With reference to Rajya Sabha elections, which of the following statements is NOT correct?

(b) is NOT correct. The State-specific domicile requirement was relaxed — an elector anywhere in India can contest from any State, upheld in Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2006). Note 01 (Parliament), Section 02.
Q32Direct

The 104th Constitutional Amendment, 2019:

(a). EWS was the 103rd (2019); the delimitation freeze came via the 42nd (1976, extended later). Amendment-number swaps between the 103rd and 104th are an easy trap because both are from 2019. Note 01 (Parliament), Section 01.
Q33How many ⚠

Consider the following minimum-age requirements:

  1. Lok Sabha membership — 25 years
  2. Rajya Sabha membership — 30 years
  3. President — 35 years
  4. Governor — 30 years

How many of the pairs given above are correct?

(c). The Governor's minimum age is 35, not 30. The full ladder: 25 (LS/Assembly) → 30 (RS/Council) → 35 (President, VP, Governor). Notes 00–01 across both topics.
Q34Pairs

Consider the following pairs of body and presiding officer:

  1. Lok Sabha — Speaker
  2. Rajya Sabha — Vice-President as ex-officio Chairman
  3. Joint sitting of both Houses — Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(b). A joint sitting is presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha (then Deputy Speaker, then RS Deputy Chairman) — the RS Chairman never presides over one. Note 01 (Parliament), Section 04.
Q35Statements

With reference to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, consider the following statements:

  1. The Speaker votes only in the case of a tie.
  2. The Speaker's certification of a Bill as a Money Bill is final.
  3. The Speaker's resignation is addressed to the President.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). Resignation goes to the Deputy Speaker — the "one step down" pattern (President→VP, VP→President, Speaker→Deputy Speaker). Note 01 (Parliament), Section 04.
Q36Direct

How many joint sittings of the two Houses of Parliament have been held so far in India's history?

(c). Dowry Prohibition Bill (1961), Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill (1978), POTA (2002). Memorise the trio as a set. Note 01 (Parliament), Section 04.
Q37Statements

With reference to a joint sitting under Article 108, consider the following statements:

  1. It cannot be summoned to resolve a deadlock over a Money Bill.
  2. It cannot be summoned for a Constitutional Amendment Bill.
  3. It is presided over by the President.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). Money Bills need no joint sitting (the Lok Sabha wins automatically after 14 days); Amendment Bills must pass each House separately by special majority. The President summons but the Speaker presides. Notes 01–02 (Parliament).
Q38Direct

The Rajya Sabha must return a Money Bill to the Lok Sabha within:

(b). With or without recommendations — and if it does nothing for 14 days, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses anyway. Note 02 (Parliament), Section 02.
Q39Negative ⚠

With reference to a Financial Bill Category I (Article 117(1)), which of the following statements is NOT correct?

(c) is NOT correct. That 14-day limitation applies only to true Money Bills. A Financial Bill I follows the ordinary-Bill procedure after introduction — the Rajya Sabha can amend or reject it fully. Note 02 (Parliament), Section 02.
Q40How many ⚠

Consider the following matters:

  1. Imposition or abolition of a tax
  2. Regulation of the Union government's borrowing
  3. Appropriation of money out of the Consolidated Fund of India
  4. Imposition of fines or fees by a regulatory authority

A Bill dealing exclusively with how many of the above qualifies as a Money Bill under Article 110?

(c). Matters 1–3 are in the Article 110(1) list. Fines, fees and service payments are expressly outside it — a Bill about them is not a Money Bill merely because money changes hands. Note 02 (Parliament), Section 04.
Q41Assertion–Reason ⚠

Statement-I: Passing a wide-ranging law as a Money Bill effectively removes the Rajya Sabha's ability to block or amend it.
Statement-II: A Bill certified by the Speaker as a Money Bill requires, in effect, only the Lok Sabha's approval to reach the President.

(a). This is the mechanism behind the Aadhaar Act (2016) Money Bill controversy: certification + the 14-day deemed-passage rule together sideline the Rajya Sabha, which explains exactly why the classification battle matters. Note 02 (Parliament), Section 02.
Q42Direct

The notice period required for introducing a Private Member's Bill in Parliament is:

(c). One month, versus 7 days for a Government Bill — and they are taken up essentially only on Fridays, which is why so few have ever become law. Note 02 (Parliament), Section 03.
Q43Statements

With reference to an ordinary Bill, consider the following statements:

  1. It can originate in either House of Parliament.
  2. It goes through three readings in each House.
  3. It is deemed passed if the second House takes no action on it for 14 days.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a). The 14-day deemed-passage rule belongs to Money Bills only. For an ordinary Bill, a second House sitting on it for 6+ months is a ground for a joint sitting — not automatic passage. Note 02 (Parliament), Sections 02–03.
Q44Direct

Under Article 85, the maximum permissible gap between two sessions of Parliament is:

(c). The only hard constitutional rule on session timing — meaning Parliament must meet at least twice a year. The three-sessions-a-year pattern is pure convention. Note 03 (Parliament), Section 01.
Q45Statements

Consider the following statements:

  1. Prorogation of a House is done by the President.
  2. Bills pending in Parliament lapse on prorogation.
  3. Adjournment sine die is done by the presiding officer of the House.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(b). Pending Bills do NOT lapse on prorogation (pending notices do). Bills lapse only on dissolution, and even then with exceptions — see the next question. Note 03 (Parliament), Section 01.
Q46How many ⚠

On the dissolution of the Lok Sabha, consider the following Bills:

  1. A Bill pending in the Lok Sabha
  2. A Bill passed by the Lok Sabha but pending in the Rajya Sabha
  3. A Bill pending in the Rajya Sabha that has not yet been passed by the Lok Sabha
  4. A Bill passed by both Houses and awaiting the President's assent

How many of the above lapse?

(b). Bills 1 and 2 lapse. Bill 3 (originating and still sitting in the never-dissolved Rajya Sabha) survives, and Bill 4 (past both Houses) survives. Verify each independently — the format punishes half-knowledge. Note 03 (Parliament), Section 01 trap box.

Q48Negative ⚠

With reference to parliamentary privileges, which of the following statements is NOT correct?

(c) is NOT correct. Freedom from arrest covers civil cases only (session + 40 days either side) — criminal arrest is never protected. This civil/criminal line is the most-tested privileges fact. Note 03 (Parliament), Section 04.
Q49Statements

Consider the following statements about cut motions:

  1. A Policy Cut motion seeks to reduce a Demand for Grant to Re. 1.
  2. An Economy Cut motion seeks to reduce the Demand by a specific amount.
  3. A Token Cut motion seeks to reduce the Demand by Rs. 100 to voice a specific grievance.

Q50How many ⚠

Consider the following items of expenditure:

  1. Salaries of Supreme Court judges
  2. Salary of the Comptroller and Auditor General

Mains practice — write these out by hand, 10 minutes each

M1. "The President of India is a constitutional head, but not a rubber stamp." Discuss with reference to the situations in which the President exercises genuine discretion. (150 words)
Skeleton: Intro — 42nd/44th Amendment arc (advice binding, one return allowed). Body — hung-Parliament PM choice; dismissal of a ministry that lost majority; dissolution advice from a defeated ministry; the Article 74 return valve; pocket-veto timing (Zail Singh, 1986). Conclusion — discretion survives at the edges the text leaves open, which is precisely where constitutional crises occur.
M2. The Governor's discretionary space has been progressively narrowed by judicial review rather than constitutional amendment. Examine with reference to S.R. Bommai (1994), Rameshwar Prasad (2006) and Nabam Rebia (2016). (250 words)
Skeleton: Intro — Article 163's textual discretion vs. its practical shrinkage. Body — Bommai: floor test + Art 356 reviewability; Rameshwar Prasad: mala fide dissolution struck down (but not undone — note the remedy gap); Nabam Rebia: summoning on advice, not discretion; Punchhi's unimplemented fixed-tenure fix. Conclusion — courts, not Parliament, built the modern limits; the office's accountability question remains open.
M3. "The Money Bill route has quietly shifted the balance of bicameralism in India." Critically examine with reference to Article 110 and the Speaker's certification power. (250 words)
Skeleton: Intro — Article 110's "exclusively" test. Body — Speaker's final, non-justiciable certification; 14-day deemed passage; the Aadhaar Act 2016 controversy; Rajya Sabha's reduction to an advisory chamber for anything so certified; contrast with Financial Bills I/II where bicameralism survives. Conclusion — a procedural device with structural consequences; reform proposals (judicial reviewability of certification) worth one line.
