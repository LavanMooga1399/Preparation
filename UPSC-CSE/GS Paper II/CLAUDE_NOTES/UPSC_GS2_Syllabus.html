01

The character of this paper

Before deciding how much of each topic to do, understand what kind of paper GS-II is. This single realisation reshapes your whole approach:

GS-II is a hybrid — a static skeleton (Constitution & institutions) wrapped in a current-affairs skin.

You cannot mug it. Across recent years, well over half the questions ask you to apply a static concept to something in the news — a bill, a judgment, a scheme, a summit. So your preparation runs on two tracks at once: a one-time static push, and a continuous current-affairs drip that never stops until the exam.

02

Weightage — where the marks live

The four clusters are never split evenly. Trend analysis of recent years shows Polity dominating, with the other three trailing. Weight decides where your hours go — not the number of syllabus lines.

◆ Why the overlap is a gift

A single question on RTI is Governance and Polity and Social Justice at once. Because UPSC blurs boundaries, covering the core well pays across multiple clusters — you're never studying just one box.

Polity & Constitution

PRIORITY 1MOSTLY STATIC9 topics

  • 2Functions and responsibilities of the Union and the States, issues and challenges pertaining to the federal structure, devolution of powers and finances up to local levels and challenges therein.

Two-track routine — static vs dynamic

The static/dynamic split dictates your daily habit, not just your syllabus allocation.

05

Initial heads-up — what to do first

If sequencing GS-II from a cold start, this is the order that gives the best return on effort:

1

Lock the Polity static core

Largest, most stable, highest-overlap block — and it makes the other three easier. Bank it early from a standard text, then revise.

2

Make Social Justice your differentiator

Most aspirants write it poorly, so a little extra effort visibly lifts your rank. Stock up on schemes, committees and data.

3

Keep IR light but continuous

Frameworks + headlines, never rote. Build country/grouping templates and update them with the news.

4

Let Governance ride the coattails

Cheapest cluster — it piggybacks on the other three plus a thin layer of 2nd ARC concepts. Don't over-invest.

Mental model: Polity is the foundation, Social Justice the differentiator, IR the current-affairs test, and Governance the connective tissue.

06

The method — how to read any syllabus

The transferable skill. Run any paper's syllabus through these six lenses and you'll produce this same terrain map yourself.

LENS 1

Decompose

Break each fat syllabus line into its smallest examinable units. One point often hides 3–4 topics.

LENS 2

Cluster

Group the units into natural families. This shows where the mass of the paper sits.

LENS 3

Weight

Assign each cluster a rough mark-share from PYQ frequency. The step that decides where your hours go.

LENS 4

Classify static vs dynamic

Book-answered or newspaper-answered? This decides how you study each unit, not just how much.

LENS 5

Spot anchors & repeaters

Find the 3–4 themes UPSC returns to yearly in each cluster. These are your non-negotiables.

LENS 6

Map the overlaps

Note where topics bleed across GS papers, Essay and interview. High-overlap = best return on effort.

◆ One honest caveat

The weightage percentages are directional averages from trend analysis, not official counts — UPSC publishes no weightage and it fluctuates year to year. Before finalising your strategy, count the last 3–4 years' actual papers yourself; doing that count is the exercise, and it cements the terrain far better than reading mine.
