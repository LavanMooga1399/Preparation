Indian Constitution — Salient Features & the Preamble

Senior-mentor notes on the "features" element of Topic 1's syllabus line: what makes the Indian Constitution distinctive, where each feature was borrowed from, and the Preamble's exact wording, key terms, and legal status.

◆ How this note fits the wider set

The GS-II syllabus line for Topic 1 has six elements: historical underpinnings, evolution, features, amendments, significant provisions, basic structure. This file covers "features" — the Constitution's salient characteristics and the Preamble that summarises them. It follows Note 1 (Historical Underpinnings & Evolution) and precedes the notes on Union & Territory, Citizenship, Amendment, Basic Structure, and Fundamental Rights, each kept as its own file for focused revision.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

Imagine you just met India's Constitution for the first time and had thirty seconds to describe its personality. "Salient features" is exactly that exercise, done properly: not the fine legal detail of any one Article, but the handful of big design choices that make this Constitution recognisably itself — is power split between a centre and states, or kept in one place? Can ordinary citizens sue the government if it breaks its own promises? Can the rulebook be changed easily, or is it deliberately hard to change? Answer six or seven questions like that, and you have described the whole Constitution's character in outline.

The Preamble is simply the Constitution's own thirty-second answer to that same exercise — one paragraph, placed right at the start, in which the framers describe in their own words what kind of country they were building and why. Because it is short, memorable, and was written last (after the rest of the Constitution was mostly settled), it works almost like a mission statement: read it, and you already know the spirit the 395 Articles that follow are supposed to serve.

Everything below is really just unpacking those two ideas in exam-ready detail: first, the list of "personality traits" (Section 02), then a word-by-word tour of the mission statement itself (Section 03).

Syllabus placement & the logical chain

Official GS-II syllabus wording (verbatim): "Indian Constitution— historical underpinnings, evolution, features, amendments, significant provisions and basic structure."

Once the historical build-up and the Constituent Assembly's work are clear (Note 1), the natural next question is: what did all that effort actually produce? Salient Features answers that at the level of character and design; the Preamble answers it in the Constitution's own words — its self-description of what kind of state India chose to be.

02

Salient Features of the Constitution

This sub-part is "identify and don't confuse" territory. Prelims rarely asks for a definition of a feature — it asks you to place a fact under the right feature, or to spot which country a borrowed provision actually came from. Mains uses these features as ready-made analytical lenses for almost any Polity answer: is a given development consistent with India's federal, secular, or parliamentary character?

The Constituent Assembly did not invent from a blank page. Dr. Ambedkar himself acknowledged that the Constitution borrows liberally, adapting the best available provisions from other constitutions to Indian conditions. That borrowing, combined with genuinely original choices — a single, integrated judiciary; a blend of federal and unitary features unlike any other federation — gives the Constitution its distinctive character.

🔴 Prelims — verbatim-memorisable facts

●Original Constitution: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules.
●DPSP borrowed from Ireland; Fundamental Duties from the USSR (added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976).
●Concurrent List and joint sitting of Houses borrowed from Australia.
●Amendment procedure (Art. 368) itself borrowed from South Africa.
●Suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency traces to the Weimar Constitution (Germany).
●India is a "Union of States" (Art. 1) — the word "federal" never appears in the Constitution.
●K.C. Wheare's label for India: "quasi-federal."
⚠️

Trap: Do not mix up "borrowed from Ireland" (DPSP, Presidential election method, RS nominations) with "borrowed from USSR" (Fundamental Duties, ideal of justice in Preamble) — Prelims regularly swaps these two source-country pairs in statement questions.

03

The Preamble

The Preamble looks like a formality. It is not. It is the Constitution's own summary of itself, and every key word in it — Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic, Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity — carries case law and syllabus links. It is worth being able to write from memory: Prelims has tested exact recall of its legal status more than once.

The Preamble is based on the Objective Resolution moved by Nehru on 13 December 1946 (covered in Note 1) — adopted with only cosmetic changes. It was enacted along with the rest of the Constitution on 26 November 1949, but unlike every other part, it has been amended only once, by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, which inserted three words: "Socialist," "Secular," and "Integrity."

Key words in the Preamble

WordMeaningStatus
SovereignIndia is internally supreme and externally free of any outside control; Commonwealth membership is voluntary and doesn't dilute this.Original, 1950.
SocialistCommitment to reducing inequality and securing a welfare state via a "mixed economy" model — democratic socialism, not Soviet-style state ownership.Added by 42nd Amendment, 1976.
SecularThe State has no religion of its own and treats all religions with equal respect ("principled distance"), not a rigid Western wall of separation.Word added 1976; the character was implicit from 1950 via Articles 25–28.
Positive vs negative secularism — the exact distinction testedThe Western (negative) concept means a complete wall of separation between religion and State: the State neither aids nor interferes with any religion, and stays wholly aloof. The Indian (positive) concept means the State keeps no religion of its own but gives equal respect and equal protection to all — and may actively intervene in religious affairs for social reform (abolishing untouchability under Article 17, opening Hindu temples to all classes under Article 25(2)(b), regulating secular activities associated with religious practice). India funds minority educational institutions and legislates on religious endowments precisely because our secularism is engaged, not aloof. Trap statement: "The Indian Constitution embodies the negative concept of secularism" is FALSE — India is positive/principled-distance secularism; the negative concept is the Western one.
DemocraticCovers political democracy (universal franchise, responsible government) as well as social and economic democracy.Original, 1950.
RepublicHead of State (President) is elected, directly or indirectly, for a fixed term — not a hereditary monarch.Original, 1950.
Justice — social, economic, politicalAbsence of discrimination (social); fair distribution of wealth (economic); equal political rights (political). Traced to the Russian Revolution's ideals.Original, 1950.
LibertyOf thought, expression, belief, faith and worship — traced to the French Revolution.Original, 1950.
EqualityOf status and of opportunity.Original, 1950.
FraternityA sense of brotherhood assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation."Integrity" added 1976; rest original.

Is the Preamble "part of" the Constitution? — the doctrine's evolution

🔴 Prelims — verbatim-memorisable facts

●Preamble amended only once — 42nd CAA, 1976 — added Socialist, Secular, Integrity.
●Berubari (1960): not part of Constitution → Kesavananda Bharati (1973): reversed, IS part → LIC of India (1995): reaffirmed.
●Preamble is not enforceable in a court of law on its own (non-justiciable).
●Preamble draws directly from the Objective Resolution (Nehru, 13 Dec 1946).
●Preamble is amendable under Article 368, but not so as to damage the basic structure it itself expresses.
●Dr. Balram Singh v. Union of India (Nov 2024): SC dismissed the challenge to "socialist" and "secular" — both words stay in the Preamble.
✚

Recent-judgment layer — the socialist/secular challenge finally decided (2024). Petitions (including by former MPs) argued the 42nd Amendment's insertion of "Socialist" and "Secular" was invalid — passed during the Emergency, allegedly against the framers' intent (the Assembly had consciously debated and declined to add both words in 1948-49). In Dr. Balram Singh v. Union of India (November 2024), the Supreme Court dismissed the challenge, holding: the amending power under Article 368 extends to the Preamble; "socialist" in India's context means a welfare state committed to equality of opportunity — it does not bind India to any particular economic model and has never prevented private enterprise; and "secularism" was already part of the basic structure per Kesavananda and S.R. Bommai — the 1976 insertion made explicit what was implicit from 1950. Exam anchors: adoption-date argument rejected (a Preamble carrying the 1949 date can still be validly amended later); both words upheld; secularism as basic structure reaffirmed.

🌱 Concept in plain English — the three justices, and what "distributive justice" means

The Preamble promises justice in three forms, and the trio is a tested set: social justice (no discrimination by caste, creed, sex — equal social standing), economic justice (no discrimination by wealth or income — narrowing rich-poor gaps), and political justice (equal access to political power — one person, one vote). Now the term that caught the Week 1 test out: distributive justice is the combination of social and economic justice — the idea that a just society must actively redistribute opportunity and resources, not merely referee a neutral contest. Its intellectual momentum worldwide came from socialist thought — the Russian Revolution of 1917 is the standard historical marker for socio-economic rights entering constitutional vocabularies globally, which is why our Preamble's justice clauses read "social, economic and political" in that deliberate order, reversing the French Revolution's purely political liberty-equality-fraternity emphasis.

↓ One line to retain: distributive justice = social + economic justice combined, delivered through redistribution.

⚠️

Trap: "Secular" and "Socialist" are commonly assumed to be original 1950 words — they are not. Only "Integrity" in "unity and integrity of the Nation" is the third word the 42nd Amendment added; "unity of the Nation" alone was the 1950 original phrase.

Value-additions to drop into any answer: (1) Name-drop precisely — Sir Ivor Jennings' "quasi-federal" framing alongside K.C. Wheare's, to show awareness of competing scholarly labels, not just one; (2) Analogy — the Preamble as the Constitution's identity card, reissued exactly once (1976) when its core facts changed; (3) Current link — Supreme Court references to "constitutional morality" and the basic structure doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati) continue to anchor live debates on the validity of recent amendments and ordinances, a ready governance-and-constitutionalism hook.

05

PYQ analysis (2013 onward)

Trend verdict — stated plainly. The Preamble is tested directly and often, especially its legal status and key words. "Salient Features" as a standalone list is rarely asked head-on in a genuine, verifiable PYQ — it is tested piecemeal through its individual components (federalism, secularism, DPSP, Fundamental Duties), each of which gets its own dedicated PYQ-rich note later in this series (Amendment/Basic Structure, Fundamental Rights). A number of "Salient Features MCQs" circulate on coaching sites without a traceable UPSC source; this file does not cite them as genuine.

M6Practice · Week 1 test10 marks · 150 words

"The Preamble is the manifestation of the basic fundamental values and philosophy on which the entire Constitution of India has been built." Discuss.

Directive: "Discuss" a quoted claim. Test it rather than merely endorse it. The strongest structure: how the claim is true (the values), how it is legally real (the case law), and the qualification (non-justiciability) — then a verdict.
Skeleton
  1. Intro: source in the Objectives Resolution
  2. The values it declares
  3. Its legal status — Berubari → Kesavananda → 2024
  4. The limitation — not enforceable
  5. Verdict

Model answer

Based on the Objectives Resolution (13 December 1946), the Preamble is the Constitution's own summary of itself — K.M. Munshi's "horoscope of our sovereign democratic republic."

The values it declares

Source of authority ("We, the People"); nature of the State — Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic; and the objectives of justice, liberty, equality and fraternity. Each is operationalised elsewhere: Part III delivers liberty and equality, Part IV social and economic justice, Article 51A fraternity.

Its legal status — the arc

Berubari Union (1960) held the Preamble was not part of the Constitution; Kesavananda Bharati (1973) reversed this, holding it part of the Constitution and reflective of the basic structure; LIC of India (1995) reaffirmed it; Dr. Balram Singh (2024) upheld the 42nd Amendment's insertion of "socialist" and "secular", confirming the Preamble is amendable yet substantively protected.

The qualification

It is not directly enforceable and confers no power; its force is interpretive. The claim nonetheless holds — the Preamble is not decorative but the declared philosophy the rest of the document operationalises.

M72015 · GS-II12 marks · 200 words

The concept of cooperative federalism has been increasingly emphasised in recent years. Highlight the drawbacks in the existing structure and the extent to which cooperative federalism would answer the shortcomings.

Directive: "Highlight… and the extent to which…" Two demands: identify drawbacks, then deliver a degree judgement on whether cooperative federalism cures them. The second half is where most answers under-perform — they praise cooperative federalism instead of testing it.
Skeleton
  1. Intro: definition + why emphasised
  2. Drawbacks — legislative, administrative, fiscal
  3. What cooperative federalism cures
  4. What it cannot cure
  5. Degree verdict + way forward

Model answer

Cooperative federalism means Centre and States acting as partners in a common national enterprise rather than as rival power centres — an emphasis strengthened by GST, NITI Aayog and the pandemic response.

Drawbacks in the existing structure

Legislative: Union dominance through Articles 249, 250 and 253, and the repugnancy rule in Article 254. Administrative: misuse of Article 356 — checked but not eliminated by S.R. Bommai (1994) — and friction over the Governor's office, most recently the Bill-assent litigation of 2025. Fiscal: dependence on Union transfers, a shrinking divisible pool as cesses and surcharges rise, and borrowing limits under Article 293, now contested in Kerala's Article 131 suit.

What cooperative federalism answers

The GST Council (Article 279A) has delivered consensus-based tax decisions; the Inter-State Council (Article 263) and Zonal Councils enable dialogue; NITI Aayog replaced directive planning with consultation.

What it cannot answer

It is a convention, not a constitutional guarantee. In Mohit Minerals (2022) the Court held GST Council recommendations non-binding, describing Indian federalism as "a dialogue between cooperative and uncooperative federalism." Structural imbalance in the Seventh Schedule, and Article 356 itself, survive untouched. Durable reform needs the Sarkaria and Punchhi Commission proposals on the Governor and a transparent, formula-based fiscal transfer.

Value additions used: Arts 249 / 250 / 253 / 254 / 356 / 293 / 279A / 263 / 131 · Bommai (1994) · Mohit Minerals (2022) + its quotable line · Kerala's pending Art 131 suit · cess-and-surcharge issue · Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions.

Bought: the Sarkaria Commission alongside Punchhi — two named commissions for four words, and the pair is what examiners expect on Centre–State reform.
What would cost you marks: treating cooperative federalism as an unqualified good — the question explicitly asks "to what extent"; covering only fiscal drawbacks and omitting the legislative and administrative limbs.

  • 395 / 22 / 8 — original Articles / Parts / Schedules.
  • GoI Act 1935 → federal scheme, Governor, PSCs, emergency powers.

  • South Africa → Art. 368 amendment procedure.
  • India = "Union of States" (Art. 1) — "federal" never used; K.C. Wheare: quasi-federal.
  • Preamble amended once — 42nd CAA, 1976 — added Socialist, Secular, Integrity.
  • Berubari (1960) not part → Kesavananda Bharati (1973) IS part → LIC of India (1995) reaffirmed.

  • Essential federal-character marker (2021 PYQ): an independent judiciary, not merely elected representation.
  • India's federalism is not a "coming-together" agreement among pre-existing units — it's a unilateral constitutional creation (2017 PYQ).
