Indian Constitution — Historical Underpinnings & Evolution

◆ How this note fits the wider set

The official GS-II syllabus line reads: "Indian Constitution — historical underpinnings, evolution, features, amendments, significant provisions and basic structure." That is six distinct examinable units, not one. Each unit gets its own file so that revision means opening the one file you need, not scrolling through unrelated material.

This file covers the first two units — historical underpinnings (colonial constitutional evolution, 1773–1947) and the Constitution's own evolution through the Constituent Assembly (1946–1950). These two are merged into a single file because they are one continuous narrative — the second cannot be understood without the first.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

Why does a note about India's Constitution start with a list of British colonial laws from the 1700s? Because the Constitution was not written on a blank page in 1946. For 175 years before that, the British were already running India through a series of Acts of their own Parliament — each one patching the failures of the last, and each one quietly teaching Indian leaders what to keep and what to throw out when their turn came to write a Constitution of their own. So this section is really just a "prototype history" — a list of earlier, cruder attempts at the same job (organising who governs India, and how), read in order so you can see the design slowly improving.

One Act on this list matters far more than the rest: the Government of India Act, 1935. Think of it as the last, most sophisticated prototype — so sophisticated that when Indian leaders finally sat down to write the real Constitution, they borrowed roughly two-thirds of its structure wholesale rather than starting over. Once you hold that one fact — "1935 is the blueprint" — the rest of the timeline below is just filling in which earlier Act contributed which smaller piece.

The second half of this note then covers a different question entirely: once India was finally free to write its own Constitution from scratch (1946 onward), who actually did the writing, and how long did it take? That is the Constituent Assembly story — a specific group of people, meeting in specific sessions, over roughly three years.

02

Historical Background — from Company rule to Independence

Treat this sub-part as pure static recall — one focused sitting is enough. The examiner does not ask for an "analysis" of the Regulating Act; the task is to place each Act correctly on a timeline and not confuse it with its neighbours. The one Act to know cold is 1935 — it is the direct blueprint for roughly two-thirds of the present Constitution.

India's constitutional journey did not begin in 1950 — it was assembled piece by piece across 175 years of colonial administration, each Act patching the failures of the last. Two broad eras matter: Company rule (1773–1858), when Parliament in London slowly clipped the East India Company's political power, and Crown rule (1858–1947), when India was administered directly by Britain while Indians were let in — grudgingly and incrementally — to the legislative process.

Act / YearWhat it actually didWhy UPSC cares
Regulating Act, 1773First step by British Parliament to control Company's affairs; created the office of Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings first), subsuming the Bombay and Madras presidencies under Bengal's control; also established a Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774)."First" markers are classic prelims bait — first GG of Bengal, first step of parliamentary control. Note what it did not do: it gave the Governor-General no veto power over his council — a real trap in statement-based questions.
Amending Act, 1781 (Act of Settlement)Passed to fix defects in the Regulating Act, 1773; exempted the Governor-General and Council — and the Company's servants — from the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court at Calcutta for acts done in their official capacity, and excluded revenue matters from the Court's jurisdiction entirely.The Act most candidates skip between 1773 and 1784 — tests as a "which Act carved the executive out of the Supreme Court's reach" fact.
Pitt's India Act, 1784Introduced dual control — Court of Directors (commercial) and Board of Control (political) — Company's political affairs subordinated to the Crown.Distinguish "regulate" (1773) from "dual control/subordinate" (1784).
Charter Acts, 1793–1853Periodic renewals of Company's trading charter; 1833 ended Company's commercial functions entirely (became purely administrative), centralised legislative power in Governor-General of India; 1853 introduced open competition for civil service and separated legislative & executive functions of the Council for the first time.1833 = Company becomes administrative-only; 1853 = first sign of a separate legislature.
Government of India Act, 1858Crown took over administration from the Company after the 1857 revolt; created Secretary of State for India (accountable to British Parliament) and the post of Viceroy.Marks the hard boundary between "Company rule" and "Crown rule."
Indian Councils Act, 1861Began associating Indians with law-making (non-official Indians nominated to the Viceroy's council); restored legislative power to Bombay & Madras; laid the base of the portfolio system."First" Indians in legislature — a recurring prelims fact.
Indian Councils Act, 1892Introduced indirect elections (still nomination-based in substance) and a limited power to discuss the budget and ask questions.Do not confuse with 1909 — 1892 gave a voice, not a separate electorate.
Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto)Introduced separate electorates for Muslims — the single most consequential and controversial feature of this Act.1909 = separate electorate. High-frequency trap against 1919/1935.
Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford)Introduced dyarchy in the provinces (subjects split into "transferred" and "reserved"); bicameral central legislature with some directly elected members; separate electorates extended; created the office of High Commissioner for India, resident in London, taking over some of the Secretary of State's functions; required at least half the members of the Secretary of State's council to have 10 years' public-service experience in India; also set up a Central Public Service Commission.1919 = dyarchy at the provincial level only. Contrast sharply with 1935. Two favourite numeric traps: the High Commissioner post is 1919, not 1935 or 1858; and the experience threshold is exactly 10 years, not 15.
Government of India Act, 1935Proposed an All-India Federation (British provinces + princely states — never actually came into force); introduced provincial autonomy (dyarchy abolished in provinces); introduced dyarchy at the Centre instead; established the Federal Court (1937) and the Reserve Bank of India (1935); Governors given special/discretionary powers.The single most important Act — roughly 250+ articles and most schedules of India's Constitution trace back to this one law (federal scheme, Governor's office, Public Service Commissions, emergency provisions, administrative details).
Indian Independence Act, 1947Ended British paramountcy, partitioned British India into India and Pakistan, made the Constituent Assembly a fully sovereign law-making body.This is the Act that freed the Constituent Assembly to write a wholly original Constitution.

03

Making of the Constitution — the Constituent Assembly

This is the highest-yield sub-part of Topic 1. Prelims tests exact dates, numbers and "who chaired what"; Mains tests whether a candidate can argue that three years was a remarkably short time for a document this comprehensive — and credit the Government of India Act, 1935 for that speed (a genuine, verified PYQ — see below).

The idea of a Constituent Assembly was first floated by M.N. Roy in 1934 and formally demanded by the Congress in 1935; Britain conceded it only through the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946. The Assembly was indirectly elected — members were chosen by the elected members of the provincial legislative assemblies through a single, transferable vote system of proportional representation, with princely states nominating their own representatives.

⚠️

High-frequency trap (a real 2023 Prelims statement): A statement claims the Drafting Committee was set up "on 26th November 1949." This is false — the Drafting Committee was set up on 29 August 1947; 26 November 1949 is the date the Constitution was adopted, over two years later. Do not let the shared date "26 November" fuse these two facts in memory.

04

Mains craft — model intro & value-additions

Model intro line (liftable): "That a nation as vast and diverse as India could draft a wholly original, 395-article Constitution in under three years was possible only because the Constituent Assembly did not start from a blank page — it stood on the administrative scaffolding the Government of India Act, 1935 had already built."

✚

Value-additions to drop into any answer: (1) Name-drop precisely — B.N. Rau as Constitutional Advisor (often forgotten in favour of Ambedkar alone); (2) Analogy — the 1935 Act as the "load-bearing skeleton" onto which the Assembly added democratic and rights-based "flesh"; (3) Current link — 26 November 2024 marked 75 years of the Constitution's adoption, observed with a special joint sitting of Parliament and a year-long "Hamara Samvidhan, Hamara Samman" campaign — the freshest possible hook for any historical-underpinnings answer.

📖 Constituent Assembly ammunition — answering the "slavish imitation" charge

The single best Mains upgrade for this note: Assembly members themselves attacked the draft as a "slavish imitation" of the 1935 Act — "a lawyer's paradise," carried "bag and baggage" from colonial legislation. Ambedkar's reply (worth quoting near-verbatim): there is nothing wrong in borrowing, since "nobody holds any patent rights in the fundamental ideas of a Constitution"; what was borrowed was administrative machinery, while the animating principles — universal adult franchise, fundamental rights, an independent judiciary enforcing them — were precisely what the 1935 Act lacked. He added that constitutions the world over inevitably resemble each other because the terrain of government is common; the test of originality is adaptation to the country's needs, not novelty of parts. Pair the charge with this reply and any "how original is the Indian Constitution?" question is fully armed — criticism, rebuttal, and a verdict.

✚

Why the Constitution won widespread acceptance — the tested factor-set. A Week 1 test question asked this directly, so hold the standard three-factor answer: (1) Inclusive Assembly composition — though elected on a limited franchise, the Constituent Assembly deliberately included representatives of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, women, and religious minorities including Indian Christians, Muslims, Sikhs and Parsis, so every major community could see itself in the document; (2) the Congress party's broad national legitimacy — as the vehicle of the freedom movement it commanded acceptance across regions and classes, and its Assembly dominance transferred that legitimacy to the Constitution; (3) consensus-style working — nearly three years of open debate, amendment, and accommodation rather than majority steam-rolling, so even defeated positions had been visibly heard. The exam-precise caution: acceptance came from representativeness-by-design and process, not from universal adult franchise in the Assembly's own election — that franchise arrived only with the first general election of 1951-52.

M32015 · GS-I12 marks · 200 words

It would have been difficult for the Constituent Assembly to complete its historic task of drafting the Constitution for Independent India in just three years but for the experience gained with the Government of India Act, 1935. Discuss.

Directive: "Discuss." A reasoned debate ending in a position. The statement is a claim — your job is to test it, not simply agree. So: evidence for the claim, then the significant qualification, then a verdict.
Skeleton
  1. Intro: the timeline fact (2 yrs 11 mths 18 days)
  2. What the 1935 Act supplied — five borrowings
  3. What it could not supply — the democratic core
  4. Verdict: scaffolding, not blueprint

Model answer

The Constituent Assembly framed a 395-article Constitution in 2 years, 11 months and 18 days. That speed is inexplicable without the administrative template already supplied by the Government of India Act, 1935, from which roughly two-thirds of the Constitution's structure derives.

What the 1935 Act supplied

The federal scheme with its three-fold division of powers, carried into Article 246 and the Seventh Schedule; provincial autonomy, matured into responsible government at both levels; the Federal Court, reconstituted as the Supreme Court under Article 124; the office of Governor; emergency provisions; and the public service commissions. Much drafting was therefore adaptation, not invention.

What it could not supply

The 1935 Act was a colonial statute without a soul: no Fundamental Rights, no Directive Principles, no universal adult franchise, no popular sovereignty. Those came from the Objectives Resolution (13 December 1946) and the Assembly's own deliberation. The speed also owed much to B.N. Rau's draft, the committee system, and Congress dominance enabling consensus.

The claim is therefore largely valid but incomplete. The 1935 Act was scaffolding that saved time on machinery; the Assembly supplied the democratic and rights-based architecture that made the document a Constitution rather than an administrative code — as Ambedkar replied to the "slavish imitation" charge, "nobody holds any patent rights in the fundamental ideas of a Constitution."

Value additions used: 2y 11m 18d · Art 246 + Seventh Schedule · Art 124 · Objectives Resolution 13 Dec 1946 · B.N. Rau draft · committee system · Ambedkar's patent-rights reply.
Budget check: 217 / 200 words. Cut: provincial bicameralism from the borrowings list, and the standalone sentence introducing the alternative explanations for the Assembly's speed. Bought: the exact date of the Objectives Resolution, and tighter phrasing that lets the "scaffolding vs architecture" verdict land as one clean sentence.
What would cost you marks: agreeing with the statement uncritically — "discuss" requires the counter-side; listing 1935 Act features without connecting each to a present-day Article; ignoring the other reasons for the speed.
M4Practice · Week 1 test10 marks · 150 words

How far does the Constitution of India owe its genesis to the Government of India Act, 1935? Discuss.

Directive: "How far… Discuss." A degree question. It must not end in yes or no — it must end in how much. Structure: the extent it is true → the extent it is not → the net degree stated in words.
Skeleton
  1. Intro: the ~two-thirds claim
  2. Extent of the debt — structural borrowings
  3. Limits of the debt — what 1935 lacked
  4. Verdict stated as a degree

Model answer

The Government of India Act, 1935 supplies roughly two-thirds of the Constitution's structure — a substantial but not total debt.

The extent of the debt

Structurally it is large: the federal scheme and three-fold division of powers (Article 246, Seventh Schedule); provincial autonomy; the Federal Court, reconstituted as the Supreme Court (Article 124); the office of Governor; emergency provisions; and the public service commissions.

The limits of the debt

Philosophically it is small. The Act had no Fundamental Rights, no Directive Principles, no universal adult franchise and no popular sovereignty; it was controlled devolution, not self-government. Those came from the Objectives Resolution (1946). Other features were borrowed elsewhere — Fundamental Rights from the USA, Directive Principles from Ireland, parliamentary government from Britain, suspension of rights during emergency from the Weimar Constitution.

The genesis is therefore substantially but not wholly traceable to 1935: the Act supplied the machinery, the Assembly the mandate. Ambedkar's defence stands — borrowing administrative form is legitimate, since "nobody holds any patent rights in the fundamental ideas of a Constitution."

Value additions used: two-thirds figure · Art 246 + Seventh Schedule · Art 124 · Objectives Resolution · borrowing sources (USA / Ireland / Britain / Weimar) · Ambedkar quotation.
Budget check: 170 / 150 words. Cut: the repeated "administrative vocabulary of Indian governance" phrase and the restated list of what the Assembly added. Bought: the Weimar source for emergency suspension of rights — a fourth borrowing source at the cost of six words.
What would cost you marks: ending with "yes, it owes its genesis" instead of a stated degree; writing the same content as M3 without adapting to the shorter word limit — at 150 words you must compress, not truncate.
M5Practice · Week 1 test10 marks · 150 words

When is Constitution Day celebrated in India? Elaborate the historical underpinnings of the Indian Constitution and their influence on the evolution of the Constitution.

Directive: two-part — a factual "when", then "elaborate". Elaborate = give a detailed account. The factual half must be answered in one line and precisely; the marks are all in the second half. Do not let the first half consume space.
Skeleton
  1. Date + why that date
  2. Colonial-statute lineage
  3. Nationalist lineage
  4. Influence on the final text

Model answer

Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) is observed on 26 November, marking the Assembly's adoption of the Constitution in 1949; it has been formally celebrated since 2015. Commencement followed separately on 26 January 1950.

Colonial-statute lineage

Regulating Act (1773) → Pitt's India Act (1784) → Charter Acts (1813–1853) → Indian Councils Acts (1861–1909) → Montagu–Chelmsford reforms (1919) introducing provincial dyarchy → Government of India Act, 1935, the direct template for federalism, provincial autonomy and the Federal Court.

Nationalist lineage

Nehru Report (1928) with its rights charter → Karachi Resolution (1931) on fundamental and economic rights → Cabinet Mission Plan (1946), under which the Assembly was constituted → Objectives Resolution (13 December 1946), which became the Preamble almost unchanged.

Their combined influence

The colonial statutes supplied administrative machinery; the nationalist stream supplied purpose — Fundamental Rights, universal adult franchise, and sovereignty vested in the people. The Constitution's character as a borrowed structure with an indigenous soul is the direct product of these two lineages meeting in 1946–49.

Value additions used: 26 Nov 1949 vs 26 Jan 1950 distinction · full statute chronology as an arrow-chain · Nehru Report 1928 · Karachi Resolution 1931 · Cabinet Mission 1946 · Objectives Resolution 13 Dec 1946 → Preamble.
Budget check: 155 / 150 words. Cut: prose connectives between the statutes — both lineages are now arrow-chains, which is faster to read and faster to write in the hall. Bought: the subject-matter of the Karachi Resolution (fundamental and economic rights), and the space to state the two-lineage verdict as its own sentence.
What would cost you marks: confusing adoption (26 Nov 1949) with commencement (26 Jan 1950); listing Acts as a bare chronology without saying what each contributed; forgetting the nationalist stream entirely — most answers cover only the colonial one.

  • 1773 Regulating Act — first GG of Bengal (Warren Hastings).
  • 1833 Charter Act — Company ends trade; GG of India created.
  • 1858 Crown takes over — Secretary of State + Viceroy (Canning).
  • 1909 Morley-Minto — separate electorates (Muslims).
  • 1919 Mont-Ford — dyarchy in provinces.
  • 1935 GoI Act — dyarchy at Centre; provincial autonomy; federation proposed (never enforced); RBI set up; blueprint of ~⅔ of our Constitution.
  • 1947 Indian Independence Act — CA becomes a fully sovereign body.
  • 1934/1935 Idea (M.N. Roy) → formal demand (INC).
  • 1946 Cabinet Mission Plan concedes a Constituent Assembly.
  • 9 Dec 1946 First sitting; League boycotts; Sinha = provisional president.
  • 11 Dec 1946 Rajendra Prasad elected permanent Chairman.
  • 13 Dec 1946 / 22 Jan 1947 Objective Resolution moved / adopted.
  • 29 Aug 1947 Drafting Committee formed — Ambedkar, 7 members.
  • 26 Nov 1949 Constitution adopted (Constitution Day).
  • 26 Jan 1950 Constitution enforced (Republic Day).
  • 389 → 299 members before/after Partition (229 + 70).
