Federal System

Senior-mentor notes on why India is called quasi-federal — the federal features that make it a Union of States, the unitary features that give the Centre an unmistakable upper hand, and the four scholarly labels UPSC keeps testing as a matching exercise.

Union of States Quasi-Federal S.R. Bommai (1994) Wheare · Austin · Jennings Article 1

◆ Why this note exists

Topic 2's official syllabus line bundles three distinct examinable units into one sentence — this is the first of a three-note series (Week 2, Days 4–6 of the study plan) that unpacks it. This note covers the "issues and challenges pertaining to the federal structure" element: what makes India federal, what makes it unitary, and why every serious scholar has needed a hyphenated label to describe the mix.

Notes 02 (Centre-State Relations) and 03 (Inter-State Relations) build directly on the vocabulary fixed here — read this one first.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

Countries generally organise power in one of two shapes. In a unitary country (Britain, France), all real power sits with one central government, which can create, reshape, or abolish local/regional governments at will — the regions have no independent existence of their own. In a genuinely federal country (the USA, classically), power is constitutionally divided between a central government and separately-sovereign states, neither of which can simply overrule or abolish the other — the states pre-existed the union and agreed to join it, so the union cannot dissolve them.

India doesn't fit cleanly into either box, and this note exists entirely because of that mismatch. India clearly looks federal on paper — there is a Union government and separate State governments, each with its own powers, and a written Constitution splitting subjects between them (Section 02 below). But look closer and the Centre keeps a genuinely unitary-style upper hand: it can redraw a State's boundaries by ordinary law, it controls the officers who run State administration, and in an emergency it can simply take over a State's government outright (Section 03). India is a hybrid — federal in form, but with the Centre holding stronger cards than a "pure" federation like the USA would allow.

Because no single word captures that hybrid cleanly, four different scholars each coined their own label for it (Section 04) — and because India itself refuses to even use the word "federation" in its own text, calling itself a "Union of States" instead (Article 1), this entire note is really one long answer to a single question: exactly how federal is India, and exactly where does the Centre still win?

02

Federal features of the Indian Constitution

A federation, classically, is a system where power is constitutionally divided between a central authority and constituent units, each supreme within its own sphere. India's Constitution builds in six of the standard federal markers — though, as Section 03 shows, each one is quietly qualified.

Federal featureHow it shows up in India
Dual polityA Union government and State governments, each with its own executive, legislature and judiciary-facing powers.
Division of powersThe Seventh Schedule splits subjects into Union, State and Concurrent Lists (Note 02 covers this mechanically).
Written ConstitutionThe longest written constitution in the world — the basic document from which both Centre and States derive their powers.
Supremacy of the ConstitutionLaws inconsistent with the Constitution, whether Union or State, are void (Article 13).
RigidityFederal provisions (Article 368's Route 3 — Note 05, Topic 1) need State ratification to amend, protecting the federal balance from unilateral Union change.
Independent judiciary & bicameralismA single integrated judiciary adjudicates Centre-State disputes; the Rajya Sabha exists to represent the States at the Union level.
◆

Article 1 framing: The Constitution never uses the word "federation." Article 1 instead declares India "a Union of States" — deliberately signalling, per the Constituent Assembly debates, that the units did not come together by agreement (unlike the USA) and cannot secede from it.

03

Unitary features of the Indian Constitution

Every federal feature above is matched by a centralising counterweight — this is the half of the note Prelims tests most aggressively, because "which of these is NOT a federal feature" questions are usually really asking "which of these is actually a unitary feature in disguise."

Unitary featureWhat it does
Strong CentreThe Union List has more, and more important, subjects than the State List; residuary powers also rest with the Union (unlike the USA, where they rest with the states).
States not indestructibleParliament can alter a State's boundaries, name or area by simple majority (Articles 2–4, Note 03 of Topic 1) — states have no guaranteed territorial integrity.
Single ConstitutionExcept Jammu & Kashmir's now-repealed special position, no State has its own separate constitution — unlike the USA, where each state has one.
Single citizenshipOne Indian citizenship for the whole country, not separate State citizenships (unlike the USA).
FlexibilityMost of the Constitution can be amended by Parliament alone (Route 1 & 2, Note 05 of Topic 1) — only federal provisions need State ratification.
Integrated judiciaryOne hierarchy of courts enforces both Union and State laws — the US has separate state and federal court systems.
All-India ServicesIAS, IPS and IFoS officers serve the States but are recruited, trained and ultimately controlled by the Union.
Governor as Union agentAppointed by the President, not elected — often read as the Centre's watching brief over State administration.
Emergency provisionsArticles 352, 356 and 360 let the Union, in defined circumstances, override the federal division of power entirely and govern a State directly.
04

Critical evaluation — the scholarly labels

Because the federal and unitary columns above are both genuinely long, no single word describes India's system cleanly — four scholars have each proposed their own label, and UPSC has tested the matching between scholar and label directly.

◆

Value-addition — S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994): the Supreme Court held that federalism is part of the Constitution's Basic Structure (Note 06, Topic 1) and cannot be amended away — the strongest judicial statement that, whatever label scholars prefer, India's federal character is constitutionally permanent, not merely a policy choice Parliament could reverse. The same judgment restricted the misuse of Article 356 (President's Rule) by holding it justiciable.

✚

What President's Rule actually does — the three-part effect of an Article 356 proclamation (a Week 2 question tested this precisely). When constitutional machinery in a State fails, the President can: (1) assume to himself the functions of the State government and the powers of the Governor (in practice the Governor administers as the President's agent); (2) declare that the powers of the State Legislature shall be exercised by Parliament (Parliament may in turn delegate this to the President); and (3) make incidental provisions. The hard limit — the trap option: the President cannot assume the powers of the High Court or suspend any constitutional provision relating to it. The judiciary stays entirely outside President's Rule.

⚠️

Common trap: "Quasi-federal" is Wheare's phrase specifically — do not attribute it to Austin, Jennings or Morris-Jones in a matching question. Several online compilations disagree on which scholar said what; the pairing used here (Wheare–quasi-federal, Austin–cooperative, Jennings–centralising tendency, Morris-Jones–bargaining) is the one that recurs most consistently across independent sources and matches the standard Laxmikanth framing — hold this version as the default.

✚

Recent-judgment layer — federalism's three live battlegrounds. (1) In re: Article 370 (Dec 2023): upheld the special-status abrogation, holding J&K possessed no internal sovereignty distinct from other States — simultaneously a win for the "indestructible Union" reading and fresh evidence for the strong-Centre critique (full detail: Topic 1, Note 03). (2) Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (May 2023): a Constitution Bench held Delhi's elected government controls "services" (bureaucracy) in NCT outside land/police/public order — grounding it in a "federal, representative democracy" principle; Parliament responded within months with the GNCTD (Amendment) Act, 2023 creating an LG-dominated services authority, whose challenge is pending — a textbook legislature-vs-court federalism cycle. (3) State of Kerala v. Union of India (2024): Kerala's original suit (Article 131) claiming Union-imposed net-borrowing ceilings violate its fiscal autonomy was referred to a Constitution Bench — putting fiscal federalism (borrowing limits, cess/surcharge exclusion from the divisible pool) squarely into justiciable territory. Any Mains answer on "federalism under strain" should draw one example from each: territorial (370), administrative (Delhi), fiscal (Kerala).

PYQ analysis

Trend verdict: Prelims tests this note as a "which of these is NOT a federal feature" or "identify the unitary feature" statement-MCQ — confirmed genuine in 2017. The scholar-label matching format (Section 04) is a recurring Prelims pattern across multiple recent cycles, though this note cites only the pairing that could be cross-verified, per the integrity note below.

2017Prelims

"Which one of the following is not a feature of Indian federalism?"

  • (a) independent judiciary
  • (b) powers clearly divided between Centre and States
  • (c) unequal representation of federating units in the Rajya Sabha
  • (d) it is the result of an agreement among the federating units
Verdict: direct hit — Ans. (d)
  • The Indian Union was not formed by agreement among States, unlike the USA — it was created by the Constitution, not by a compact between pre-existing sovereign units.
  • See Section 02’s Article 1 framing above: "Union of States", deliberately chosen by Ambedkar over "Federation".

M16Practice10 marks · 150 words

Is 'quasi-federal' still the most accurate description of Indian federalism, given the rise of cooperative and competitive federalism?

Directive: a direct question demanding a judgement. Treat as "evaluate" — weigh the label against current reality and deliver a verdict. Do not merely list federal and unitary features; the question is about the adequacy of a label.
Skeleton
  1. Intro: Wheare's label + why it stuck
  2. Why it still holds
  3. What has changed since
  4. Verdict on the label

Model answer

K.C. Wheare called India "quasi-federal" — a unitary State with subsidiary federal features — because the Constitution combines a federal division of powers with an unmistakably strong Centre.

Why the label still holds

Article 1 calls India a "Union of States"; States cannot secede and their boundaries may be altered by simple majority under Article 3; the Union dominates legislatively through Articles 249, 250, 252, 253 and the repugnancy rule in 254; Article 356 permits President's Rule; and single citizenship, an integrated judiciary and All-India Services (Article 312) all cut against classical federalism.

What has changed

S.R. Bommai (1994) made federalism part of the basic structure and Article 356 justiciable. Coalition politics after 1989 gave States real bargaining power. The GST Council (Art 279A), the Inter-State Council (Art 263) and NITI Aayog created negotiated decision-making.

Verdict

"Quasi-federal" remains accurate for the text but incomplete for the practice. Austin's "cooperative federalism" and Morris-Jones's "bargaining federalism" capture the working reality better: a strong-Centre federation with negotiated politics.

◆ One-page recall sheet

Last-mile revision — Federal System

  • Article 1: India is a "Union of States" — the word "federation" never appears in the Constitution.
  • Federal features: dual polity, division of powers (7th Schedule), written & supreme Constitution, rigidity for federal provisions, independent judiciary, bicameralism.
  • Unitary features: strong Centre, states not indestructible, single Constitution & citizenship, flexible amendment for most provisions, integrated judiciary, All-India Services, Governor as Union appointee, emergency provisions (Arts 352/356/360).
  • Residuary powers rest with the Union in India — opposite of the USA, where they rest with the states.
  • Wheare → Quasi-federal. Austin → Cooperative federalism. Jennings → Centralising tendency. Morris-Jones → Bargaining federalism.
  • S.R. Bommai (1994): federalism is Basic Structure; Article 356 is justiciable.
  • Genuine Prelims repeater: 2017, "which is NOT a federal feature" — answer, no agreement among federating units.
