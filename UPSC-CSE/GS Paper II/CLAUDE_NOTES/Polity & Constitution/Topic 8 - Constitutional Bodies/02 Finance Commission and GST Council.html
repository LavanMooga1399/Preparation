Finance Commission & GST Council

Articles 280 and 279A — the two bodies that divide India's money. One is temporary and advisory, the other permanent with a weighted vote, and almost every question turns on that contrast.

Art 280 · Art 279A 16th FC — award 2026–31 Devolution 41% GST 2.0 — 5% and 18% Three-fourths, one-third, two-thirds
41%the 16th Finance Commission's devolution share — unchanged from the 15th
◆ Why this is the most current-affairs-exposed note of the week

Both bodies moved in the last twelve months. The 16th Finance Commission submitted its report on 17 November 2025 and it was tabled in Parliament on 1 February 2026, with an award period running 2026–27 to 2030–31 — which begins in the current financial year. And the GST Council abolished two slabs in September 2025, leaving a two-rate structure.

Any textbook printed before 2026 has the wrong Finance Commission and the wrong GST rates. That is an advantage if you carry the new figures and a liability if you do not, so the numbers in Section 02 and Section 03 are worth learning exactly.

Week 6, Day 6 of the study plan, sourced from NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work and Laxmikanth Chapters 46–47.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

India's Constitution gives the Union the most lucrative taxes and the States most of the expensive responsibilities — health, police, agriculture, law and order. That mismatch is deliberate, and it means money must flow downward. The question is: how much, and on what basis?

Answering that is the job of the Finance Commission. It is not a permanent body. The President constitutes one every fifth year, it studies the problem, it recommends how the divisible pool of central taxes should be split between the Union and the States (vertical devolution) and among the States themselves (horizontal devolution), and then it ceases to exist. Its recommendations are advisory — but by convention they are accepted, because rejecting them would be politically explosive.

The GST Council is the opposite kind of body: permanent, created in 2016 when GST subsumed most indirect taxes. Because both the Union and every State surrendered taxing powers to GST, neither can set rates alone. So the Council is a standing negotiating chamber with a carefully engineered voting rule — one where the Union cannot win alone and the States cannot win without it.

Finance Commission — Article 280

PointDetail
Constitution of the bodyThe President constitutes a Finance Commission every fifth year or earlier if he considers necessary — Art 280(1). It is therefore a quasi-judicial, temporary body, not a permanent one.
CompositionA Chairman and four other members, appointed by the President. They are eligible for reappointment.
QualificationsPrescribed by Parliament under Art 280(2), not by the Constitution. Under the Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951: the Chairman must have experience in public affairs; the four members must be — a High Court judge or one qualified to be one · a person with special knowledge of the finances and accounts of Government · a person with wide experience in financial matters and administration · a person with special knowledge of economics.
Functions — Art 280(3)(a) the distribution of the net proceeds of taxes between the Union and the States, and the allocation among States of their share — vertical and horizontal devolution; (b) the principles governing grants-in-aid to the States out of the Consolidated Fund of India (Art 275); (c) measures needed to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of panchayats and municipalities — added by the 73rd and 74th Amendments; (d) any other matter referred by the President in the interests of sound finance.
ReportSubmitted to the President, who causes it to be laid before each House of Parliament together with an explanatory memorandum on the action taken — Art 281.
Binding?Advisory only. The recommendations do not bind the Union Government. By long convention they are accepted, particularly on tax devolution.
!
The Article 280(3)(c) limb is the one candidates forget, and it is the one that changed. The original Article 280 had only three clauses. The 73rd and 74th Amendments added the duty to recommend measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund to supplement the resources of panchayats and municipalities — which is how the Finance Commission acquired a local-government mandate. Note the wording carefully: the Union Finance Commission does not give money directly to local bodies; it recommends measures to augment the State's fund. Direct local-body devolution within a State is the job of the State Finance Commission under Article 243-I, constituted by the Governor every fifth year.
◆
The State Finance Commission — the Week 5 gap, now covered. Art 243-I (panchayats) and Art 243Y (municipalities): the Governor constitutes a State Finance Commission every fifth year to review the financial position of local bodies and recommend the distribution of the State's taxes between the State and its panchayats and municipalities. Its composition and qualifications are prescribed by the State legislature. Its report is laid before the State legislature with an action-taken memorandum. The Week 5 paper asked exactly this and it was not in the notes at the time.

The devolution ladder, because these three numbers are constantly swapped. 13th FC — 32%. 14th FC — 42%. 15th FC — 41%. 16th FC — 41%. The 14th Commission's jump from 32 to 42 was the largest single increase in the history of the institution and is the one most often asked. ◆
Horizontal devolution criteria — what is in and what is out. The 15th Commission used: income distance (45%) · population, 2011 (15%) · area (15%) · forest and ecology (10%) · demographic performance (12.5%) · tax and fiscal effort (2.5%). The 16th keeps the structure but drops tax and fiscal effort and adds contribution to GDP at 10%. Two items that sound plausible but have never been criteria: governance reforms and literacy — the 2023 PYQ was built on exactly that distinction.
03

GST Council — Article 279A

📖 The story — why the voting rule looks the way it does

The 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 created GST, and with it a genuine constitutional problem. Both Parliament and every State legislature gave up taxing powers into a common pool. If the Union could then set rates alone, the States had surrendered sovereignty for nothing. If any State could veto, nothing would ever be decided.

So Article 279A engineers a deadlock-proof but Union-proof rule. A decision needs three-fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting. The Union's vote is weighted at one-third of the total votes cast, and all the States together at two-thirds. Do the arithmetic: the Union alone has 33.3% — not enough to pass anything, but more than the 25% needed to block. And the States together have 66.7% — also short of three-fourths. Neither side can act without the other. That is the design, and it is the single most examinable fact about the Council.

Quorum is one-half of the total members.

PointDetail
CreationArticle 279A, inserted by the 101st Amendment, 2016. Constituted by the President within sixty days of the Article's commencement. A permanent constitutional body.
CompositionChairperson — the Union Finance Minister. Members — the Union Minister of State for Finance or Revenue, and the Minister in charge of Finance or Taxation, or any other Minister, nominated by each State. The States choose one among themselves as Vice-Chairperson.
VotingDecisions by three-fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting. Union = one-third, all States together = two-thirds. Quorum one-half.
FunctionRecommends on: the taxes to be subsumed · goods and services to be subject to or exempt from GST · model GST laws and principles of levy · the threshold turnover for exemption · rates including floor rates with bands · special rates for a specified period · special provisions for the north-eastern and hill States · and the date on which GST is levied on petroleum products.
Binding?Recommendatory only. In Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022) the Supreme Court held that the Council's recommendations are not binding on the Union or the States, that both have simultaneous power to legislate on GST, and that the Council's recommendations have persuasive value — a significant reading for fiscal federalism.
Dispute resolutionArt 279A(11) — the Council shall establish a mechanism to adjudicate disputes between the Union and States, between States, or between the Union and any State on the one side and one or more States on the other. As yet not established, which is a standing criticism.
!
GST 2.0 — the rates changed in September 2025. On 3 September 2025 the Council approved rate rationalisation, effective 22 September 2025. The 12% and 28% slabs were abolished, leaving a two-rate structure — 5% (merit) and 18% (standard) — plus a 40% special rate for luxury and sin goods such as pan masala, tobacco, aerated drinks and luxury cars. Everyday items including hair oil, soap, toothpaste, bicycles and packaged foods moved to 5%; small cars and motorcycles up to 350cc moved from 28% to 18%. A statement describing four slabs of 5, 12, 18 and 28 is now wrong, and it is what most printed material still says.
◆
What GST did not subsume — a reliable question. Outside GST: petroleum crude, high-speed diesel, petrol, natural gas and aviation turbine fuel (constitutionally within GST but taxed at a date the Council decides) · alcohol for human consumption (excluded altogether) · electricity · stamp duty · and basic customs duty. Also remember the two funds: the GST Compensation Cess, which compensated States for revenue loss for the first five years, and the fact that the compensation guarantee ended in June 2022.

Pass 2 — the adjacent surface

◆ Why this section exists

Week 4 lost eleven questions to material one step outside the schedule's wording — and one of the eleven was the State Finance Commission (Art 243-I), which is this chapter's nearest neighbour. Week 5 then lost a question on Article 131's exclusions. Both are the same failure: the note covered the institution and stopped at its boundary.

So this section is the fiscal machinery around Articles 280 and 279A — the funds the money sits in, the grants that are not Finance Commission grants, the bodies that are not constitutional, and the part of central revenue the States never see.

The part of the pool the States never see — and it is the biggest grievance in fiscal federalism

!
Cesses and surcharges are excluded from the divisible pool. Under Art 270, what is shared is the net proceeds of Union taxes — and Art 271 lets Parliament levy a surcharge for Union purposes whose proceeds go wholly to the Union. Cesses are outside the pool on the same principle. So a devolution figure of 41% is 41% of a pool that excludes them, and the States' effective share of gross central revenue is materially lower. This single point is the strongest available argument in any Mains answer on fiscal federalism, and it is the fact a Prelims statement will quietly omit.

The three funds — Articles 266 and 267

FundArticleThe tested fact
Consolidated Fund of India266(1)All revenues, loans raised and repayments. No money may be withdrawn except by appropriation made by law. Finance Commission grants under Art 275 are charged on it.
Public Account of India266(2)Money where the government acts as a banker — provident funds, small savings. No parliamentary appropriation is needed for withdrawals. That exemption is the examinable difference.
Contingency Fund of India267Established by law of Parliament, held by the President, placed at his disposal for unforeseen expenditure pending parliamentary authorisation. Corpus ₹30,000 crore since 2021. A State's Contingency Fund is at the Governor's disposal.

Grants — and the three different articles they come from

TypeArticleDistinguishing feature
Statutory grants-in-aid275On the Finance Commission's recommendation, charged on the Consolidated Fund of India. Includes specific grants for the welfare of Scheduled Tribes and for administration of Scheduled Areas.
Discretionary grants282Made by the Union or a State for any public purpose, even outside its legislative competence. No Finance Commission recommendation required. This is the constitutional basis for centrally sponsored schemes — and the reason the Union can spend on State subjects.
Loans293A State may borrow only within India, and needs the Union's consent if it is still indebted to the Union.
!
Article 282 is the one that gets missed. It permits grants for any public purpose without any Finance Commission involvement, which is precisely how the Union funds schemes in State subjects such as health and education. A statement that “all central grants to States flow on the Finance Commission's recommendation” is false — and note the absolute “all” as the signal.

◆
The CAG is the neighbour most likely to appear beside the Finance Commission, because the two bracket the same money — one recommends how it is shared, the other audits how it is spent. Hold the mirror facts: appointed by the President, removed like a Supreme Court judge (as with the CEC), six years or 65 (as with the UPSC Chairman), salary charged on the Consolidated Fund, and — unlike the Election Commissioners — a complete bar on further office afterwards, exactly like the UPSC Chairman under Art 319. Four constitutional offices, one shared architecture, and the exam builds questions out of the places where they differ.

GST — the articles, the judgment, and the rate story

ItemDetail
Art 246AThe enabling article — concurrent taxing power for the Union and the States on GST. The only place the Constitution grants simultaneous taxing power in this form.
Art 269AIGST — inter-State supply, levied and collected by the Union and apportioned between the Union and the States.
Art 279A(11)The Council shall establish a mechanism to adjudicate disputes — between the Union and States, or between States. Worth remembering that the Council itself has a dispute-settlement duty.
Art 366(12A)Defines GST — a tax on supply of goods or services except alcoholic liquor for human consumption. Petroleum products are constitutionally within GST but taxed at a nil rate until the Council decides otherwise: a crucial difference from alcohol, which is excluded outright.
Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022)The Council's recommendations are persuasive, not binding — both Parliament and State legislatures have equal, simultaneous power to legislate on GST. The Council is a recommendatory body, and a statement calling its decisions binding is false.
Compensation cessGuaranteed States 14% annual revenue growth for five years from 2017. The cess itself was extended beyond 2022 to service the borrowing raised during the pandemic shortfall.
GST 2.0At its 56th meeting (3 September 2025) the Council collapsed the four-slab structure into two — 5% and 18% — with a special 40% de-merit rate for luxury and sin goods (ultra-premium vehicles, tobacco, pan masala, online gaming). Effective 22 September 2025; tobacco continued at old rates until the compensation-cess obligation was discharged.
!
Do not write “GST now has two rates” and stop. There are two standard slabs plus a 40% de-merit rate, and a nil/exempt category besides — plus special rates on bullion. A “how many” statement built on “GST has exactly two rates” is false, and it is precisely the kind of half-remembered headline this setter converts into a statement.

Consider the following statements about grants to States:

  1. Grants under Article 275 are made on the recommendation of the Finance Commission and are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
  2. Grants under Article 282 may be made for any public purpose without a Finance Commission recommendation.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  • (a) 1 only
  • (b) 2 only
  • (c) Both 1 and 2
  • (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (c) — Both 1 and 2
  • Both accurate. Art 282 is the constitutional basis of centrally sponsored schemes and lets the Union spend on State subjects.
  • Another deliberate “both correct” set — the instinct to assume one must be wrong is what this trains out.
Practice · Pairs

Consider the following pairs:

  1. Article 266 — Consolidated Fund and Public Account
  2. Article 267 — Contingency Fund
  3. Article 280 — Finance Commission
  4. Article 279A — Comptroller and Auditor-General

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (c) — only three
  • Pairs 1, 2 and 3 are correct.
  • Pair 4 is false: Art 279A is the GST Council. The CAG is Arts 148–151.
Practice · Assertion–Reason

Consider the following two statements:

  1. Statement-I: The GST Council was designed so that neither the Union nor the States can carry a decision on their own.
  2. Statement-II: Both Parliament and the State legislatures surrendered taxing powers when GST was introduced.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

  • (a) Both correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I
  • (b) Both correct but Statement-II does not explain Statement-I
  • (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is not
  • (d) Statement-I is not correct but Statement-II is
Answer: (a)
  • Both are correct, and the mutual surrender of taxing power is precisely why the voting rule was engineered that way.
  • If either side could set rates alone, the other would have surrendered sovereignty for nothing. The 3/4 threshold is the constitutional expression of that bargain.
Practice · Negative

Which one of the following statements about the Finance Commission is NOT correct?

  • (a) It is constituted by the President every fifth year or earlier
  • (b) Its recommendations are binding on the Union Government
  • (c) It consists of a Chairman and four other members
  • (d) Its members are eligible for reappointment
Answer: (b) is NOT correct
  • The recommendations are advisory. They are accepted by long convention, particularly on tax devolution — convention is not obligation.
  • Contrast the two “advice” regimes you now know: the Election Commission's advice on disqualification binds the President; the Finance Commission's does not. Same word, opposite effect.

(C) Trap statements — True or False

◆ Why this bucket exists, and how to work it

Twenty-one of the fifty questions in the Week 4 paper were “how many of the above” — 42 per cent — scoring 52 per cent. The format cannot be solved by elimination. The skill it rewards is marking one statement true or false, independently.

Twenty statements from this note. Decide before you click. In a chapter this full of numbers, most false statements are a fraction or a figure moved one step.

Trap 1 · True or False

The Finance Commission is a permanent constitutional body.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Constitutional but temporary — constituted every fifth year, and it ceases once it reports. The GST Council is the permanent one.
Trap 2 · True or False

The Finance Commission consists of a Chairman and four other members.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • Four members plus the Chairman, appointed by the President, and all are eligible for reappointment — unlike a PSC member under Art 316(2).
Trap 3 · True or False

The qualifications of Finance Commission members are laid down in the Constitution.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Art 280(2) leaves them to Parliament — supplied by the Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951.
Trap 4 · True or False

The recommendations of the Finance Commission are binding on the Government of India.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Advisory. Accepted by convention, not by compulsion — the report is laid before Parliament with an action-taken memorandum under Art 281.
Trap 5 · True or False

The 14th Finance Commission raised vertical devolution to 42 per cent.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • From the 13th's 32% — the largest single increase in the institution's history, and the most-asked number in the chapter.
Trap 6 · True or False

The 15th Finance Commission reduced devolution to 41 per cent because of a squeeze on State finances.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • The one-point fall reflects the reorganisation of Jammu & Kashmir into Union Territories, moving its share out of the States' pool. Correct number, wrong reason — a favourite construction.
Trap 7 · True or False

Cesses and surcharges form part of the divisible pool.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Excluded. Art 271 sends a surcharge wholly to the Union. The single strongest point in a fiscal-federalism answer.
Trap 8 · True or False

The Union Finance Commission recommends the distribution of a State's taxes between the State and its panchayats.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • That is the State Finance Commission, Art 243-I. The Union Commission recommends measures to augment the State's Consolidated Fund — Art 280(3)(c). A one-level shift.
Trap 9 · True or False

A State Finance Commission is constituted by the Governor every fifth year.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • Arts 243-I and 243Y. Composition and qualifications are prescribed by the State legislature. This was the Week 4 gap.
Trap 10 · True or False

Article 280(3)(c) on local bodies was part of the Constitution as originally enacted.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Added by the 73rd and 74th Amendments. The original Art 280 had three clauses — an “originally enacted” stem is always worth checking against an amendment.
Trap 11 · True or False

The GST Council was created by the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • Art 279A, inserted by the 101st Amendment. Pair it with 246A (concurrent power) and 269A (IGST).
Trap 12 · True or False

A decision of the GST Council requires two-thirds of the weighted votes of members present and voting.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Three-fourths. Two-thirds is the States' combined weight — the question moves one fraction into another's place.
Trap 13 · True or False

The Union Government alone can block a resolution in the GST Council.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • Its 1/3 exceeds the 1/4 needed to defeat a three-fourths threshold. It can veto but not dictate — the asymmetry is the design.
Trap 14 · True or False

The recommendations of the GST Council are binding on the State legislatures.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Mohit Minerals (2022) — persuasive only; both legislatures hold simultaneous power under Art 246A.
Trap 15 · True or False

Alcoholic liquor for human consumption is outside GST.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • Excluded by the definition in Art 366(12A) itself. Petroleum is different — constitutionally inside GST but at a nil rate until the Council decides. Two exclusions of entirely different kinds.
Trap 16 · True or False

Since September 2025 GST has exactly two rates.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Two standard slabs — 5% and 18% — plus a 40% de-merit rate and a nil/exempt category. “Exactly two” is the half-remembered headline this setter converts into a false statement.
Trap 17 · True or False

Money can be withdrawn from the Public Account only by an appropriation made by law.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • That requirement attaches to the Consolidated Fund. The Public Account holds money the government merely banks, and needs no appropriation.
Trap 18 · True or False

The Contingency Fund of India is at the disposal of the President.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • Art 267 — established by law of Parliament, held by the President for unforeseen expenditure. A State's fund is at the Governor's disposal.
Trap 19 · True or False

NITI Aayog is a statutory body.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (b) — False
  • Executive — a Cabinet resolution of 1 January 2015, chaired by the Prime Minister. Neither constitutional nor statutory, like the Planning Commission before it.
Trap 20 · True or False

The Comptroller and Auditor-General is barred from holding any further office under the Government after retirement.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • A complete bar, matching the UPSC Chairman under Art 319 — and in deliberate contrast with the Election Commissioners, for whom no bar exists at all.

M49Practice · Week 615 marks · 250 words

"The Finance Commission and the GST Council are the two pillars of Indian fiscal federalism, but both rest on recommendations that bind no one." Critically examine.

Directive: "Critically examine." Concede the shared weakness, then show why the two bodies survive it for different reasons — and where that reasoning fails.
Skeleton
  1. The two designs
  2. The shared weakness
  3. Why the FC works anyway
  4. Why the Council is more fragile
  5. Reform and verdict

Model answer

The two bodies solve different problems. The Finance Commission under Art 280 is temporary and quasi-judicial — constituted every fifth year to divide the divisible pool vertically and horizontally. The GST Council under Art 279A is permanent and political — a standing chamber where the Union and the States jointly set indirect tax rates neither can set alone.

The shared weakness

Neither binds. Finance Commission recommendations are advisory; the Union need only lay an action-taken memorandum under Art 281. And in Mohit Minerals (2022) the Supreme Court held GST Council recommendations to be persuasive, not binding, since Union and States hold simultaneous legislative power over GST.

Why the Finance Commission survives it

Convention has hardened into near-obligation on tax devolution — no Union government has rejected a devolution recommendation. Its quasi-judicial character, statutory qualifications and public report make departure costly. The 16th Commission (Panagariya), tabled 1 February 2026 for 2026–31, retained devolution at 41% — continuity that itself signals the convention's strength.

Why the Council is more fragile

Its authority is arithmetical, not conventional: three-fourths of weighted votes, Union one-third, States two-thirds. That guarantees neither side can act alone but guarantees nothing about trust — and the compensation guarantee's expiry in June 2022, together with the failure to establish the dispute-resolution mechanism Art 279A(11) requires, has left States without recourse.

The proposition is right about the law and wrong about the consequence. The Finance Commission works because convention substitutes for compulsion; the Council is strained because it has arithmetic but no adjudicator. The reform is not to make recommendations binding — it is to build the dispute-resolution mechanism the Constitution already mandates.

Value additions used: Arts 280, 281, 279A and 279A(11) · Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022) · 16th FC, Panagariya, tabled 1 Feb 2026, 41% retained · compensation cess ending June 2022 · the three-fourths / one-third / two-thirds arithmetic.
Budget check: 284 / 250 words. Cut: the horizontal devolution criteria and the GST rate structure — both Prelims material, neither bearing on bindingness. Bought: the contrast between convention and arithmetic as sources of authority, which is what makes this a critical examination rather than two descriptions, and the Art 279A(11) failure, which supplies a reform the Constitution itself already demands.
What would cost you marks: treating the two bodies as parallel — they are not, and the answer's whole value lies in distinguishing why one survives non-bindingness and the other does not; and proposing that recommendations be made binding without addressing that this would strip elected legislatures of taxing power.

Last-mile revision — Finance Commission & GST Council

  • Art 280 — President constitutes a Finance Commission every 5th year or earlier. Chairman + 4 members. Reappointment allowed. Quasi-judicial, temporary.
  • Qualifications prescribed by Parliament (1951 Act): Chairman — public affairs; members — HC judge or qualified to be · finance and accounts of Government · financial matters and administration · economics.
  • Art 280(3): (a) distribution of net proceeds — vertical and horizontal · (b) principles of grants-in-aid (Art 275) · (c) measures to augment a State's fund for panchayats and municipalities — added by the 73rd/74th Amendments · (d) any matter referred by the President.
  • Art 281 — report to the President, laid before each House with an action-taken memorandum. Advisory only; accepted by convention.
  • Devolution ladder: 13th 32% · 14th 42% · 15th 41% · 16th 41%. The 15th's cut reflected J&K reorganisation, not a squeeze.
  • 15th FC — N.K. Singh, 2021–26, 2011 census, criteria: income distance 45 · population 15 · area 15 · forest & ecology 10 · demographic performance 12.5 · tax and fiscal effort 2.5.
  • 16th FC — Arvind Panagariya, submitted 17 Nov 2025, tabled 1 Feb 2026, award 2026–27 to 2030–31, devolution 41%, grants ₹9.47 lakh crore, new 10% weight for GDP contribution, tax and fiscal effort removed, deficit cap 3% of GSDP, end to off-budget borrowing.
  • Never criteria: governance reforms · stable government · literacy.
  • State Finance Commission — Arts 243-I and 243Y, constituted by the Governor every 5th year; composition and qualifications by the State legislature; report to the Governor, laid before the State legislature.
  • Art 279A · GST Council — 101st Amendment, 2016, permanent. Chair: Union Finance Minister. States elect a Vice-Chairperson. Quorum one-half. Three-fourths of weighted votes; Union one-third, States two-thirds → Union can block (needs ¼) but cannot pass alone.
  • Mohit Minerals (2022) — recommendations not binding; Union and States have simultaneous power. Art 279A(11) dispute mechanism still not established.
  • GST 2.0 — from 22 Sept 2025: slabs 5% and 18%, plus 40% for luxury and sin goods. 12% and 28% abolished. Compensation cess guarantee ended June 2022.
  • Outside GST: alcohol for human consumption · electricity · stamp duty · basic customs. Deferred: crude, diesel, petrol, natural gas, ATF.
