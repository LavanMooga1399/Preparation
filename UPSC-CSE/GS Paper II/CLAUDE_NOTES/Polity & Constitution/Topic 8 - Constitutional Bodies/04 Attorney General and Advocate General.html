Attorney General & Advocate General

Articles 76 and 165 — the government's lawyers, not the government's officers. Almost every question here turns on a single idea: they may speak in the legislature but never vote, and they hold office at pleasure with no security at all.

Art 76 · AG Art 165 · AG of a State Speak, never vote Pleasure doctrine Not a whole-time counsel
0security of tenure · and 0 votes in either House
◆ Where the marks are, and where they are not

This is a short chapter with a high question yield, because the office sits at the junction of three things the examiner loves: the legislature (rights of audience and participation), the executive (appointment and pleasure), and the judiciary (qualification and right of audience in any court).

The dominant trap is the participation rule. The Attorney General has the right to speak in and take part in the proceedings of both Houses, their joint sitting and any committee of which he is named a member — but no right to vote. UPSC has asked this twice in the years covered by your PYQ compilation, in 2013 and 2022, and both are reproduced below.

Week 6, Day 3 of the study plan, alongside the Election Commission note. Sourced from Laxmikanth Chapters 53–54 and the bare Constitution.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

The Government of India goes to court constantly, and it needs a lawyer. That lawyer is the Attorney General for India under Article 76 — the country's first law officer. Every State needs the same thing, so Article 165 creates an Advocate General for each State.

Two features make the office strange, and both are examinable. First, he is not a whole-time government servant. He may take private clients, though not against the Government. Second, he has no security of tenure whatsoever — he holds office during the pleasure of the President, and the Constitution fixes no term and prescribes no removal procedure. Compare that with everything else in Topic 8: the CEC, the UPSC Chairman, the CAG all have elaborate removal protections. The law officer has none, deliberately, because he must share the Government's confidence.

The third feature is the one that produces questions: he may sit and speak in Parliament without being a member — and may never vote. He has the rights of a member, minus the one that counts.

The office — Article 76

PointDetail
AppointmentBy the President. The Attorney General is the first law officer of the Government of India.
QualificationA person qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court — that is, a citizen of India who has been a judge of a High Court for five years, or an advocate of a High Court for ten years, or a distinguished jurist in the President's opinion.
TermThe Constitution prescribes none.
RemovalThe Constitution prescribes no procedure and no ground. He holds office during the pleasure of the President.
RemunerationSuch as the President may determine. It is not fixed by the Constitution and is not charged on the Consolidated Fund.
DutiesTo give advice to the Government of India on legal matters referred by the President; to perform other duties of a legal character assigned by the President; and to discharge functions conferred by the Constitution or any other law.
Rights of audienceA right of audience in all courts in the territory of India.
Rights in ParliamentUnder Art 88 — the right to speak in and take part in the proceedings of both Houses, any joint sitting, and any committee of which he is named a member. No right to vote.
PrivilegesEnjoys all the privileges and immunities of a member of Parliament.
!
Three statements that look right and are not. (1) “The Attorney General is a whole-time counsel for the Government” — false. He is not debarred from private practice; the only bar is that he may not advise or hold a brief against the Government of India, and may not defend an accused in a criminal prosecution without the Government's permission. (2) “He must resign when the Government that appointed him resigns” — false as a matter of constitutional law. The Constitution says nothing of the kind; it is a convention, and UPSC tested exactly this distinction in 2022. (3) “He is a member of the Union Council of Ministers” — false. He is not a minister and not a member of the Cabinet.

!
The mirror is exact, so the swaps are cheap. The distinguishing pairs are: President / Governor · Supreme Court / High Court qualification · all courts in India / all courts in the State · Art 88 / Art 177. A statement that the Advocate General is appointed by the Chief Minister, or must be qualified to be a Supreme Court judge, is false — and both are standard distractors.
◆
The law officers below the Attorney General are not constitutional at all. The Solicitor General of India and the Additional Solicitors General assist the AG, but they are not mentioned in the Constitution — they are appointed by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet, and Article 76 names only the Attorney General. This is the exact point on which the 2022 question below was built: a statement bracketing the AG and the SG together as the officers allowed to participate in Parliament is false, because Art 88 covers the Attorney General alone.

!
Article 88 has a second name in it, and it is the one candidates forget. The marginal heading is “Rights of Ministers and Attorney-General as respects the Houses”. It confers the same speak-but-not-vote right on a Minister who is not a member of that particular House — which is how a Rajya Sabha minister speaks in the Lok Sabha. So a question asking who may take part in the proceedings of a House without being its member has two correct classes of answer, not one.

!
The six-month rule sits one step away and is asked far more often than the AG himself. A non-member may be a Minister for six months under Arts 75(5) and 164(4), and must be qualified to be a member of the legislature — which is more than being eligible to vote. Prelims 2020 built a whole question on that difference. The Attorney General, by contrast, is under no such time limit, because he never becomes a member at all.

Where the Attorney General actually appears

SettingDetail
Article 143 referencesThe President may refer a question of law or fact of public importance to the Supreme Court; the AG appears for the Union. The opinion is advisory and not binding, and the Court may decline to answer — except a reference under the proviso concerning pre-Constitution treaties, which it must answer. The 2025 Presidential reference on the Governor's assent timelines is the live example, answered by a Constitution Bench in November 2025.
Contempt of courtA criminal contempt petition by a private citizen requires the written consent of the Attorney General — a genuine gatekeeping power, and one of the very few places his consent is a legal precondition.
Constitution Bench mattersHe appears for the Union in every major constitutional challenge, and is entitled to notice where a substantial question of constitutional interpretation arises.

Prelims Questions

(A) Genuine previous-year questions

2022UPSC Prelims · Statements

Consider the following statements:

  1. Attorney General of India and Solicitor General of India are the only officers of the Government who are allowed to participate in the meetings of the Parliament of India.
  2. According to the Constitution of India, the Attorney General of India submits his resignation when the Government which appointed him resigns.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  • (a) 1 only
  • (b) 2 only
  • (c) Both 1 and 2
  • (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (d) — Neither 1 nor 2
  • Statement 1 fails on two counts. Art 88 covers the Attorney General alone — the Solicitor General has no such right and is not even mentioned in the Constitution. And the AG is not the only such officer: ministers who are not members of a particular House take part under the very same article.
  • Statement 2 fails on the words “According to the Constitution”. Resignation on a change of government is a convention; the Constitution prescribes no term, no ground and no procedure — he simply holds office during pleasure.
  • The transferable habit: when a stem attributes a practice to the Constitution, ask whether the text actually says it. Two of your Week 5 losses were the same failure.
2013UPSC Prelims · Direct

The Attorney General of India can

  • (a) take part in the proceedings of the Lok Sabha
  • (b) be a member of a committee of the Lok Sabha
  • (c) speak in the Lok Sabha
  • (d) vote in the Lok Sabha
Answer: (c) — speak in the Lok Sabha
  • The examiner is separating speaking from voting: option (d) is the trap and is squarely wrong.
  • Note carefully why (b) is not the keyed answer — Art 88 lets him take part in a committee of which he is named a member, which is a qualified right rather than a free-standing entitlement to committee membership. The unambiguous, unqualified power in this list is the right to speak.
  • Read together with the 2022 question, the pattern is plain: this office is examined almost entirely through Article 88.

(B) Practice questions

Practice · How many

Consider the following statements about the Attorney General for India:

  1. He must be qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court.
  2. He holds office during the pleasure of the President.
  3. His remuneration is charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
  4. The Constitution prescribes a term of five years for his office.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (b) — only two
  • Statements 1 and 2 are correct.
  • Statement 3 is false: remuneration is determined by the President and is not charged. Compare the CAG, the CEC and the Public Service Commissions, whose salaries all are charged — the law officer is the odd one out.
  • Statement 4 is false: no term is prescribed at all.
Practice · Negative

Which one of the following statements about the Attorney General is NOT correct?

  • (a) He has the right of audience in all courts in the territory of India
  • (b) He enjoys the privileges and immunities of a member of Parliament
  • (c) He is debarred from private legal practice during his term
  • (d) He is appointed by the President
Answer: (c) is NOT correct
  • He is not a whole-time counsel and private practice is permitted. The bar is narrower: he may not advise or hold a brief against the Government of India, and may not defend an accused in a criminal prosecution without permission.
  • Contrast the UPSC Chairman under Art 319, whose post-office bar is total. Different offices, opposite treatment — and that contrast is itself examinable.
Practice · Pairs

Consider the following pairs:

  1. Article 76 — Attorney General for India
  2. Article 88 — rights of the Attorney General as respects the Houses
  3. Article 165 — Advocate General for the State
  4. Article 178 — rights of the Advocate General in the State legislature

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (c) — only three
  • Pairs 1, 2 and 3 are correct.
  • Pair 4 is off by one: the Advocate General's rights are in Article 177. Article 178 is the Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Assembly.
  • Learn 88 and 177 as a couplet — Union and State versions of the same right.
Practice · Assertion–Reason

Consider the following two statements:

  1. Statement-I: The Constitution lays down no procedure for the removal of the Attorney General.
  2. Statement-II: The Attorney General must enjoy the confidence of the Government he advises.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

  • (a) Both correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I
  • (b) Both correct but Statement-II does not explain Statement-I
  • (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is not
  • (d) Statement-I is not correct but Statement-II is
Answer: (a)
  • Both correct, and the second is the design reason for the first. Security of tenure exists to let an office defy the executive; a lawyer who must share his client's confidence needs the opposite arrangement.
  • That single sentence is the cleanest way to justify the pleasure doctrine in a Mains answer — and the cleanest way to distinguish this office from the CEC, the CAG and the PSCs in Prelims.
Practice · How many

Consider the following statements about the Advocate General of a State:

  1. He is appointed by the Chief Minister of the State.
  2. He must be qualified to be appointed a Judge of a High Court.
  3. He has the right to speak in the proceedings of the State legislature.
  4. He has the right of audience in all courts in the territory of India.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (b) — only two
  • Statements 2 and 3 are correct.
  • Statement 1 puts the Chief Minister where the Governor belongs — the standard authority swap.
  • Statement 4 over-extends the territory: his audience right runs within the State. The all-India right belongs to the Attorney General. Same sentence, one word moved.
Practice · Negative

Which one of the following is NOT a constitutional office?

  • (a) Attorney General for India
  • (b) Solicitor General of India
  • (c) Advocate General of a State
  • (d) Comptroller and Auditor-General of India
Answer: (b) — Solicitor General of India
  • The Constitution names the Attorney General (Art 76) and the Advocate General (Art 165) — and nobody else among the law officers.
  • The Solicitor General and the Additional Solicitors General are appointed by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet. This is the precise error the 2022 PYQ was built to catch.

Mains Questions

M51Practice · Week 615 marks · 250 words

The Attorney General is the only constitutional functionary who holds office wholly at the pleasure of the executive. Is that a defect in constitutional design, or its point? Critically examine.

Directive: "Critically examine." The question offers two readings and expects you to choose. Take the second — the absence of tenure is deliberate — then concede where it genuinely bites.
Skeleton
  1. The office and its bare terms
  2. Why no security was given
  3. Where the design fails
  4. Comparison and reform
  5. Verdict

Model answer

Article 76 creates the Attorney General as the first law officer, requires only that he be fit to be a Supreme Court judge, and then stops: no term, no grounds of removal, no procedure, and remuneration as the President may determine. He holds office during pleasure.

Why the silence is deliberate

Security of tenure exists so that an office may defy the executive — which is why the CEC is removable only like a Supreme Court judge and the CAG carries a complete post-retirement bar. The Attorney General's function is the opposite: he is counsel, and the relationship of counsel and client rests on confidence. A lawyer his client cannot dismiss is a lawyer his client cannot trust. On that reading the silence in Article 76 is not an omission but a design choice.

Where it nonetheless bites

Two ways. He exercises genuinely public functions that are not client work — most clearly the statutory consent required before a private criminal contempt petition, where he acts as a gatekeeper for the court rather than an advocate for the Union. And he alone among law officers may speak in Parliament under Art 88, a role in which the House is entitled to a view that is not merely the Government's brief.

Reform

Not tenure, which would defeat the purpose — but a reasoned public record for contempt consents, and a convention that the office is not filled by someone who has recently held political office.

The design is sound where he is counsel and thin where he is a public gatekeeper. The fix belongs to those functions, not to the tenure.

Value additions used: Art 76 with all four silences · the counsel-confidence rationale · contrast with the CEC's and CAG's protections · Art 88 · the contempt-consent gatekeeping power.

Last-mile revision — Attorney General & Advocate General

  • Art 76 — Attorney General, first law officer, appointed by the President, qualified to be a Supreme Court judge (10 years as an advocate).
  • No term · no removal ground · no procedure. Holds office during pleasure. Remuneration as the President determines — not charged on the Consolidated Fund.
  • Art 88 — right to speak and take part in both Houses, joint sittings, and committees of which he is named a member. NO VOTE. Has the privileges of an MP. Art 88 also covers ministers not belonging to that House.
  • Not a whole-time counsel — private practice allowed, but never against the Government, and no defending an accused without permission. Not a minister, not a civil servant.
  • Art 165 — Advocate General, appointed by the Governor, qualified to be a High Court judge, pleasure of the Governor, audience within the State, rights under Art 177.
  • Solicitor General & Additional SGs — NOT in the Constitution. Appointed by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet. No Art 88 right. Prelims 2022 was built on this.
  • PYQ pattern: 2013 — can he vote? No, he may speak. 2022 — is the AG-and-SG bracket right? No. Is resignation constitutional? No, convention.
  • AG's consent in writing is required for a private criminal contempt petition.
  • Art 143 — advisory, not binding, Court may decline — except the pre-Constitution treaty proviso. Contrast Art 317(1), where the Court's advice binds.
  • Six-month rule — Arts 75(5) / 164(4); a non-member minister must be qualified to be a member, which is more than being eligible to vote.
