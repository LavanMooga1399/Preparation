NCSC, NCST, NCBC & the Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities

Four safeguard offices built on one template — Articles 338, 338A, 338B and 350B. Three are near-identical by design, the fourth is deliberately not, and every question in this chapter lives in that difference.

Art 338 · SC Art 338A · ST Art 338B · OBC Art 350B · Linguistic 65th · 89th · 102nd · 105th
4offices · but only three are commissions
◆ Where the marks are, and where they are not

Do not memorise these three commissions separately. They share a single blueprint: Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three other members, all appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal, reporting to the President, with the powers of a civil court. Learn the template once, then learn the four places it varies.

The genuinely high-value fact is the amendment lineage. Each commission got its constitutional status in a different year by a different amendment, and this setter builds “how many are constitutional bodies” questions out of exactly that. Prelims 2023 asked it directly and the answer was only one of four.

Week 6, Day 5 of the study plan, sourced from Laxmikanth Chapters 48–51 and the bare Constitution.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

The Constitution promises equality, but a promise with nobody watching it is just a sentence. So it creates standing watchdogs for the groups most likely to be left out — Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and socially and educationally backward classes — plus a single officer for linguistic minorities.

These bodies do not make policy and they do not punish. They investigate, monitor, inquire and advise, and they report. Their real power is two-fold: they hold the powers of a civil court when inquiring, and the government must be consulted on major policy matters affecting the group. The report goes to the President, who lays it before Parliament with a memorandum explaining any recommendation not accepted — the same accountability-by-publicity model you met with the Public Service Commissions.

The one that breaks the pattern is Article 350B. It is not a commission at all — it is a single Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities. That difference alone has carried questions.

01

The shared template — and where it breaks

Learn this once. It is true of all three commissions.

FeatureThe common template — NCSC, NCST, NCBC
CompositionA Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson, and three other members — five in all.
AppointmentBy the President, by warrant under his hand and seal.
Conditions of service and tenureDetermined by the President by rule. The Constitution fixes no term — in practice three years by rule.
Powers while inquiringAll the powers of a civil court trying a suit — summoning witnesses, requiring documents, receiving evidence on affidavit.
Consultation dutyThe Union and every State Government shall consult the Commission on all major policy matters affecting the group concerned.
ReportingReports to the President — annually and at such other times as it thinks fit. The President lays every report before Parliament with a memorandum on action taken and reasons for non-acceptance. Where a report concerns a State, a copy goes to the Governor, who lays it before the State legislature.
!
Two things the template does not include, and both are tested. (1) There is no constitutional qualification prescribed for any member of any of the three. (2) There is no constitutionally fixed tenure — it is left to presidential rule, exactly as with the Election Commissioners' six-year term. A statement asserting that the Constitution lays down a term or a qualification is false for all three.

The four places the template breaks

DifferenceDetail
1 · The article and the amendment338 NCSC · 338A NCST · 338B NCBC — each arriving in a different year by a different amendment. See the lineage below.
2 · Anglo-IndiansThe NCSC also discharges functions in relation to the Anglo-Indian community. The NCST does not, and the NCBC does not. This is the single cleanest discriminator between NCSC and NCST.
3 · Who else it advisesThe NCST carries additional duties in respect of the administration of Scheduled Areas and Tribal Areas — the Fifth and Sixth Schedule surface, which the other two never touch.
4 · Article 350B is not a commissionA single Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities — no Vice-Chairperson, no members, no civil-court powers conferred by the article.
02

The amendment lineage — this is the exam's favourite ground

📖 The story — one commission becomes three

The Constitution as enacted had only a Special Officer for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes under the original Article 338 — one officer, investigating and reporting. Over four decades that was judged too thin.

65th Amendment, 1990. The Special Officer was replaced by a multi-member National Commission for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes — one commission for both groups.

89th Amendment, 2003. That joint commission was split in two: the NCSC stayed in Article 338, and a new Article 338A created the NCST, which began functioning in 2004. The reasoning was that the two groups' problems had little in common — one is defined by caste disability, the other by geography, forest rights and the Fifth and Sixth Schedules.

102nd Amendment, 2018. The NCBC, a statutory body since 1993 (created after Indra Sawhney), was given constitutional status through a new Article 338B. The same amendment inserted Article 342A, which made the President, in consultation with the Governor, the authority to specify socially and educationally backward classes — read by the Supreme Court in the Maratha reservation case (2021) as stripping States of the power to identify their own SEBCs.

105th Amendment, 2021. Parliament reversed that reading and restored to the States the power to identify and specify their own socially and educationally backward classes, by clarifying Art 342A and adding Art 366(26C). A judgment, then a corrective amendment — the same shape as the Governor-assent and CEC-appointment sagas.

AmendmentYearWhat it did
65th1990Replaced the Special Officer for SC/ST with a multi-member joint Commission.
89th2003Split the joint commission — NCSC under Art 338, NCST under the new Art 338A. NCST began work in 2004.
102nd2018Gave the NCBC constitutional status under new Art 338B; inserted Art 342A.
105th2021Restored to the States the power to identify their own SEBCs, undoing the Supreme Court's reading of the 102nd.
03

Article 350B — the one that is not a commission

PointDetail
What it createsA Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities — one officer, not a commission. Appointed by the President.
Inserted byThe 7th Amendment, 1956, on the recommendation of the States Reorganisation Commission — which is why it sits with the language articles rather than with the safeguard commissions.
DutyTo investigate all matters relating to the safeguards provided for linguistic minorities under the Constitution, and to report to the President on such intervals as the President may direct.
ReportingThe President lays all such reports before each House of Parliament and sends them to the Governments of the States concerned.
In practiceDesignated the Commissioner for Linguistic Minorities, under the Ministry of Minority Affairs, headquartered at Allahabad.
!
The three swaps built on Art 350B. (1) It is a Special Officer, not a Commission — a statement calling it the “National Commission for Linguistic Minorities” is false. (2) The National Commission for Minorities is a different body entirely and is statutory, under the NCM Act, 1992 — not constitutional. (3) Art 350A is the neighbouring duty on every State to provide primary education in the mother tongue to children of linguistic minority groups; 350B is the officer who watches it. Adjacent numbers, adjacent subjects.
◆
Hold the language cluster together, since 350B drags it in. Art 343 — Hindi in Devanagari as the official language of the Union. Art 344 — an Official Language Commission and a Parliamentary Committee on official language. Art 345–347 — official languages of a State. Art 348 — the language of the Supreme Court and High Courts remains English until Parliament provides otherwise. Art 351 — the directive to promote the spread of Hindi. The Eighth Schedule now lists 22 languages; Sindhi was added by the 21st Amendment (1967), Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali by the 71st (1992), and Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santhali by the 92nd (2003).

!
Articles 341 and 342 carry an exception worth more than the rule. The President specifies which castes or tribes are Scheduled, by public notification, in consultation with the Governor where a State is concerned. But once that first notification is issued, only Parliament by law may include or exclude any group — the President cannot vary his own list a second time. A statement saying the President may amend the list by a later notification is false, and it is exactly the kind of “who may, and only once” distinction this setter builds on.

The three live issues the Week 6 schedule names for this day

The plan's Current Affairs column attaches one issue to each Commission. They are not decoration — this is the setter telling you where the dynamic questions will come from.

NCSC → the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989

📖 The story — a statute, a judgment that weakened it, and a Parliament that struck back

The Commission's duty under Art 338 is to investigate and monitor safeguards for Scheduled Castes. The most important safeguard it monitors is the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989, which creates a distinct class of offences committed against SC and ST persons, provides for Special Courts and Exclusive Special Public Prosecutors, and was widened by a 2015 amendment that added new offences and a duty of relief and rehabilitation.

March 2018 — Subhash Kashinath Mahajan. The Supreme Court, concerned about misuse, layered safeguards on to the Act: a preliminary enquiry before registering an FIR, and prior sanction before arresting a public servant. In effect it read protections for the accused into a statute built to protect complainants.

August 2018 — Parliament reversed it. The SC/ST (PoA) Amendment Act, 2018 inserted section 18A, expressly removing the requirement of a preliminary enquiry and of prior approval for arrest, and barring anticipatory bail.

February 2020 — Prathvi Raj Chauhan. A three-judge Bench upheld the 2018 amendment, while noting that anticipatory bail may still be available where the complaint is prima facie untenable.

!
Notice the shape, because you have now met it four times in Topic 8. A judgment reads something into the law; Parliament amends to reverse it; the amendment is then tested and upheld. Mahajan → 2018 Amendment → Prathvi Raj Chauhan is the same arc as 102nd Amendment → Maratha reservation reading → 105th Amendment, as Anoop Baranwal → CEC Act 2023, and as the Governor's assent saga. When a stem offers a judgment on a socially contested statute, always ask whether Parliament has since answered it.

NCBC → sub-categorisation within the OBCs, and the Rohini Commission

PointDetail
The problemThe 27% OBC quota is a single undivided block. The complaint is that a handful of already-advanced communities within the list capture most of it, while the most backward OBCs get little.
The CommissionConstituted October 2017 under Article 340, chaired by Justice G. Rohini, to examine sub-categorisation of OBCs and the equitable distribution of benefits.
The findingRoughly 40 of some 5,000–6,000 OBC sub-groups — under 1% of the OBC population — had taken about 50% of central reservation benefits in jobs and education.
The reportSubmitted to President Droupadi Murmu on 31 July 2023, after thirteen extensions. It has not been made public, and no legislation or executive order implementing sub-categories has followed.
The StatesAround eleven States and UTs already sub-categorise OBCs in their own lists — the Union list is the one still undivided.
The link to makePair it with Davinder Singh (2024), which permitted sub-classification within Scheduled Castes. The Court has now blessed the principle for SCs while the OBC report sits unpublished — a contrast worth one sentence in any Mains answer on reservation.
!
Article 340 is the sleeper here. The Rohini Commission was appointed under Art 340 — the President's power to appoint a Commission to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes. That is the same article under which the Kaka Kalelkar Commission (1953) and the Mandal Commission (1979) were appointed. So the family is: Art 340 for an investigating commission · Art 338B for the permanent NCBC · Art 342A for specifying the list. Three articles, three different jobs, routinely swapped.

NCBC → the caste census

◆
The NCBC's second live issue is data: sub-categorisation and any revision of quotas require numbers that have not existed since 1931, the last Census to enumerate caste for the whole population. Post-Independence Censuses counted only SCs and STs. The Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs decided on 30 April 2025 to include caste enumeration; the Union Cabinet approved the Census scheme on 4 June 2025 with an outlay of about ₹11,718 crore, and the gazette notification followed on 16 June 2025. Census 2027 will be India's first digital Census and will run in two phases — house-listing April to September 2026, population enumeration February 2027. The caste question is open-ended: a respondent states the caste in their own words rather than picking from a list, which is itself the methodological objection most often raised.
!
Keep three dates apart. 1931 — last full caste enumeration. 2011 — the Socio-Economic and Caste Census (SECC), which did collect caste data but was not part of the Census proper and whose caste figures were never released. 2027 — the first caste enumeration within the Census since Independence. A statement that India has not collected caste data since 1931 is false because of the SECC; a statement that the Census has enumerated caste since Independence is also false. The distinction is between the Census and a survey.

(A) Genuine previous-year questions

◆ An honest note on this bucket

UPSC has asked very few direct questions on the NCSC and NCST themselves. A search of the 2009–2025 PYQ compilation turns up one question that touches this note squarely — the 2023 constitutional-status question below — and none at all on Article 350B.

That is worth knowing rather than papering over. It does not mean the topic is low-yield: it means the examiner attacks it through status and amendment questions rather than by asking about the commissions directly. Build for that angle, and treat the practice bucket below as the real drill.

2023UPSC Prelims · How many

Consider the following organizations/bodies in India:

  1. The National Commission for Backward Classes
  2. The National Human Rights Commission
  3. The National Law Commission
  4. The National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission

How many of the above are constitutional bodies?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (a) — Only one
  • Only the NCBC is constitutional, under Art 338B, and only since the 102nd Amendment, 2018.
  • The NHRC is statutory (1993), the Law Commission is a body constituted by executive resolution, and the NCDRC is statutory under the Consumer Protection Act.
  • The transferable lesson: a body's status is a dated fact. The same question set before 2018 would have answered none.
2015UPSC Prelims · Direct

The Government of India has established NITI Aayog to replace the

  • (a) Human Rights Commission
  • (b) Finance Commission
  • (c) Law Commission
  • (d) Planning Commission
Answer: (d) — Planning Commission
  • Included here because it drills the same reflex this note trains: which body is which, and what status does it hold.
  • Both NITI Aayog and the Planning Commission are executive bodies created by resolution — neither is constitutional, unlike the Finance Commission in option (b).

(C) Trap statements — True or False

◆ Why this bucket exists, and how to work it

Twenty-one of the fifty questions in the Week 4 paper were “how many of the above” — 42 per cent — scoring 52 per cent. The format cannot be solved by elimination; each statement stands alone.

Twenty statements from this note. Decide before you click. In a chapter built on three near-identical bodies, most false statements are a feature moved from one commission to another.

Trap 1 · True or False

The National Commission for Scheduled Castes consists of a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and three other members.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True

  • (b) False

  • (b) False

The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes discharges functions in relation to the Anglo-Indian community.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False

  • (a) True
  • (b) False
Answer: (a) — True
  • Summoning witnesses, requiring documents, receiving affidavit evidence — the standard investigative-body package.

The Constitution prescribes qualifications for members of the NCSC.

  • (a) True
  • (b) False

  • (b) False

M50Practice · Week 615 marks · 250 words

The constitutional commissions for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Backward Classes are built on an identical template but have delivered unequal results. Examine.

Directive: "Examine." Take the claim apart. The template is genuinely identical — so the explanation for unequal results must lie elsewhere, and that is the answer.
Skeleton
  1. The shared template
  2. The structural weakness common to all
  3. Why outcomes still differ
  4. Reform
  5. Verdict

Model answer

Articles 338, 338A and 338B create three commissions on one blueprint: a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three members appointed by the President; the powers of a civil court while inquiring; a duty on the Union and the States to consult on major policy; and reports laid before Parliament with an action-taken memorandum.

The common weakness

The template's authority is recommendatory. None of the three can compel, and none has a fixed constitutional tenure or prescribed qualifications — both are left to presidential rule, leaving composition open to patronage. Their real leverage is publicity, which works only where the group already has political voice.

Why outcomes diverge

Three reasons. Age: the NCSC has functioned since 1990 and the NCST since 2004, while the NCBC has held constitutional status only since 2018. Subject matter: the NCST alone confronts the Fifth and Sixth Schedule machinery, PESA and forest rights — questions of land and self-government that no advisory body can settle. Legal churn: the NCBC's remit was destabilised within three years, as the 102nd Amendment was read to strip States of their SEBC power and the 105th restored it.

Reform

Fix tenure and qualifications in the Constitution as with the Election Commission; require a time-bound government response to each recommendation; and give the NCST a statutory role in Fifth Schedule administration rather than an advisory one.

An identical template does not produce identical institutions. Where the subject matter is contested and the mandate recent, an advisory body will underperform — the design guarantees a voice, not an outcome.

Value additions used: Arts 338/338A/338B with the shared template · the 65th, 89th, 102nd and 105th Amendments · civil-court powers and the consultation duty · Fifth and Sixth Schedules, PESA, FRA · the Maratha reservation case.

Last-mile revision — NCSC, NCST, NCBC & Art 350B

  • Template for all three: Chairperson + Vice-Chairperson + 3 members = 5. Appointed by the President by warrant under hand and seal. Civil court powers. Report to the President → Parliament with action-taken memorandum; State reports → Governor → State legislature.
  • No constitutional qualification. No constitutional tenure. Both by presidential rule.
  • Art 338 NCSC — also Anglo-Indians. Art 338A NCST — also Scheduled & Tribal Areas. Art 338B NCBC.
  • Lineage: 65th (1990) Special Officer → joint Commission · 89th (2003) split into NCSC + NCST, NCST works from 2004 · 102nd (2018) NCBC constitutional + Art 342A · 105th (2021) States' SEBC power restored, Art 366(26C).
  • Art 350B — Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, 7th Amendment 1956, on the States Reorganisation Commission's recommendation. Not a commission. Art 350A — mother-tongue primary education.
  • Arts 341/342 — President specifies the first list in consultation with the Governor; thereafter only Parliament may include or exclude.
  • NOT constitutional: National Commission for Minorities (1992) · NHRC (1993) · NCW (1990) · NCPCR (2005) · NCDRC · Law Commission (executive). Prelims 2023 — only one of four was constitutional.
  • Reservation ladder: Indra Sawhney 1992 — 27%, 50% ceiling, creamy layer, no promotion quota · 77th/81st/82nd/85th restored promotion, backlog, relaxation, consequential seniority · M. Nagaraj 2006 · Jarnail Singh 2018 — creamy layer in promotions · Janhit Abhiyan 2022 — EWS outside the ceiling · Davinder Singh 2024 — sub-classification within SCs allowed.
  • Fifth Schedule — Tribes Advisory Council (up to 20, ¾ ST MLAs), Governor's report and modification power. Sixth Schedule — Autonomous District Councils, only Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram.
  • Eighth Schedule — 22 languages. 21st (Sindhi) · 71st (Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali) · 92nd (Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali).
