UPSC & State Public Service Commissions

Articles 315–323. Two commissions built from the same blueprint, and examined almost entirely on the two places where the blueprint deliberately differs — who appoints, and who removes.

Articles 315–323 Governor appoints, President removes Six years or 65 / 62 Advisory only Art 320(3)
65/62the retirement ages — UPSC and State PSC. Learn them as a pair
◆ This is the note the Week 5 paper already reached into

In the Week 5 Prelims paper, Q10 asked whether the Governor appoints members of the State Public Service Commission, and Q25 asked about enlarging the jurisdiction of a State PSC. Both came a week before this topic was scheduled. That is this setter's habit — he reaches forward and backward across the plan — and it is why the State PSC half of this note matters as much as the UPSC half.

The whole chapter turns on one sentence: the Governor appoints, but only the President can remove. Everything else is detail hung on that frame.

Week 6, Day 4 of the study plan, sourced from NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work and Laxmikanth Chapters 44–45.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

Civil servants must be chosen on merit, not on the preference of whoever is in power. So the Constitution creates permanent, independent recruitment bodies: the Union Public Service Commission for the Union's services, and a State Public Service Commission for each State.

Here is the design problem the framers had to solve. If the State government both appoints and removes the people who select its officers, the Commission is no more independent than any department. So they split it: the Governor appoints the members of a State PSC, but only the President can remove them, and then only after an inquiry by the Supreme Court. A State government that dislikes its own Commission cannot simply dismiss it.

The second thing to hold is what these bodies actually do. They recommend; they do not appoint. Their advice under Article 320(3) is not binding, and the government may reject it — though it must record its reasons and report them to the legislature. A Commission that recruits without the power to compel is exactly the kind of half-power the examiner enjoys testing.

Three details that produce questions. (1) The 65 / 62 split is the single most reusable number pair in this chapter — and note that a Joint State PSC member also retires at 62. (2) The one-half rule requires ten years in government service, not ten years of any distinguished career; and it applies to members, with no qualification prescribed for the Chairman. (3) The Governor may appoint an acting Chairman of a State PSC when the office is vacant; the President does so for the UPSC.
◆
The Joint State Public Service Commission is the odd one out, and worth two minutes. Unlike the UPSC and a State PSC, a JSPSC is not a directly constitutional body — it is created by an Act of Parliament, on the request of the State legislatures concerned. Its chairman and members are appointed by the President, not by any Governor, and they are removed by the President like everyone else in this chapter. Term: six years or 62. The UPSC may also, at the request of two or more States and with the President's approval, help those States frame and operate joint recruitment schemes — Art 321 territory.
02

Removal — the asymmetry that decides questions

📖 The design, and why it is built this way

A State PSC's members are appointed by the Governor. They can be removed by only one person in India: the President. Not the Governor who appointed them, and not the State government they serve.

The reason is structural. If the appointing authority could also dismiss, the Commission would sit at the pleasure of the State executive — and the whole point of an independent recruitment body is that it can refuse to do what that executive wants. So the Constitution deliberately puts removal one level up, and then adds a second lock: for misbehaviour, the President must first refer the matter to the Supreme Court, and the Court's advice, after inquiry, is binding on him. That is unusual — an Art 143 opinion is normally advisory, but here Art 317(1) makes it binding.

This is the mirror of a pattern you have already met. The Governor appoints State PSC members but cannot remove them. Compare: the Governor appoints the Chief Minister but only the Assembly can remove him; the President appoints judges but only Parliament can initiate removal. Appointment and removal are deliberately separated wherever independence matters.

GroundWho removesProcedure
Misbehaviour — Art 317(1)PresidentOnly after the Supreme Court, on a reference by the President, holds an inquiry and upholds the cause. The Court's advice is binding. The President may suspend the member pending the report.
Adjudged insolventPresidentRemoval without a Supreme Court reference.
Engages in paid employment outside the duties of officePresidentSame.
Unfit by reason of infirmity of mind or bodyPresidentSame.
!
The distinction the examiner sets. Only misbehaviour requires the Supreme Court reference. The other three grounds — insolvency, paid employment, infirmity — are removed by the President directly. A statement saying "a member of a State PSC can be removed only after a Supreme Court inquiry" is therefore too broad and false. And a statement saying "the Governor may remove a member of the State Public Service Commission" is false on every ground.

Article 319 — the ladder of post-retirement eligibility, and it is asked directly.
UPSC Chairman — not eligible for any further employment under the Government of India or of a State. A complete bar.
UPSC member — eligible to become UPSC Chairman, or Chairman of a State PSC, but nothing else.
State PSC Chairman — eligible to become UPSC Chairman or member, or Chairman of another State PSC, but nothing else.
State PSC member — eligible to become Chairman of that or any other State PSC, or UPSC Chairman or member, but nothing else.

The pattern: you may move up or sideways within the Commission system, never out into government — and the UPSC Chairman, at the top, can go nowhere at all. Remember it as a ladder with a closed roof.
03

Functions — and the limits on them

FunctionDetail
ExaminationsConducts examinations for appointment to the services of the Union (UPSC) or of the State (State PSC).
Consultation — Art 320(3)Must be consulted on: methods of recruitment to civil services and posts; principles for appointments, promotions and transfers, and the suitability of candidates; all disciplinary matters affecting a civil servant, including memorials and petitions; claims for costs of legal proceedings incurred defending acts done in official capacity; and claims for a pension in respect of injuries.
Assisting StatesThe UPSC may assist two or more States, at their request and with the President's approval, in framing and operating joint recruitment schemes.
Extension of functionsArt 321 — Parliament may confer additional functions on the UPSC, and a State legislature on a State PSC, including in respect of the services of a local authority or other body corporate.
Placing services within scopeArt 320(2) — the UPSC or a State PSC shall, if the President or Governor so requires, be consulted on any matter; and services may be brought within the Commission's purview by that route.
!
The three limits — this is where the marks are.
(1) The advice is not binding. Art 320(3) obliges the government to consult, not to obey. Where advice is rejected, the reasons must be recorded and a memorandum laid before the legislature — accountability by publicity, not by compulsion.
(2) The Commission does not appoint. It recommends; the appointing authority is the government.
(3) Consultation itself can be dispensed with. Under the proviso to Art 320(3), the President or Governor may make regulations specifying matters on which the Commission need not be consulted — and such regulations must be laid before the legislature for at least fourteen days, which may modify or annul them. So even the duty to consult has an exit.

Two more exclusions worth holding: the Commission is not consulted on reservations for backward classes, or on the claims of SCs and STs to posts, and it has no role in the selection of All India Services personnel for State cadres beyond the initial recruitment.
◆
Enlarging the jurisdiction of a State PSC — the Week 5 question. Under Art 321, the State legislature may confer additional functions on a State PSC. This is one of the matters on which the Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha have equal powers when it concerns the UPSC, and it is a State legislature matter for a State PSC. If a stem asks who may enlarge the jurisdiction of a Public Service Commission, the answer is the legislature — never the executive, and never the Commission itself.

Prelims Questions

(A) Genuine previous-year questions

2023UPSC Prelims · How many

Consider the following organizations/bodies in India:

  1. The National Commission for Backward Classes
  2. The National Human Rights Commission
  3. The National Law Commission
  4. The National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission

How many of the above are constitutional bodies?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (a) — only one
  • Only the NCBC is constitutional, since the 102nd Amendment, 2018 inserted Article 338B. The NHRC is statutory (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993), the Law Commission is neither statutory nor constitutional — it is an executive body constituted by government resolution — and the NCDRC is statutory under the Consumer Protection Act.
  • This is the exact taxonomy Week 6 is built on. Carry the four-way test: constitutional (in the text) · statutory (created by an Act) · executive (created by a resolution) · quasi-judicial (a function, not a source).
  • Placed here because the same question also disposes of the UPSC's status: it is constitutional, from Art 315.
2013UPSC Prelims · Direct

Which one of the following statements is correct?

  • (a) In India, the same person cannot be appointed as Governor for two or more States at the same time
  • (b) The Judges of the High Court of the States in India are appointed by the Governor of the State just as the Judges of the Supreme Court are appointed by the President
  • (c) No procedure has been laid down in the Constitution of India for the removal of a Governor from his/her post
  • (d) In the case of a Union Territory having a legislative setup, the Chief Minister is appointed by the Lt. Governor on the basis of majority support
Answer: (c)
  • Included here for the appointment-versus-removal pattern it illustrates. Option (c) is correct because the Governor holds office during the President's pleasure with no laid-down removal procedure — the opposite of a State PSC member, where the procedure is spelt out in detail and reserved to the President.
  • Hold the contrast: the Governor can be removed with no procedure at all; the people he appoints to the State PSC can only be removed by the President after a Supreme Court inquiry. The appointee is better protected than the appointer.

(B) Practice questions

Practice · Negative

Which one of the following statements about a State Public Service Commission is NOT correct?

  • (a) Its chairman and members are appointed by the Governor
  • (b) Its chairman and members can be removed by the Governor
  • (c) Its expenses are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State
  • (d) Its members hold office for six years or until the age of sixty-two
Answer: (b) is NOT correct
  • Removal is by the President alone — Article 317. The Governor appoints and cannot remove, and that separation is the whole point of the design.
  • This exact fact was tested in the Week 5 paper as part of Q10 and is the most predictable question in the chapter.
Practice · How many

Consider the following statements about the Union Public Service Commission:

  1. The Constitution does not specify the strength of the Commission.
  2. At least one-half of its members must have held office under the Government of India or of a State for at least ten years.
  3. Its chairman is not eligible for any further employment under the Government of India or of a State.
  4. Its advice under Article 320(3) is binding on the Government.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (c) — only three
  • Statements 1, 2 and 3 are correct. Statement 4 is wrong — the advice is advisory only. Where it is rejected, the government must record its reasons and lay a memorandum before the legislature.
  • Statement 3 is the top rung of the Article 319 ladder: the UPSC Chairman is barred from everything, while everyone below him may move up or sideways.
Practice · How many

Consider the following statements regarding the removal of a member of a Public Service Commission:

  1. He may be removed by the President on the ground of misbehaviour after an inquiry by the Supreme Court.
  2. The Supreme Court's advice in such a reference is binding on the President.
  3. He may be removed by the President if he is adjudged an insolvent, without any reference to the Supreme Court.
  4. The President may suspend him pending the Supreme Court's report.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (d) — all four
  • All accurate. The one worth dwelling on is statement 2 — a Supreme Court opinion is normally advisory, but under Art 317(1) it binds the President. That is a genuine exception to the general rule you learnt in Week 5.
  • Another "all correct" set. The instinct to find a flaw is what this format punishes.
Practice · Pairs

Consider the following pairs of Article and subject:

  1. Article 317 — removal and suspension of a member of a Public Service Commission
  2. Article 319 — prohibition on holding office after ceasing to be a member
  3. Article 320 — functions of Public Service Commissions
  4. Article 322 — annual report of the Commission

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

Practice · Assertion–Reason

Consider the following two statements:

  1. Statement-I: A State government cannot dismiss the members of its own Public Service Commission.
  2. Statement-II: Members of a State Public Service Commission can be removed only by the President.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

  • (a) Both correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I
  • (b) Both correct but Statement-II does not explain Statement-I
  • (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is not
  • (d) Statement-I is not correct but Statement-II is
Answer: (a)
  • Both correct, and the second is precisely why the first is true. Article 317 reserves removal to the President, so the State executive has no route to dismiss the body that recruits its officers.
  • Three-step method: truth of I, truth of II, then the link. Here the link is direct and causal.
Practice · Direct

A Joint State Public Service Commission is constituted by

  • (a) the Governors of the States concerned, by agreement
  • (b) Parliament, by law, on the request of the State legislatures concerned
  • (c) the President, by order
  • (d) the Union Public Service Commission
Answer: (b)
  • A JSPSC is therefore a statutory body, unlike the UPSC and State PSCs which are constitutional — but its chairman and members are appointed and removed by the President, and they retire at 62.
  • Whenever a body sits between two levels of government, check the instrument that creates it. That is what decides its status.
Practice · Negative

Which one of the following is NOT a matter on which a Public Service Commission must be consulted under Article 320(3)?

  • (a) Methods of recruitment to civil services and civil posts
  • (b) Disciplinary matters affecting a person serving under the Government
  • (c) The manner in which reservations for backward classes are to be made
  • (d) Claims for reimbursement of legal costs incurred in defending official acts
Answer: (c) is NOT such a matter
  • The Commission is not consulted on reservations for backward classes, or on the claims of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes to posts.
  • Remember also the proviso to Art 320(3): the President or Governor may make regulations specifying further matters on which consultation is not required — laid before the legislature for fourteen days.

Mains Questions

Written to the MAINS_ANSWER_CRAFT.md standard.

M48Practice · Week 610 marks · 150 words

"The Public Service Commissions are protected in their composition but powerless in their advice." Examine.

Directive: "Examine." Two halves to test — the protection, and the powerlessness. Agree with both, then say what follows.
Skeleton
  1. The protection
  2. The powerlessness
  3. Why the design is deliberate
  4. Verdict

Model answer

The protection is real and unusually strong. Members of a State Public Service Commission are appointed by the Governor but removable only by the President, and for misbehaviour only after a Supreme Court inquiry whose advice binds him — Art 317. Conditions of service cannot be varied to disadvantage (Art 318), expenses are charged on the Consolidated Fund (Art 322), and Art 319 bars almost all post-retirement office.

The powerlessness

Against that, Art 320(3) obliges the government only to consult. The advice does not bind; the Commission recommends but does not appoint; and the proviso permits regulations excluding consultation altogether. Rejection carries no sanction beyond a memorandum laid before the legislature.

Why

The asymmetry is deliberate. Independence was needed to keep selection honest; binding advice would have transferred executive responsibility for the services to an unelected body. The framers secured the process and left the decision political.

The proposition holds, and the remedy is not to make advice binding but to make rejection costly — reasoned, published, and debated.

Value additions used: Arts 317, 318, 319, 320(3) and its proviso, 322, 323 · the binding Supreme Court reference as an exception to advisory jurisdiction · the memorandum requirement.
Budget check: 173 / 150 words. Cut: the 65/62 ages and the one-half rule — Prelims facts that do not bear on either half of the proposition. Bought: the "why" paragraph, which turns a list of complaints into a constitutional argument, and the closing reform, which is specific rather than a call for balance.
What would cost you marks: arguing that the advice should be made binding without confronting the accountability objection — the examiner will read it as unconsidered; and describing UPSC functions at length, which the question does not ask about.

Last-mile revision — UPSC & State PSCs

  • Art 315 — UPSC for the Union, a PSC for each State, Joint State PSC by Parliament on the States' request (so a JSPSC is statutory, not constitutional).
  • Appointment: UPSC → President · State PSC → Governor · Joint State PSC → President.
  • Removal: the PRESIDENT, in every case. Never the Governor. Misbehaviour → reference to the Supreme Court, whose advice binds; President may suspend meanwhile. Insolvency · paid employment · infirmity → removed directly, no reference.
  • Art 316 — strength fixed by the executive, not the Constitution. At least one-half of members with 10 years in government service. 6 years or 65 (UPSC) · 6 years or 62 (State and Joint PSC). No reappointment.
  • Art 319 ladder: UPSC Chairman → nothing. UPSC member → UPSC Chairman or State PSC Chairman. State PSC Chairman → UPSC Chairman/member or another State PSC Chairman. State PSC member → State PSC Chairman anywhere, or UPSC Chairman/member. Up or sideways, never out.
  • Art 320(3) — consultation on recruitment methods · principles of appointment, promotion, transfer and suitability · disciplinary matters · legal costs of official acts · injury pensions. Not on reservation or SC/ST claims. Proviso — regulations may exclude consultation, laid before the legislature for 14 days.
  • The advice is never binding. The Commission recommends; the government appoints. Rejection requires reasons and a memorandum to the legislature.
  • Art 321 — Parliament may extend UPSC functions; a State legislature may extend a State PSC's, including to local authorities.
  • Art 322 charged expenses · Art 323 annual report — to the President / Governor, laid before the legislature.
  • Status test for Week 6: constitutional = in the text (UPSC, State PSC, ECI, Finance Commission, NCSC, NCST, NCBC, CAG, AG) · statutory = created by an Act (NHRC, NCDRC, Joint State PSC, CIC, Lokpal) · executive = created by resolution (NITI Aayog, Law Commission).
