Cabinet Committees & Parliamentary Forums

Two bodies the Constitution never mentions, both of which do a great deal of real work — and both examined almost entirely on who chairs what.

Extra-constitutional Rules of Business · Art 77(3) CCS & CCEA Appointments Committee Forums since 2005
0times either body is named in the Constitution
◆ A short note, and deliberately so

This is the smallest topic in Week 4 and it is worth being honest about that. There is no doctrine here, no case law and no amendment history — just two lists and a set of chairmanships. What makes it examinable is precisely that it is extra-constitutional: a question can assert that Cabinet Committees are "provided for in the Constitution" and be wrong, or swap the Home Minister for the Prime Minister as chair and be wrong, and both look plausible if you have only skimmed.

Learn the chairs. That is the note.

Week 4, Day 5 of the study plan, sourced from NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work Chapter 13 and Laxmikanth Chapters 22 and 25.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

A Union Cabinet of around thirty ministers cannot usefully sit around one table and decide everything. So the work is divided among Cabinet Committees — small groups of ministers, each handling one field, which take decisions the full Cabinet then usually just ratifies. Security, economic affairs, appointments and parliamentary business each have their own.

None of this is in the Constitution. The committees exist under the Rules of Business that the President makes under Article 77(3) for the more convenient transaction of government business. The Prime Minister constitutes them, decides who sits on them and can abolish or create them at will — which is why their number and names change with every government.

Parliamentary Forums are a different animal that gets bracketed with them because both are non-constitutional and both are chaired by senior figures. A Forum is not a decision-making body at all: it is a cross-party group of MPs set up from 2005 onward to sensitise members on a specific issue — water, population, children, disaster management — with no power to decide anything.

01

Cabinet Committees — nature and basis

PointDetail
Constitutional statusExtra-constitutional — the Constitution nowhere mentions them. They are established under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, framed by the President under Article 77(3), which empowers the President to make rules for the more convenient transaction of business.
Two kindsStanding — permanent, reconstituted with each government. Ad hoc — temporary, set up for a specific problem and dissolved when it reports. Ad hoc committees are also called Groups of Ministers (GoMs); an Empowered GoM can take a decision itself, an ordinary GoM only recommends.
Who sets them upThe Prime Minister, according to the needs of the time. The PM decides their number, composition and functions, and can alter any of these at any time.
MembershipNormally three to eight members. Usually only Cabinet Ministers, but non-Cabinet Ministers are not barred from membership — a common trap statement claims they are.
Who chairsWhoever the PM decides. In practice the Prime Minister chairs most of the important ones, and where the PM is a member of a committee, the PM invariably presides.
What they doThree functions: resolve issues referred to them, work out proposals for the Cabinet's consideration, and take decisions — though the Cabinet may review a decision.
!
The three statements most likely to be planted. (1) "Cabinet Committees are provided for in the Constitution" — false; they rest on Rules of Business under Art 77(3). (2) "Only Cabinet Ministers may be members" — false; non-Cabinet Ministers may be included. (3) "The Cabinet cannot review a Cabinet Committee's decision" — false; it can. Note also that Article 77(3) is about allocation and convenience, not about creating authority — the authority is the Cabinet's throughout.
02

The standing committees — and who chairs each

The number has varied between six and ten across governments; eight is the figure usually cited. Learn the four in bold — they carry almost all the questions.

CommitteeRemit and chair
Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC)Decides senior appointments across the Union government — Secretaries, and other posts in the higher civil services. Chaired by the Prime Minister. Distinctive in that its membership is very small, typically the PM and the Home Minister.
Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS)The apex body on defence expenditure, national security, law and order, and senior appointments in the security establishment. Chaired by the Prime Minister. Members typically the Ministers of Defence, Home, External Affairs and Finance.
Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)Reviews economic trends and clears major economic and investment decisions. Chaired by the Prime Minister.
Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary AffairsHandles the government's legislative programme and the progress of business in Parliament. Chaired by the Home Minister — this is the exception that gets tested, because every other major committee is chaired by the PM.
Cabinet Committee on Political AffairsDeals with policy matters and problems of political significance, including Centre–State relations. Chaired by the Prime Minister.
Cabinet Committee on AccommodationAllotment of government accommodation to ministers, MPs and officials. Chaired by the Home Minister.
Cabinet Committee on Investment and GrowthClearance and monitoring of major investment projects and time-bound approvals.
Cabinet Committee on Employment and Skill DevelopmentEmployment generation and skilling policy.
✚
The one-line memory hook. Ask "does this committee decide something the Prime Minister must own?" Security, the economy, senior appointments and political affairs — the PM chairs all four. Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation are the housekeeping committees, and both go to the Home Minister. If a question puts the Home Minister in charge of the CCS or the PM in charge of Parliamentary Affairs, it has swapped them.
03

Parliamentary Forums

PointDetail
What they areCross-party groups of members from both Houses, created from 2005 onwards to sensitise members on critical issues and to give them a space to engage experts and ministries outside the adversarial floor of the House.
StatusNot constitutional and not statutory — created by the presiding officers under the Rules, on the initiative of the Speaker.
PowersNone that bind. A Forum does not legislate, does not summon, does not report to the House for action. Its function is awareness and capacity-building. This is the cleanest contrast with a parliamentary committee, which does examine, summon and report.
ChairmanshipThe Speaker of the Lok Sabha is ex-officio President of every Forum — except the Forum on Population and Public Health, where the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is ex-officio President and the Speaker becomes ex-officio Co-President. Ex-officio Vice-Presidents are the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, the Deputy Speaker, the concerned Ministers, and the Chairmen of the relevant Departmentally Related Standing Committees.
MembershipNot more than 31 members, excluding the President, Co-President and Vice-Presidents — not more than 21 from the Lok Sabha and not more than 10 from the Rajya Sabha. A member may join more than one Forum.
The ForumsThe first was Water Conservation and Management, constituted by the Speaker on 12 August 2005. Others cover Youth · Children · Population and Public Health · Global Warming and Climate Change · Disaster Management · Artisans and Craftspeople · Sustainable Development Goals. Eight are usually cited.
!
Forum versus committee — the distinction the question actually tests. A parliamentary committee is constituted by the House or the presiding officer, works under its direction, reports to the House, and derives its authority ultimately from Articles 105 and 118. A Forum reports to nobody and decides nothing; it exists to inform members. If a statement gives a Forum the power to examine a ministry's spending or summon officials, it has confused it with a Departmentally Related Standing Committee.
04

Prelims Questions

Practice · Statements

With reference to Cabinet Committees, consider the following statements:

  1. They are established by the Rules of Business framed under Article 77(3).
  2. Their membership is restricted to Cabinet Ministers alone.
  3. They are set up by the Prime Minister according to the needs of the time.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

  • (a) 1 and 2 only
  • (b) 2 and 3 only
  • (c) 1 and 3 only
  • (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c) — 1 and 3 only
  • Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Cabinet Committees rest on the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules made under Article 77(3), and the Prime Minister constitutes them.
  • Statement 2 is wrong. Membership is usually confined to Cabinet Ministers, but non-Cabinet Ministers are not barred. The word "alone" is what makes the statement false.
  • Watch for absolutes — "alone", "only", "always". They are the commonest way a true-sounding statement is made false.
Practice · Negative

Which one of the following is NOT ordinarily chaired by the Prime Minister?

  • (a) Cabinet Committee on Security
  • (b) Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs
  • (c) Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs
  • (d) Appointments Committee of the Cabinet
Answer: (c) is NOT chaired by the Prime Minister
  • The Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs is chaired by the Home Minister, as is the Committee on Accommodation.
  • Security, Economic Affairs, Appointments and Political Affairs are all chaired by the Prime Minister.
  • The hook: the PM chairs what the PM must own — security, the economy, senior appointments, politics. The housekeeping committees go to the Home Minister.
Practice · Statements

With reference to Parliamentary Forums, consider the following statements:

  1. They have a statutory basis under a law made by Parliament.
  2. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha is the ex-officio President of every Forum without exception.
  3. A Forum may summon officials and report its findings to the House for action.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) All three
  • (d) None
Answer: (d) — none
  • Statement 1 is wrong. Forums are neither constitutional nor statutory — they were created by the presiding officers under the Rules, from 2005.
  • Statement 2 is wrong on the exception. The Speaker presides over every Forum except Population and Public Health, where the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is ex-officio President and the Speaker is Co-President.
  • Statement 3 is wrong. That describes a parliamentary committee. A Forum has no power to summon, examine or report for action — its purpose is to sensitise members.
  • "None of the above" is a real answer and appears in genuine papers. Do not treat it as a filler option.
Practice · Pairs

Consider the following pairs:

  1. Appointments Committee of the Cabinet — Prime Minister
  2. Cabinet Committee on Accommodation — Home Minister
  3. Parliamentary Forum on Water Conservation — Speaker of the Lok Sabha

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) All three
  • (d) None
Answer: (c) — all three
  • All three chairs are correct. Water Conservation and Management was the first Forum, constituted on 12 August 2005, and the Speaker presides as with every Forum except Population and Public Health.
  • Note the pattern once more: a set of three where all are correct. The reflex to find a flaw costs marks.
Practice · Direct

A Group of Ministers that has been authorised to take a decision itself, rather than merely to recommend, is described as:

  • (a) A Standing Cabinet Committee
  • (b) An Empowered Group of Ministers
  • (c) A Departmentally Related Standing Committee
  • (d) A Parliamentary Forum
Answer: (b) — an Empowered Group of Ministers
  • An ad hoc Cabinet Committee is commonly called a Group of Ministers. Where it is authorised to decide rather than recommend, it is an Empowered GoM.
  • (c) is a parliamentary body examining ministries — a different institution entirely, sitting under Articles 105 and 118 rather than Article 77(3).
Practice · Assertion–Reason

Statement-I: The number and composition of Cabinet Committees changes from one government to the next.

Statement-II: They are created under the Rules of Business rather than by the Constitution, and the Prime Minister may alter them at will.

Which one of the following is correct?

  • (a) Both are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation of Statement-I
  • (b) Both are correct but Statement-II is not the correct explanation of Statement-I
  • (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
  • (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct
Answer: (a)
  • Both correct, and the second is exactly why the first is true. Because the basis is the Rules of Business under Art 77(3) and not the Constitution, no amendment is needed to add, merge or abolish a committee.
  • Contrast a constitutional body such as the Finance Commission (Art 280) or the UPSC, whose existence no Prime Minister can alter.

Mains Questions

Written to the MAINS_ANSWER_CRAFT.md standard.

M33Practice · Week 410 marks · 150 words

"Cabinet Committees have become the real locus of executive decision-making in India." Comment.

Directive: "Comment." Form a judgement on the claim and support it — shorter and more opinionated than "examine", but the opinion must be evidenced.
Skeleton
  1. What they are and their basis
  2. Why the claim has force
  3. The constitutional qualification
  4. Verdict

Model answer

Cabinet Committees are extra-constitutional bodies created under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules framed by the President under Article 77(3). The Prime Minister constitutes them, fixes their composition and may alter them at will.

Why the claim has force

A Council of Ministers capped at 15% of the Lok Sabha still numbers around eighty, and the Cabinet around thirty — too many for genuine deliberation. Decisions on national security run through the CCS, major economic clearances through the CCEA, and senior civil-service appointments through the ACC, each chaired by the Prime Minister. The full Cabinet frequently ratifies rather than decides, and Empowered Groups of Ministers can conclude matters outright.

The constitutional qualification

But locus is not authority. Article 74 vests advice in the Council of Ministers as a body; Article 75(3) makes that whole Council collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. A Committee's decision remains reviewable by the Cabinet, and no Minister can disown one on the floor. Concentration of process has not shifted accountability.

The claim is therefore accurate descriptively and misleading constitutionally. Committees are where decisions are made; the Cabinet remains where they are owned — and that gap between the two is precisely what critics of prime-ministerial government point to.

Value additions used: Art 77(3) and the Transaction of Business Rules · the 15% cap giving a concrete size · CCS, CCEA and ACC named with their remits · Empowered GoM · Arts 74 and 75(3) · the process-versus-accountability distinction.
Budget check: 176 / 150 words. Cut: the standing/ad hoc taxonomy and the three functions of a committee — both true, neither serving the question's claim. Bought: the Article 74/75(3) qualification, which is what converts a description into a judgement and satisfies "comment".
What would cost you marks: listing the committees without taking a position; and omitting the accountability point, which is the only thing that makes the answer constitutional rather than administrative.

Last-mile revision — Cabinet Committees & Parliamentary Forums

  • Cabinet Committees are extra-constitutional — Rules of Business under Article 77(3). Set up by the PM, number and composition alterable at will.
  • Two kinds: standing and ad hoc (Groups of Ministers). An Empowered GoM decides; an ordinary GoM recommends.
  • Usually 3–8 members. Non-Cabinet Ministers may be members — "only Cabinet Ministers" is a false statement.
  • The Cabinet can review a Committee's decision.
  • PM chairs: Security (CCS) · Economic Affairs (CCEA) · Appointments (ACC) · Political Affairs.
  • Home Minister chairs: Parliamentary Affairs · Accommodation. These two are the exceptions and the tested ones.
  • Parliamentary Forums — neither constitutional nor statutory; created from 2005 by the presiding officers. No power to summon, examine or report for action.
  • Speaker is ex-officio President of every Forum except Population & Public Health, where the RS Chairman presides and the Speaker is Co-President.
  • Forum size: max 31 excluding office-holders — up to 21 LS + 10 RS. Vice-Presidents include the Deputy Speaker, Deputy Chairman, concerned Ministers and DRSC Chairmen.
  • First Forum: Water Conservation and Management, 12 August 2005.
  • Forum vs committee: a committee reports to the House and draws authority from Arts 105 and 118; a Forum reports to nobody.
