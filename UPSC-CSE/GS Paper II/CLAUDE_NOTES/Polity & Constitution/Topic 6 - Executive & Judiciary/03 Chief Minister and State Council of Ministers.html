Chief Minister & State Council of Ministers

Articles 163–164 — the State mirror of the Prime Minister and Union Council. Structurally almost identical, and examined almost entirely on the handful of places where the mirror is deliberately imperfect.

Articles 163–164 Art 164(1A) — 15% cap Tribal Welfare Minister Art 167 — CM's duties Shamsher Singh (1974)
12the floor of the 15% cap — a State ministry can never be smaller
◆ Read this against the PM note, not on its own

Ninety per cent of this note is Topic 6 Note 02 with "President" swapped for "Governor" and "Lok Sabha" for "Legislative Assembly". That similarity is exactly why questions here are set on the differences: the minimum-size floor that has no Union equivalent, the compulsory Tribal Welfare Minister in four named States, and the fact that a State minister's six-month rule sits in a differently numbered clause.

Week 4, Day 2 of the study plan, sourced from NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work and Laxmikanth Chapters 31–32.

🌱 Start here — plain English first

A State is governed the same way the Union is. The Governor is the constitutional head who signs things; the Chief Minister is the political head who decides them. The Governor appoints the Chief Minister, but in practice has no choice when one party holds a clear Assembly majority — the discretion appears only in a hung Assembly, which is the situation the Governor note deals with at length.

The Chief Minister then picks a team — the Council of Ministers — whom the Governor appoints on the CM's advice. That Council is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly: if the Assembly votes no confidence, the whole Council goes, not just the minister who caused the trouble. Individually, each minister holds office "during the pleasure of the Governor", which in practice means the Chief Minister can have any one of them dropped without the government falling.

If that sounds exactly like the Prime Minister, it is meant to. The framers copied the Union model down to the wording. Learn the differences and you have learnt this chapter.

01

Appointment & tenure — Article 164

PointDetail
AppointmentThe Governor appoints the Chief Minister; other Ministers are appointed by the Governor on the CM's advice (Art 164(1)). There is no separate election "for Chief Minister" — the Governor invites whoever can command the Assembly's confidence.
Need not be a memberA person who is not a member of the State legislature may be appointed CM or Minister, but must become a member within six months or cease to hold office — Article 164(4). Note the clause number: the Union equivalent is Art 75(5).
Which HouseA Minister may belong to either House in a bicameral State, and can speak in both but vote only in the House of which they are a member — the State analogue of Article 88.
TenureMinisters hold office during the pleasure of the Governor (Art 164(1)), exercised on the Chief Minister's advice. A Chief Minister commanding an Assembly majority cannot in practice be dismissed — the "pleasure" is real only once the majority is lost.
Collective responsibilityThe Council is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly — Article 164(2). Note: to the Assembly alone, never to the Legislative Council, even in a bicameral State.
Oath and salariesOath of office and secrecy administered by the Governor (Art 164(3)); salaries determined by the State legislature (Art 164(5)).
!
The two provisions with no Union equivalent — this is where the marks are. (1) Article 164(1A), inserted by the 91st Amendment, 2003: the Council of Ministers including the Chief Minister must not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Legislative Assembly — but with a minimum of 12. The Union cap has a ceiling and no floor; the State cap has both. For a small Assembly like Sikkim's 32 seats, 15% would be under 5, so the floor of 12 governs. (2) Article 164(1) proviso: in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha there shall be a Minister in charge of tribal welfare, who may also hold charge of welfare of Scheduled Castes and Backward Classes. Bihar was in this list until the 94th Amendment, 2006 removed it and added Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand.
02

Powers & functions of the Chief Minister

AreaWhat the Chief Minister actually does
In relation to the CouncilRecommends who is appointed a Minister; allocates and reshuffles portfolios; can ask a Minister to resign or advise the Governor to dismiss one. The CM's own resignation or death dissolves the entire Council — the death or resignation of any other Minister merely creates a vacancy. That asymmetry is the clearest proof the CM is not merely first among equals.
In relation to the Governor (Art 167)The State analogue of Article 78. The CM must communicate all decisions of the Council relating to State administration and legislative proposals; furnish information the Governor calls for; and, if the Governor requires, place before the Council any matter on which a Minister has decided alone. Article 167 is examined as often as its content deserves — it is the CM's only expressly enumerated duty.
In relation to the legislatureAdvises the Governor on summoning and proroguing the Assembly and on its dissolution; announces government policy on the floor.
Other rolesChairman of the State Planning Board; a member of the Inter-State Council and of the Governing Council of NITI Aayog; vice-chairman of the relevant Zonal Council by rotation, for a year at a time; chief spokesman of the government; and the political head of the State's crisis machinery.
✚
The advice the Governor need not take. Because Article 163 leaves the Governor a discretionary sphere the President does not have, there are matters on which the CM's advice does not bind — recommending President's Rule, reserving a Bill for the President, and the Article 371-series responsibilities. This is the single structural difference between the CM's position and the Prime Minister's, and it is why Centre–State friction is fought through the Governor's office rather than the Chief Minister's.
03

The State Council of Ministers

PointDetail
CompositionThree ranks, exactly as at the Union: Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State and Deputy Ministers. Some States also have a Parliamentary Secretary, a practice courts have struck down in several States as a device to evade the 15% cap.
Council vs CabinetEvery Cabinet Minister is a member of the Council of Ministers; not every member of the Council sits in the Cabinet. Collective responsibility under Art 164(2) binds the entire Council, not merely the Cabinet tier — so a Deputy Minister who never attends a Cabinet meeting still falls with the government.
Aid and adviceArticle 163(1): there shall be a Council of Ministers to aid and advise the Governor, except in so far as the Governor is required to act in his discretion. Article 163(2) makes the Governor's own decision on what is discretionary final, and 163(3) bars courts from inquiring into what advice was tendered — the State analogue of Article 74(2).
Is the advice binding?Yes, outside the discretionary sphere. Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974), seven judges, settled that the Governor is a constitutional head who acts on ministerial advice save in a few well-known exceptional situations. Note the contrast: Article 74 was amended (42nd and 44th) to spell out that Union advice binds; Article 163 was never amended at all.
DisqualificationArticle 164(1B), also from the 91st Amendment: a member disqualified on the ground of defection is also disqualified from being appointed a Minister for the duration of the remaining term.
!
Two responsibility rules, one Article. Collective responsibility sits in 164(2) and runs to the Legislative Assembly alone. Individual responsibility runs through the "pleasure" clause in 164(1), exercised on the CM's advice. The Assembly cannot remove an individual Minister by voting against them — it can act only against the Council as a whole. Collective responsibility supplies the norm; individual responsibility supplies the sanction.

✚
Why the mirror is imperfect, in one line for Mains. The President's discretion is unwritten and situational; the Governor's is textually anchored in Article 163 and enlarged by Articles 200 and 356. That is a deliberate design choice, not an oversight — the framers wanted a Union agent in every State — and it is why Sarkaria, Punchhi and the 2025 assent litigation all concern the Governor and never the President.
!
Confidence is not only lost by a no-confidence motion. The Council must also resign if the Budget is rejected, if any cut motion is carried against the government, if the Motion of Thanks is defeated, or if a confidence motion it moves is lost. A censure motion is the exception — the Council need not resign but must seek the confidence of the House as soon as possible. The working rule: money defeat = confidence defeat; criticism is not. Full treatment in Topic 5, Note 03.

Prelims Questions

2019UPSC Prelims · Direct

In the Constitution of India, promotion of international peace and security is included in the

  • (a) Preamble to the Constitution
  • (b) Directive Principles of State Policy
  • (c) Fundamental Duties
  • (d) Ninth Schedule
Answer: (b) — Directive Principles
  • Article 51, in Part IV. Included here as a reminder that State-executive questions sit inside a wider Part III/IV frame you are already carrying.
  • The distractor to watch is (c): Article 51A duties are citizen-facing, whereas Article 51 directs the State.
Practice · Statements

With reference to Article 164 of the Constitution, consider the following statements:

  1. The total number of Ministers including the Chief Minister shall not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Legislative Assembly.
  2. The number of Ministers in a State shall in no case be less than twelve.
  3. A Minister who is not a member of the State legislature must become one within six months.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) All three
  • (d) None
Answer: (c) — all three
  • All three are accurate: 164(1A) supplies both the 15% ceiling and the floor of twelve, and 164(4) the six-month rule.
  • The floor of twelve is the fact most often missed, because the Union cap has no equivalent. For a small Assembly the floor, not the percentage, governs.
  • Another "all correct" set. If you found yourself hunting for a flaw in one of them, that is the reflex to keep working on.
Practice · Negative

Which one of the following statements about the State Council of Ministers is NOT correct?

  • (a) It is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly
  • (b) Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the Governor
  • (c) It is collectively responsible to both Houses in a bicameral State
  • (d) A Minister disqualified for defection cannot be appointed a Minister for the remaining term
Answer: (c) is NOT correct
  • Collective responsibility under Article 164(2) runs to the Legislative Assembly alone, never to the Legislative Council — exactly as the Union Council answers only to the Lok Sabha.
  • (a), (b) and (d) are all accurate; (d) is Article 164(1B) from the 91st Amendment.
  • The pattern to hold across the whole syllabus: confidence flows from the lower house only, at both levels.
Practice · Pairs

Consider the following pairs of provision and subject:

  1. Article 163 — Council of Ministers to aid and advise the Governor
  2. Article 167 — duties of the Chief Minister towards the Governor
  3. Article 164(1B) — disqualification of a defector from ministerial office

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) All three
  • (d) None
Answer: (c) — all three
  • All correct. The Union equivalents to memorise alongside: 163 ↔ 74, 167 ↔ 78, 164(1B) ↔ 75(1B).
  • Article numbers are the most mechanical marks in Polity and the easiest to lose. Learn them in Union–State pairs rather than as a flat list.
Practice · Statements

Consider the following statements regarding the Chief Minister:

  1. The resignation or death of the Chief Minister brings about the dissolution of the entire Council of Ministers.
  2. The Governor is bound by the advice of the Chief Minister in recommending President's Rule in the State.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  • (a) 1 only
  • (b) 2 only
  • (c) Both 1 and 2
  • (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (a) — 1 only
  • Statement 1 is correct, and the asymmetry matters: the death or resignation of any other Minister creates only a vacancy.
  • Statement 2 is wrong. Recommending President's Rule is one of the Governor's discretionary functions under Article 163 — a Governor who needed the Chief Minister's consent to report against that Chief Minister's own government would make Article 356 unusable.
  • Whenever a statement puts the Governor under an obligation to follow advice, check first whether the function sits in the discretionary list.
Practice · Assertion–Reason

Statement-I: The Governor's position differs from the President's despite near-identical wording on aid and advice.

Statement-II: Article 163 contains an express discretionary exception, and unlike Article 74 it has never been amended.

Which one of the following is correct?

  • (a) Both are correct and Statement-II is the correct explanation of Statement-I
  • (b) Both are correct but Statement-II is not the correct explanation of Statement-I
  • (c) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is incorrect
  • (d) Statement-I is incorrect but Statement-II is correct
Answer: (a)
  • Both correct, and the second explains the first exactly. Article 74 was amended by the 42nd (advice made binding) and the 44th (one reconsideration); Article 163 was left untouched and carries the discretionary carve-out.
  • Shamsher Singh (1974) confirmed both the President and the Governor are constitutional heads — but only the Governor has a textual discretionary sphere.
Practice · How many

In how many of the following States is a Minister in charge of tribal welfare constitutionally required?

  1. Chhattisgarh
  2. Jharkhand
  3. Bihar
  4. Odisha

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

  • (a) Only two
  • (b) Only three
  • (c) All four
  • (d) Only one
Answer: (b) — only three
  • The four States named in the proviso to Article 164(1) are Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha. Of the options listed, three qualify.
  • Bihar is the trap. It was in the original list and was removed by the 94th Amendment, 2006, which added Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand after their creation in 2000.
  • Madhya Pradesh is absent from the options — check the list you are given against the list you know, rather than counting what looks familiar.

Mains Questions

Written to the MAINS_ANSWER_CRAFT.md standard — budget-checked, lineage first, trade shown.

M31Practice · Week 410 marks · 150 words

"The Chief Minister's position mirrors the Prime Minister's in form but not in security." Discuss with reference to Articles 163 and 164.

Directive: "Discuss." Present both sides of the mirror — the identity of form, then the asymmetry — and close with a reasoned position.
Skeleton
  1. The identical form
  2. Where the mirror breaks
  3. Why it breaks — design, not accident
  4. Verdict

Model answer

The State executive replicates the Union model almost clause for clause: Article 164 mirrors Article 75 on appointment, the six-month rule, the 15% cap and collective responsibility, and Article 167 mirrors Article 78 on the head of government's duty to communicate.

Where the mirror breaks

Article 163 is not Article 74. It preserves an express discretionary sphere for the Governor, makes the Governor's view of what is discretionary final (163(2)), and has never been amended — while Article 74 was tightened twice, by the 42nd and 44th Amendments, precisely to remove Presidential discretion. Recommending President's Rule under Article 356 and reserving a Bill under Article 200 lie outside the Chief Minister's advice altogether.

Why it breaks

This is design, not oversight: the Governor is a Union appointee holding office during the President's pleasure under Article 156, with no fixed tenure. A Chief Minister therefore governs alongside an official whose security depends on New Delhi, which is why Sarkaria and Punchhi addressed the Governor and not the President.

The form is a mirror; the security is not. Shamsher Singh (1974) made both heads constitutional heads — but only one of them retains a constitutional discretion to use against the government of the day.

Value additions used: Arts 163, 163(2), 164, 167, 200, 356, 156 · the 42nd and 44th amendments to Art 74 contrasted with an unamended Art 163 · Shamsher Singh (1974) · Sarkaria and Punchhi.
Budget check: 178 / 150 words. Cut: the list of the three ministerial ranks, and a separate sentence on Art 164(1A), neither of which serves the "security" half of the question. Bought: the unamended-163 versus twice-amended-74 contrast, which is the sharpest single line available here.
What would cost you marks: listing the similarities and stopping — the question's weight is on "but not in security"; and omitting Article 156, which is what actually explains the asymmetry.

Last-mile revision — Chief Minister & State Council of Ministers

  • Art 163 aid and advice + discretionary exception; 163(2) Governor decides what is discretionary; 163(3) courts barred from the advice. Never amended — unlike Art 74.
  • Art 164(1) appointment + "pleasure of the Governor". Proviso: Tribal Welfare Minister compulsory in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha (Bihar removed by the 94th Amdt, 2006).
  • Art 164(1A) — 15% of the Assembly, minimum 12. The floor has no Union equivalent. 91st Amdt, 2003.
  • Art 164(1B) — a defector cannot be a Minister for the remaining term.
  • Art 164(2) — collective responsibility to the Legislative Assembly alone.
  • Art 164(3) oath by the Governor · 164(4) six-month rule · 164(5) salaries by the State legislature.
  • Art 167 — CM's three duties to the Governor. The State's Article 78.
  • Union ↔ State pairs: 74↔163 · 75↔164 · 78↔167 · 74(2)↔163(3) · 75(5)↔164(4) · 75(1A)↔164(1A).
  • CM's resignation/death dissolves the whole Council; any other Minister's creates only a vacancy.
  • Shamsher Singh (1974), 7 judges — Governor is a constitutional head acting on advice, save in exceptional situations.
  • Outside the CM's advice: recommending President's Rule, reserving a Bill (Art 200), Art 371-series duties.
  • CM is Chairman of the State Planning Board; member of the Inter-State Council and NITI Aayog Governing Council; Zonal Council vice-chairman by yearly rotation.
