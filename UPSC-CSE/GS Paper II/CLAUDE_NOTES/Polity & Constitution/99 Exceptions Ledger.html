The Exceptions Ledger

Every rule in the syllabus that has a carve-out, every "only" that is nearly true, and every number that has a near-identical twin — collected in one place, because this is the category UPSC builds statement questions from.

Cross-topic Statement-question fuel Updated after every test
1word is usually all that separates a true statement from a false one
◆ Why this note exists

In the Week 3 test you were asked what happens when the Budget fails to pass the Lok Sabha, and you answered in eleven seconds that it is modified and presented again. The correct answer is that the entire Council of Ministers resigns — a Budget defeat is an implied vote of no confidence. That fact is not hard. It was simply never surfaced as a rule with an exception, so there was nothing to recall.

You then asked, correctly, that such exceptions not be missed. This note is the answer. It is not new content — every entry lives in a topic note as well — but exceptions scattered across twenty notes are invisible, and gathered into one they become a pattern you can revise in ten minutes.

The working principle: an examiner cannot easily build a question from a rule everybody knows. They build it from the edge of that rule. So the edge is what you learn.

🌱 How to use this

Read it last, not first — it assumes you already know the rules these are exceptions to. Then revisit it the evening before every test. Four families are enough to hold in mind:

(1) Confidence exceptions — defeats that topple a government without a no-confidence motion, and the one that looks like it should but doesn't. (2) "Only" and "never" — powers that belong to exactly one body, where a statement naming any other body is wrong. (3) Numbers with a twin — pairs of near-identical figures where the question is simply which one goes where. (4) Rules with a carve-out — general propositions that are true except in a named case.

Every entry names the note it comes from, so you can go back to the reasoning.
01

Confidence & the executive

The rule you knowThe exception that gets asked
A government falls when a no-confidence motion is passedIt also falls on rejection of the Budget, on any cut motion carried against it, on defeat of the Motion of Thanks, and on losing a confidence motion it moved itself. Money defeat = confidence defeat. (T5 N03)
A motion that criticises the government threatens itCensure is the exception — if passed, the Council need not resign; it must seek the confidence of the House as soon as possible. An adjournment motion likewise compels nothing. (T5 N03)
Ministers hold office during the President's pleasureThe pleasure is exercised on the Prime Minister's advice, so a PM with a majority cannot be dismissed. The Lok Sabha cannot remove an individual Minister at all — only the Council as a whole. (T6 N02)
The Council of Ministers answers to ParliamentTo the Lok Sabha alone — never the Rajya Sabha, and at State level never the Legislative Council. (T6 N02, N03)
The President acts on ministerial adviceTrue after the 42nd Amendment — but the Governor retains an express discretionary sphere under Article 163, which was never amended. (T6 N01, N03)
The CM's advice binds the GovernorNot for recommending President's Rule, reserving a Bill under Art 200, or the Art 371-series duties. (T6 N03)
The death of a Minister creates a vacancyUnless it is the PM or CM — that dissolves the entire Council. (T6 N02, N03)
02

"Only" and "never" — powers belonging to exactly one body

In each row, a statement naming any body other than the one in bold is false. These are the cheapest marks in the paper and the easiest to lose.

PowerBelongs exclusively to
Pardon a death sentenceThe President alone. But the Governor can suspend, remit or commute one — losing only the pardon. On a court martial the Governor has no power at all. (T6 N00, N01)
Introduce a Money BillThe Lok Sabha — and at State level the Legislative Assembly. (T5 N02, N04)
Initiate removal of the Vice-PresidentThe Rajya Sabha alone, though the Lok Sabha must agree. (T6 N00)
Resolve to end a National EmergencyThe Lok Sabha alone, on one-tenth notice. (T1 N10)
Authorise legislation on a State List subject in the national interest (Art 249), or a new All-India Service (Art 312)The Rajya Sabha alone. (T5 N01)
Ratify a constitutional amendment on behalf of a StateThe Legislative Assembly alone — a Legislative Council has no role. (T5 N04)
Decide a Tenth Schedule disqualificationThe Speaker or Chairman — not the President, not the Election Commission. On Article 102 grounds it is the President, bound by the EC's opinion; the EC has no suo motu power. (T5 N01)
Hear disputes about a Presidential or Vice-Presidential electionThe Supreme Court, finally — and acts done before an election is voided stay valid (Art 71). (T6 N00)
Summon a joint sittingThe President, and only for Parliament. No joint sitting exists at State level, and none for a Money Bill or a Constitutional Amendment Bill. (T5 N01, N04)

04

Rules with a carve-out

The general ruleExcept
A candidate for the Lok Sabha may be an elector in any constituency in IndiaSeats in the autonomous districts of Assam, Lakshadweep and Sikkim require a local elector. The word "any" is the trap. (T5 N01)
Nominated members are excluded from the Presidential electoral collegeNominated members do vote in the Vice-Presidential election. (T6 N00)
The Speaker presides over parliamentary forumsExcept the Forum on Population and Public Health, where the Rajya Sabha Chairman does. (T6 N04)
The Prime Minister chairs the major Cabinet CommitteesExcept Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation — the Home Minister. (T6 N04)
The Speaker appoints committee chairmenTrue of the PAC too — what is special is that the appointee is from the Opposition by convention since 1967. (T5 N03)
Question Hour comes first, Zero Hour afterTrue in the Lok Sabha. In the Rajya Sabha since 2014 Zero Hour is at 11 a.m. and Question Hour at noon. (T5 N03)
A law changing a State legislature's composition is a constitutional amendmentArticle 169 expressly says a law creating or abolishing a Legislative Council is not an Art 368 amendment. (T5 N04)
Judicial salaries cannot be reducedThey can during a Financial Emergency under Art 360 — expressly including Supreme Court and High Court judges. (T1 N10)
Fundamental Rights cannot be suspendedThey can under a National Emergency — but never Articles 20 and 21, and Art 358 only on war or external aggression. (T1 N10)
The President consults the Chief Minister before appointing a GovernorThe President may; there is no obligation. "Should" or "must" makes the statement false. (T6 N01)
Ninth Schedule laws are immune from judicial reviewNot after 24 April 1973 — I.R. Coelho (2007) reopened them to basic-structure challenge. (T1 N06)
The Speaker's Money Bill certificate is finalFinal for procedure; its reviewability is an open question before a seven-judge bench after Rojer Mathew (2019). (T5 N02)
Removal of the Vice-President needs no special formality14 days' notice is required under Art 67(b) — what is absent is a stated ground, not the notice. (T6 N00)
05

Drill

Practice · How many

Consider the following:

  1. Rejection of the Budget by the Lok Sabha
  2. Passing of a censure motion against the Council of Ministers
  3. Defeat of the Motion of Thanks on the President\'s Address
  4. Passing of an adjournment motion

After how many of the above must the Council of Ministers resign?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) Only three
  • (d) All four
Answer: (b) — only two
  • 1 and 3 compel resignation — a money defeat and a defeated Motion of Thanks are both implied votes of no confidence.
  • 2 does not — after a censure motion the Council must seek the confidence of the House, not resign.
  • 4 does not — an adjournment motion carries censure but compels nothing.
Practice · Statements

Consider the following statements:

  1. A Governor can commute a death sentence for an offence against a State law.
  2. A registered voter in India can contest a Lok Sabha election from any constituency in India.
  3. Nominated members of the Rajya Sabha vote in the election of the Vice-President.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

  • (a) Only one
  • (b) Only two
  • (c) All three
  • (d) None
Answer: (b) — only two
  • 1 is correct — the Governor loses only the power to pardon a death sentence, not to commute it.
  • 2 is wrong because of the exception: Assam\'s autonomous districts, Lakshadweep and Sikkim require a local elector. "Any" is the trap.
  • 3 is correct — nominated members are excluded from the Presidential college but vote in the Vice-Presidential one.
  • Three exceptions, one question. This is exactly how the format is built.
Practice · Negative

Which one of the following statements is NOT correct?

  • (a) A Financial Emergency permits the reduction of judges\' salaries
  • (b) A law abolishing a State Legislative Council is not an amendment under Article 368
  • (c) In the Rajya Sabha, Question Hour precedes Zero Hour
  • (d) Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended during a National Emergency
Answer: (c) is NOT correct
  • Since 2014 the Rajya Sabha takes Zero Hour at 11 a.m. and Question Hour at noon. The familiar order holds in the Lok Sabha only.
  • (a), (b) and (d) are all accurate — and all three are exceptions in their own right.

One-screen recall — the exceptions

  • Money defeat = confidence defeat. Budget rejected · cut motion carried · Motion of Thanks defeated · confidence motion lost → resign. Censure or adjournment motion passed → do not resign.
  • Death sentence: Governor cannot pardon, but can suspend/remit/commute. Court martial → Governor has nothing.
  • Nominated members: out of the Presidential college, in the Vice-Presidential one.
  • "Any constituency" — false. Assam autonomous districts, Lakshadweep, Sikkim need a local elector.
  • VP removal: 14 days\' notice IS required. What is missing is a stated ground.
  • Governor appointment: President may consult the CM, never must.
  • Art 169 — abolishing a Legislative Council is not an Art 368 amendment.
  • Judicial salaries reducible only under Art 360.
  • Arts 20 and 21 never suspendable. Art 358 only on war/external aggression.
  • Zero Hour first in the Rajya Sabha since 2014; Question Hour first in the Lok Sabha.
  • Home Minister chairs Parliamentary Affairs and Accommodation; PM chairs the rest.
  • RS Chairman presides over the Population & Public Health Forum; Speaker over the rest.
  • No joint sitting at State level, or for Money Bills and Constitutional Amendment Bills.
  • Ninth Schedule laws post-24 April 1973 are reviewable (I.R. Coelho, 2007).
  • Money Bill certificate — reviewability is open, not closed (Rojer Mathew, 2019).
