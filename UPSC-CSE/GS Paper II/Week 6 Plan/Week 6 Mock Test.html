Weighted to the rolling three-week average, not to the last paper. So the mix here is DIRECT 14 · HOW MANY 12 · STATEMENTS 9 · NEGATIVE 7 · ASSERTION–REASON 4 · PAIRS 4. The five rules: (1) Attempt rate is now the binding constraint, not accuracy. Week 5 was 74.4% accurate with eleven blanks and ten minutes left on the clock — attempting those eleven at your own accuracy would have equalled the topper exactly. (2) At 90 seconds a question is a two-way guess at best: take the guess and move, because spending another minute and then leaving it blank is the worst of both outcomes. (3) On a how-many question, verify each statement independently — it cannot be solved by elimination; skip only if two or more are genuinely unknown. (4) Hunt absolutes — any, all, only, never, must. Several questions here turn on a single qualifier. (5) Keep the NOT discipline: mark each statement T/F, then re-read the stem before answering.
Four things in this paper are newer than most textbooks. All are deliberate. The consumer pecuniary limits are ₹50 lakh / ₹2 crore (revised down from ₹1 crore / ₹10 crore in December 2021); the SIR of electoral rolls was upheld in May 2026; the NCBC has been constitutional only since 2018, and the States' power to identify backward classes was restored by the 105th Amendment in 2021; and Davinder Singh (2024) permits sub-classification within Scheduled Castes. Answer any of them from an older source and you will get it wrong — which is the point of including them.

Q1How many

With reference to the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, consider the following statements:

  1. The District Commission has jurisdiction where the consideration paid does not exceed fifty lakh rupees.
  2. Pecuniary jurisdiction is determined by the amount of compensation claimed.
  3. A complaint may be filed where the consumer ordinarily resides or works.
  4. An appeal from the State Commission to the National Commission must be filed within sixty days.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(b) Only two. Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is the chapter's central error — jurisdiction follows the consideration paid, never the compensation claimed, so a ₹40 lakh purchase with a ₹5 crore claim stays in the District Commission. Statement 4 doubles the period: the appeal window is thirty days at each stage, not sixty. Topic 9 Note 00, Section 01.
Q2Direct

The National Commission (NCDRC) exercises original jurisdiction where the consideration paid exceeds

(a) ₹2 crore. The thresholds are ₹50 lakh and ₹2 crore, set by rules notified on 31 December 2021. Options (b) and (d) are the figures in the Act as enacted in 2019 — superseded, because the original limits pushed a flood of cases into the District Commissions. Any source written 2019–2021 carries the wrong pair. Topic 9 Note 00, Section 01.
Q3Negative

Which one of the following is NOT a relief that a Consumer Commission may order under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019?

(b) is NOT a relief. A Commission's reliefs under section 39 are civil — remove the defect, replace, return the price with interest, compensation, punitive damages, discontinue the unfair trade practice, corrective advertisement, withdraw hazardous goods, costs. Imprisonment arises only under section 72, and then for non-compliance with an order, not for the original defect. Topic 9 Note 00, Section 01.

Q6Statements

Consider the following statements about Lok Adalats:

  1. An award of a Lok Adalat is deemed to be a decree of a civil court.
  2. An appeal lies against an award of a Lok Adalat to the District Court.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only. The award is deemed a decree of a civil court and is final and binding — and precisely because it rests on the parties' own consent, no appeal lies against it to any court. If no settlement is reached the case simply returns to the referring court, and court fees already paid are refunded. Topic 6 Note 07, Section 05.
Q7How many

With reference to a Permanent Lok Adalat, consider the following statements:

  1. It was created in 1987 by the Legal Services Authorities Act as first enacted.
  2. Its pecuniary jurisdiction extends up to ten lakh rupees.
  3. If the parties fail to settle, it may decide the dispute on merits.
  4. It consists exclusively of serving judicial officers.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(a) Only one. Only statement 3 is correct, and it is the real discriminator: an ordinary Lok Adalat can only record a settlement, a Permanent one may decide on merits. Statement 1 is wrong on the date — Permanent Lok Adalats came by the 2002 amendment, not the 1987 Act as enacted. Statement 2 understates the limit, which is ₹1 crore. Statement 4 is wrong on composition — a judicial officer sits with others, typically a lawyer and a social worker (Prelims 2010). Topic 6 Note 07, Section 05.
Q8Assertion–Reason

Consider the following two statements:

  1. Statement-I: Free legal aid has been held to be an essential ingredient of a reasonable, fair and just procedure.
  2. Statement-II: Article 39A was inserted into the Constitution by the 42nd Amendment.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

(b) Both correct, but II does not explain I. Both statements are true — but the date of insertion is not the reason free legal aid became enforceable. Q14Statements

Consider the following statements:

  1. The Chief Election Commissioner may be removed in the same manner and on the same grounds as a judge of the Supreme Court.
  2. An Election Commissioner may be removed by the President at his discretion.

  1. Statement-I: The GST Council was designed so that neither the Union nor the States can carry a decision alone.
  2. Statement-II: Both Parliament and the State legislatures surrendered taxing powers when GST was introduced.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

(a). Both correct, and the mutual surrender of taxing power is precisely why the voting rule was engineered that way. Had either side been able to set rates alone, the other would have surrendered sovereignty for nothing; the three-fourths threshold is the constitutional expression of that bargain. Mains practice — write these out by hand, 10 minutes each

M1. "The independence of the Election Commission rests on one man's security of tenure and the goodwill of the executive." Critically examine in the light of recent legislative and judicial developments. (15 marks · 250 words)
Skeleton: Art 324's three safeguards → the asymmetry — equal vote, unequal protection; only the CEC has the Supreme Court judge standard → Anoop Baranwal (2023) filled a 73-year vacuum expressly "until Parliament legislates" → the CEC Act 2023 legislated and restored a 2:1 executive majority; challenge still pending → 2nd ARC and Law Commission 255th Report; no post-retirement bar → independence real but narrow and personal.
M2. The constitutional commissions for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Backward Classes are built on an identical template but have delivered unequal results. Examine. (15 marks · 250 words)
Skeleton: the shared template (five members, presidential warrant, civil-court powers, consultation duty) → the common weakness: recommendatory only, no fixed tenure or qualifications → why outcomes diverge — age (1990 / 2004 / 2018), subject matter (only the NCST faces the Fifth and Sixth Schedules), and legal churn (102nd read down, 105th restoring) → reform: constitutional tenure, time-bound government response → a design guarantees voice, not outcome.
M3. "The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 shifted consumer justice from adjudication alone to regulation." Examine the significance of that shift and the limits it has run into. (15 marks · 250 words)
Skeleton: the 1986 pyramid was reactive — a Commission moves only when someone complains → 2019 added the CCPA (suo motu, recall, class action, endorser liability), product liability across manufacturer/seller/service provider, and e-commerce → why regulation was the missing piece: a misleading advertisement injures millions in sums too small for any of them to litigate → limits: the 2019 thresholds worsened District pendency until revised in Dec 2021; vacancies and infrastructure bind → correct in principle, delivery turns on administrative capacity.
M4. "Cesses and surcharges have hollowed out the divisible pool." Discuss with reference to the Finance Commission's mandate and the working of fiscal federalism. (15 marks · 250 words)
Skeleton: Art 280 and the vertical/horizontal split → Art 270 shares net proceeds, but Art 271 sends a surcharge wholly to the Union and cesses sit outside → so 41% is 41% of a shrunken pool; the States' share of gross revenue is lower than the headline → add Art 282 discretionary grants funding State subjects → the GST Council as the counter-model: 1/3 · 2/3 · 3/4, veto but not dictation → way forward: cap cess share, widen the pool.
