# Test Intelligence — UGSSP Weekly Test DNA & Personal Error Patterns

This file is the distilled intelligence from Week 1 and Week 2 UGSSP (UPSC Guide Self-Study Plan) test results. It is a **binding input to every future note and question-bank generation** — read it alongside `NOTE_GENERATION_WORKFLOW.md` before writing any note. It has three parts: how this test-maker writes questions, how deep their content actually goes, and where Lavan personally loses marks (with countermeasures baked into what we build).

Update this file after **every** weekly test postmortem. Stale intelligence is worse than none.

## Part 1 — The test-maker's question DNA (observed formats, Weeks 1–2)

Every weekly test is 50 Prelims MCQs (+2 correct, −0.66 wrong, 0 skip, 60 minutes) plus a separate Mains paper (5 questions). The Day-7 weekly test covers the **whole week's topics**, and Week 2 proved it also freely reaches back into earlier weeks' topics — never assume a test is limited to the newest material.

Observed question formats, with measured frequency across 100 real questions:

1. **DIRECT single-fact** (~35–40%): "Under which Article…", "Who appoints…". Tests one precise anchor — an Article number, a year, an age, a body's composition.
2. **STATEMENTS + which is/are correct** (~20%): 2–3 statements, options like "1 only / 2 only / Both / Neither". The wrong statement usually differs from truth by ONE word or number.
3. **HOW MANY of the above** (~12%): 3–5 statements/pairs, options "Only one / Only two / All / None". Cannot be solved by elimination — every single statement must be independently verified. The test-maker's favourite way to punish partial knowledge.
4. **NEGATIVE phrasing** (~10%): "Which is/are NOT correct / incorrect?" — same as format 2 but polarity-flipped. See Part 3: this is Lavan's 0%-accuracy format.
5. **PAIRS / matching** (~6%): case→doctrine, commission→recommendation, scheme→Article. Depth is at the level of *specific named items*, not concepts.
6. **ASSERTION–REASON (Statement-I / Statement-II)** (~5%): both true? is II the explanation of I? The trap is almost always in the "correct explanation" judgment, not the truth values.
7. **Duplicate/near-duplicate questions** (observed once, Week 2 Q39=Q50): the same question appears twice in one paper. Answer it identically both times; do not second-guess on the second encounter.

Absolute-option rule of thumb the test rewards: options containing "only", "never", "all", "exclusively", "neither" are usually (not always) wrong when the subject is a broad constitutional principle, and usually RIGHT when the subject is a hard procedural rule (e.g., "only the Lok Sabha can…"). The distinction is: principles bend, procedures don't.

## Part 2 — Depth calibration (what "knowing the topic" actually means here)

The single biggest Week 2 lesson: **constitutional-text-level notes are one full layer too shallow for this test.** The answer keys cite, as routine expected knowledge:

- **Named case law with holdings** — not just Kesavananda/Maneka, but second-tier cases: Sukhdev Singh v. Bhagatram (ONGC/LIC/IFC are "State"), Chandra Mohan Khanna v. NCERT (NCERT is not "State"), Satimbla Sharma (unaided minority schools), Kuldip Nayar (RS domicile), Vishaka, Shreya Singhal, Kehar Singh + Epuru Sudhakar (pardon reviewability), Rameshwar Prasad, Nabam Rebia.
- **Scheme/initiative → Article mappings** — Janani Suraksha Yojana→Art 39, International Solar Alliance→Art 51, Child Labour Act→39(f), RTE→Art 46/21-A.
- **Statutory sections** — IPC 153A/295A/505 (hate speech), Law Commission 267th Report, IT Act provisions (with the Shreya Singhal correction).
- **Exact numbers and windows** — 14 days (Money Bill), 6 months (session gap, non-MP minister), 1/10th (quorum), 15% (91st Amendment cap), 12 (small-State minister floor), 35 (President/VP/Governor age), 25/30 (LS/RS age), 1971 Census freeze.
- **Body-by-body institutional detail** — e.g., NITI Aayog nominates a non-voting advisor to each Zonal Council; the Inter-State Water Disputes Tribunal's award is final with no SC appeal by design.

**Rule going forward:** every note must carry this depth layer at write-time (a "deep reference" of case law, schemes, sections, numbers for its topic), sourced primarily from Laxmikanth's chapter detail + the specific institutional trivia the answer keys have shown they draw on. If a fact would fit naturally in a UGSSP answer explanation, it belongs in the note BEFORE the test, not patched in after.

### The "beyond Laxmikanth" layer (adopted from Sarmad Mehraj's July 2026 lecture, cross-validated by our own postmortems)

An external check on our depth calibration — an Unacademy Polity educator's independent argument ("Laxmikanth For Indian Polity: NOT ENOUGH," youtu.be/fVw5y0C3uMM) lands on the same conclusion our Week 2 postmortem reached, and adds four layers every Polity note must now include:

1. **Recent-judgment layer (rolling ~18-month window).** Not just landmark cases but live ones: the 2024 electoral-bonds strike-down (Art 14, manifest arbitrariness), the 2025 Governor assent-timeline saga (TN Governor case → Art 143 reference → Nov 2025 Constitution Bench walk-back), footpath/menstrual-health readings of Art 21. When writing or revising a note, search for judgments from the last 18 months touching its topic and fold them in with holdings — a note without its recent-judgment layer is stale on arrival.
2. **Concept-behind-the-article layer.** Article text is the floor, not the content: Art 14 must carry reasonable classification, arbitrariness, and substantive equality (the wheelchair-vs-sprinter framing); Art 368 must carry the implied-limitations argument (Palkhivala) behind basic structure; Puttaswamy must carry the doctrine of proportionality. If a note only states what an article says, it is Prelims-only material.
3. **Constituent Assembly debate ammunition (Mains).** For any institution question ("do we need a Governor/Rajya Sabha?"), the CA's own arguments are the highest-value Mains material: why the framers kept an appointed Governor, the Rajya Sabha's revisory/cooling-chamber rationale, why no time limit was placed on assent. Each institutional note carries at least one CA-debate point.
4. **Pros/cons/way-forward framing for laws in news.** Any statute or amendment in the news window (UAPA amendment, Child Labour Amendment, simultaneous elections, UCC) gets both sides plus an optimistic way-forward line — the standard Mains answer shape.

The video's practical source stack, for reference when researching: Laxmikanth + the bare Constitution text + CA debates + two newspapers (The Hindu, Indian Express) + constitutional-law blogs and lectures. We substitute targeted web research at note-writing time for the daily newspaper habit, but the 18-month currency standard is the same.

### The coverage guarantee and the liability principle (Lavan's standing rule)

The division of responsibility is fixed: **Lavan is liable for not remembering; the notes are liable for everything else.** A wrong or skipped test answer must never trace to (a) a concept explained unclearly, or (b) a fact absent from the notes. Operationally:

- **Coverage ceiling:** at most 1–2 questions per weekly test may fall outside the notes' content. Three or more out-of-note questions = the notes failed, not the student.
- **Audit method — strict, not statistical.** The first attempt at this audit used a keyword-overlap threshold across each question's full text and reported Week 2 at "47/50 covered." That was wrong: an aggregate-similarity score passes a question whose *specific tested fact* is still absent, because the surrounding vocabulary matches. The correct method is **per-question fact probes** — for each of the 50 questions, name the precise fact(s) the answer key turns on, then grep the corpus for each one individually. Re-run under that method, Week 2 was really ~41/50, exposing nine further gaps: River Boards Act 1956 (advisory, never used); Article 356's limit (President cannot assume High Court powers); Indra Sawhney's 50% ceiling / creamy layer / 77th-81st Amendment promotion-and-backlog rules; the SMILE scheme→Article 41 mapping; DPSP's positive/socio-economic/societarian character words; UAPA and RPA as Fundamental-Duty-implementing statutes; Fundamental Duties' amendability by special majority; Parliament's exclusive extraterritorial legislative power; and the doctrine of colourable legislation (with pith-and-substance as its contrast). Week 1 under the same strict method came out clean except positive-vs-negative secularism, which existed only inside a practice-answer key rather than as explained content — itself a violation of the crystal-clarity standard below. **All gaps in both weeks are now patched; never report a coverage number derived from aggregate keyword similarity.**
- **Crystal-clarity standard:** any concept that unfolds as a series of events (a case saga, an amendment battle, an institutional evolution) gets BOTH a beginner-level story telling (prose, narrative order, the `📖` mentor-box register: who did what, why, what happened next) AND a compact flowchart/timeline SVG capturing the beats in order — the reference implementation is the 2025 Governor assent saga in Topic 6, Note 01 (story box + 4-beat timeline figure). Story for understanding, flowchart for recall; neither substitutes for the other.
- **A fact stated only inside a practice-question answer key does NOT count as covered.** Every tested fact must live in the note's explanatory body — a table row, tip box, or prose passage that teaches it — with the question bank merely drilling what the body already taught. The positive-vs-negative secularism gap was exactly this failure mode: the fact was recorded in a coaching-question verdict, so a keyword search found it, but nothing in the note ever *explained* the distinction. When auditing, search the body, not the answer keys.
- **Pre-test coverage check — run it in two passes, not one.** Before each weekly test: **(Pass 1, literal)** probe every item in the schedule PDF's "Important Topics" column against the notes corpus. **(Pass 2, adjacent)** probe the topics a setter could reasonably reach for that sit just outside the schedule's wording — the machinery, exceptions and named doctrines orbiting each listed item. Pass 2 is where the real danger lives: the Week 3 audit passed 35/38 on literal wording, but Pass 2 exposed that "Legislative Powers" had no **ordinance power (Articles 123/213)** at all, "Parliamentary Committees in the budgetary process" had no **PAC / Estimates / COPU** composition facts, "Emergency Powers" had no **Article 360**, and the presiding-officer line had no **Pro-tem Speaker / Panel of Chairpersons / removal procedure**. A schedule's bullet is a heading, not a boundary — assume the setter reads it as a chapter reference.
- Standing Pass-2 checklist for any executive/legislature topic: ordinance powers, emoluments and their charged status, election-dispute jurisdiction, removal procedures with notice periods, committee composition numbers, the exclusive powers of each chamber, and the "cannot do" limits attached to every listed power.

### Part 2C — Harvest the exceptions, not just the rules

An examiner cannot build a discriminating question from a rule everyone knows. They build it from the **edge** of that rule. So every note must surface its edges explicitly rather than leaving them buried in prose.

**The Week 3 evidence.** Q19 asked what happens when the Budget fails to pass the Lok Sabha. Lavan answered in eleven seconds that it is modified and re-presented. The correct answer is that the entire Council of Ministers resigns — a Budget defeat is an **implied vote of no confidence**. The fact is not difficult. It had simply never been surfaced as a *rule with an exception*, so there was nothing to recall under time pressure.

**Four families of exception, to be hunted deliberately in every note:**

1. **Confidence exceptions** — outcomes that carry a consequence larger than they appear to (a money defeat toppling a government), and outcomes that look decisive but are not (a censure motion, which compels no resignation).
2. **"Only" and "never"** — powers vesting in exactly one body. A statement naming any other body is false. These are the cheapest marks available.
3. **Numbers with a twin** — pairs of near-identical figures where the whole question is which one goes where: 15% with a floor of 12 versus 15% with no floor; four nomination fields versus five; 50+50 versus 20+20.
4. **Rules with a carve-out** — general propositions true except in a named case. Almost always signalled by an absolute in the stem: *any, all, every, only, must, should*.

**The rules this generates:**

- Every note carries its exceptions **visibly** — in a trap box or a dedicated row — never as a subordinate clause inside a paragraph.
- **A cross-syllabus `99 Exceptions Ledger.html`** collects them all, since exceptions scattered across twenty notes are invisible and gathered into one they become a revisable pattern. It is updated after every weekly test.
- When a test question turns on an exception that was missing, it is added to **both** the topic note and the Ledger. Adding it to only one is how it gets lost again.
- Any statement in a practice question containing an absolute should be checked against the Ledger before being marked true.

## Part 3 — Lavan's measured error patterns and the countermeasures we build in

Quantified from both scored results (Week 1: 25R/17W/8S = 38.78/60; Week 2: 16R/20W/14S = 18.80/98):

| Pattern | Evidence | Countermeasure (binding on us) |
|---|---|---|
| **NEGATIVE questions: 0% (0/6 attempted, both weeks)** | Reads "which is NOT correct", finds a correct statement, picks it. | Every note's question bank must include ≥2 negative-phrased questions. Drill ritual: on seeing NOT/incorrect, physically mark each statement T/F first, then re-read the stem before answering. |
| **Assertion–Reason: 2/5** | Fails the "is II the correct explanation of I" judgment. | Include A-R questions in every bank. Teach the 3-step: truth of I → truth of II → *only then* the explanation link. |
| **HOW MANY: ~50% + 7 skips** | Partial knowledge punished; format also causes avoidance. | Include ≥2 per bank. Teach: verify each statement independently; if ≥2 statements are genuinely unknown, SKIP — this format has the worst guess-odds of all. |
| **Endgame collapse (Week 2)** | 8 of 14 skips in the last 11 questions; pace slowed to 84s/q. | Time protocol: first pass ≤60s/question, hard. Mark-and-move. All 50 seen by minute 45; last 15 minutes only for marked returns. |
| **Guess bleed** | Week 2: −13.2 marks from 20 wrong. | 50/50 or worse → skip. Only guess at 3-options-eliminated or better. A blank is worth 0.66 more than a wrong answer, every time. |
| **Duplicate-question second-guessing** | Q39/Q50 (identical), wrong both times. | If a question repeats, answer identically without re-litigating. Mock tests we build should include one deliberate duplicate pair to drill this reflex. |

Score arithmetic to keep in front of him: at 36 attempts, moving accuracy from 44% → 70% (16→25 correct) with the same attempts = 25×2 − 11×0.66 = **42.7**, before any knowledge gains. Format drilling + guess discipline alone roughly doubles the Week 2 score.

## Part 3B — The source-coverage contract (what "a complete note" means)

Every note must be built against **six sources at once**, not one. This is the checklist that closed the Week 3 gaps and must be run for every future topic:

1. **The weekly plan's own "Important Topics" column.** This is the contract with the coaching institute. Every single listed item must be findable in the note by search — plus the adjacent surface around it (Part 5's two-pass rule). A schedule bullet is a *chapter heading*, not a boundary.
2. **Laxmikanth, section by section.** Open the corresponding chapter's contents list and tick off every sub-heading. The Week 3 audit scored Parliament at 22/37 and Committees at 4/10 on first pass — a note that "covers Parliament" while missing Question Hour types, closure motions, the three funds and the committee system is not a complete note. Target: **100% of the chapter's sub-headings represented.**
3. **The Bare Act.** Article numbers with their *clause-level* content — Art 75(1A) is the 15% cap, 75(5) is the six-month rule, and a question will turn on exactly that difference. Every institutional note carries an Articles table.
4. **Recent judgments (rolling ~18 months).** Search at write-time; never assume the textbook position still holds. Two live examples of textbooks being overtaken: Money Bill certification ("final and unquestionable" → open before a 7-judge bench after *Rojer Mathew*), and the Governor's assent timelines (imposed April 2025 → withdrawn by the Constitution Bench, November 2025).
5. **Current affairs / schemes / statutes in the news.** Scheme→Article mappings (Janani Suraksha→39, SMILE→41, ISA→51), Bills before Parliament (129th Amendment on simultaneous elections), and Acts in the news window.
6. **PYQs — genuine, verified, and reproduced as real MCQs.** See Part 4.

If any one of the six is skipped, the note is incomplete regardless of how long it is.

## Part 4 — Mandatory question-bank requirements for every note (new standing rule)

The Rank-1 student has a coaching institute feeding her solved MCQs in the exam's exact formats. We replicate that advantage in-house. Every concept note, from Week 3 onward, must ship with a **question gauntlet** — not 2–3 token predicted questions, but a bank shaped like the real paper:

- Minimum **8 MCQs per note**, distributed across the Part-1 formats, deliberately over-weighting Lavan's weak formats: ≥2 NEGATIVE, ≥2 HOW MANY, ≥1 ASSERTION–REASON, ≥1 PAIRS, remainder STATEMENTS/DIRECT.
- Every MCQ hinges on a fact actually present in the note (so a wrong answer is a revision pointer, not a mystery), with the verdict naming the section it came from.
- ≥1 Mains question per note with a model-answer skeleton (intro line → 3–4 body points with case/Article name-drops → conclusion).
- Honest labelling preserved: these are **Predicted/Practice questions, never real PYQs** — the PYQ-integrity rules in `AGENT_MENTOR_PERSONA.md` apply unchanged.
- **Genuine PYQs are mandatory too, in a separate labelled bucket (A).** A note with zero genuine PYQs is incomplete — all six Week 3 notes initially shipped with none, which was a real failure. Reproduce each PYQ with its year and stage, in full exam layout, followed by practice questions in bucket (B).
- Before each weekly test, a **full-length 50-question mock** in the exact UGSSP format (+2/−0.66, 60 min, self-scoring) covering that week's syllabus, taken under timed conditions at least one day before the real test. The mock includes one deliberate duplicate pair (Part 3, last row), a **reset/retake** control, an optional **shuffle**, and an attempt-history table.

### Part 4B — Question *presentation* rules (non-negotiable formatting)

Content is not enough; the question must look and behave like the real thing, because format familiarity is itself a scoring skill.

- **Full exam layout, never prose.** Structure: `<p class="qtext">` stem → `<ol class="subs">` numbered statements → `<p class="qtext">` closing instruction → `<ul class="opts">` with four visible `(a)–(d)` options. Never write a question as a paragraph with options buried inline, and never write a *description* of a question ("statement-based MCQ on X") — that cannot be attempted.
- **Answers hidden until the learner commits.** Options are clickable; the explanation appears only after a choice is made, with the correct option marked green and a wrong choice red. A "Show answer" button exists as a deliberate fallback. Rationale: reading a question with the answer visible produces almost no retention — the retrieval attempt *before* seeing the answer is what builds memory, and a corrected wrong guess sticks better than an unexamined right one.
- **Verdicts must name the option letter** ("Answer: (b) — …"), not only describe the answer in prose. Prose-only verdicts break auto-grading and leave the learner unsure whether they were right.
- **Every generated option set must contain the correct answer.** When auto-generating options, derive the answer from the verdict *first*, then build the option set around it. A real bug caught this way: a Rajya Sabha question whose answer was "2 only" was given options (a) 1 and 2 only / (b) 2 and 3 only / (c) 1 and 3 only / (d) 1, 2 and 3 — the right answer was absent. Also check statement-count consistency (a 3-statement question must never offer "All four").
- Include occasional **genuinely "all correct"** answer sets. Lavan's measured instinct is to reject option (d) assuming something must be wrong; that instinct must be trained out with real examples (UPSC 2012 Prelims on parliamentary control is one).

**Explanation layout — headline plus bullets, never a paragraph.**

The explanation is read at the moment of maximum attention: right after a wrong answer. A wall of prose wastes that moment. Structure every verdict as:

```html
<div class="verdict">
<b class="vans">Answer: (b)</b>
<ul class="vwhy">
  <li>The direct reason the right option is right.</li>
  <li>Why the most tempting wrong option is wrong.</li>
  <li>The transferable pattern, and the note section that covers it.</li>
</ul>
</div>
```

- **One idea per bullet.** Split on sentence boundaries; use semicolons as split points only in genuinely long explanations, since a semicolon usually joins two halves of a single thought.
- **The answer headline is separate and typographically distinct** — monospace, uppercase, teal — so the eye lands on it first, before the reasoning.
- **Never uppercase the reasoning.** `text-transform:uppercase` on a 300-character explanation is unreadable; it was in use across all 20 notes until July 2026 and was removed. Uppercase is for the answer line alone.
- **Single-sentence explanations use `<p class="vone">`, not a one-item list.** A bullet list of one looks like an error.
- **Where the verdict names the answer only in words** ("Answer: Only three (1, 2, 3)"), add the option letter as well. This is what makes click-to-grade work; 42 such verdicts were repaired in the July 2026 sweep, lifting auto-grading from 118 to 160 of 232 MCQs.

## Part 5 — Weekly postmortem loop (the process that keeps this file alive)

After every weekly test, in order:

1. Parse the Test Result PDF: per-question outcome, format classification, time-on-screen. Recompute the Part-3 table with the new data — celebrate any format that moved, flag any that didn't.
2. Mine the **full answer-key explanations** (right AND wrong answers — right-answer explanations carry adjacent testable facts too) for every case, section, scheme, number cited. Diff against the notes: anything missing goes into the relevant note's deep-reference layer within the same sitting.
3. Update Part 1 frequencies and Part 2 depth examples if the test-maker's style shifted.
4. Only then plan the next week's notes — with the gauntlet and depth rules above applied from the first draft, not retrofitted.
